## Advisor insight

The track's second-strongest game verb is *Florence*'s friction: talking is a jigsaw with many sharp pieces on the first date and one big easy piece once two people are settled. In a film nobody fits pieces, but the audience still feels friction through one thing the maker controls completely for free: the length of the shots. Give every chapter a cutting rate that tracks how settled her life was in it. The first week in a new country or a new job: many short cuts, nothing held longer than two seconds, the eye never allowed to rest, the way she could not. The long, steady middle of a marriage or a job she knew in her sleep: one shot held for twenty seconds while nothing happens except a kettle. A hard conversation: hard cuts, no dissolves, slightly mismatched framings. Then the last chapter, "now", earns the longest single shot in the film. The audience will never name the rule, but a room of relatives will breathe differently in the settled chapters, and only *her* years decide where the cuts fall, so it passes the swap test by construction. It costs nothing, it is a column in the devices log (average shot length per chapter) that the `film-story` and `cinematic-quality` tracks can measure, and it gives the maker a reason to make editing, not effects, the craft she learns first.
