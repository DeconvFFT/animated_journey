## 3. Critical issues

### 3.1 The slice measures one thing and the model predicts another
`model.csv` line 174 ("hours the selected case predicts for a chapter-1 build") is shots + chapter pass + sound, times L1: 26.5 h in Base. It contains no grade, mix, captions, master or learning hours. The playbook (Step 9) defines the measured `h_slice` as "all your hours for the slice, from the approved brief to the approved chapter, including sound, grade and the master"; research Q7 item 8 and the CSV's own input label (line 173, "incl. sound and grade") say the same. If the maker logs honestly, the measured number includes roughly 4.5 h of variable finishing (2.7 h/min × 1.67 min), up to 12 h of master-chain rehearsal (the model's 50% pre-built share of the 24 fixed hours) and some of the 30 "learning + lock" hours. A maker running exactly at Base speed would then report something like 40–45 h, `mu` would read 1.5–1.7, and T2 would say "red: cut to 8 minutes" for a film that fits. The gate that is supposed to replace guesses with measurement would instead inject a known bias. Fix in nudge 1.

### 3.2 G-SLICE cannot pass if the maker keeps the project to themself
`gates.csv` row G-SLICE lists "stranger-read passed" as must-be-true, and research Q7 acceptance item 7 says "all must pass". The playbook (Decision 8, T8) and the skill correctly say the read happens only if the maker decides to hold one. The three files disagree, and the two that make it mandatory contradict the maker's standing instruction. Fix in nudge 2, including a named substitute so "no outside reader" does not mean "no fresh eyes".

## 7. Advisor insight

The `material_in_hand_pct` column is not just a production metric; it is a portrait. In most family archives the decades with the fewest photographs of a mother are the decades she spent holding the camera, or holding everyone else. Let the archive's density set the film's pace instead of fighting it: dense decades cut quickly (12 shots a minute, many stills), sparse decades hold long on the one hand-made material (4–6 shots a minute, a single L5 reconstruction that is visibly a reconstruction), and the film says so once, plainly, in the narration: "there are almost no pictures of her from these years; she was behind the lens." That turns the plan's biggest schedule problem (uneven material) into the film's most honest device, it is true to Principle 4 because it invents nothing, and it also saves hours exactly where the model says they are scarcest. Record the mapping in `chapters.csv` as a `shots_per_min` column derived from `material_in_hand_pct`, so the pacing decision is an artefact and not a feeling.
