## 2. The Hindu-only rule and respect, checked

- **No Mughal, Islamic or Christian motif enters through this track.** The exclusions table (note "Exclusions and ambiguous cases"; playbook "What we leave out"; skill Guardrails) defaults every decorative device out (paisley, jaali, cusped arches, mehndi borders, tile patterns, decorative QR frames) and names the origin. The swastika is left to the maker alone (O-44). Dharmic-not-Hindu and Adivasi text is flagged "ask". Good.
- **Deities with respect.** The track's own deity policy is the right one: no deity image on any phone surface (lock screen, thumbnail, app icon, forwarded frame) until the maker, as her proxy, decides in writing per surface (O-42), because a phone is handled, laid face-down and sits under notifications. Her eight deities (D-31) are named as *hers* and still routed through O-42 and the Pushtimarg sprint's norms. "No Om tone as a UI sound" is a quiet, correct line.
- **Two wording problems that would misfire against her own world.** (a) The exclusion row "Angels, crosses, **bells, candles, haloes** as UI marks: Christian or colonial layer" and the skill's "crosses, haloes, candles and bells used as UI marks" are over-broad. The ghanta (temple bell), the diya and the prabhavali (the flame arch behind a murti) are Hindu; my brief flagged *church-shaped* bells, candles *where diyas belong*, and *gold-disc* haloes *drawn like a church icon*. As written, the rule excludes the very bell this note recommends as the cold-open sound. Nudge 3. (b) **The Gujarati shaping fixture is misdescribed.** The note (Q8), the fixtures README and the playbook (Exercise 7: "both are shaping test strings, not verses") quote the string as `શ્રી ક્ષત્રિય જ્ઞાન ધર્મ કિ ક્ષિ`. The committed SRT and PNG actually carry a first line `શ્રી ગણેશાય નમઃ` (śrī gaṇeśāya namaḥ, "salutations to Shri Ganesha"), the invocation that opens Hindu writing, rendered *wrongly* (visible virama in શ્રી) as a failure exhibit in a public repository. As evidence of a font failure it is legitimate; as a deliberately kept broken rendering of a deity's name, under a rule that asks for correct depiction and respect, it should be re-cut with the non-sacred line only, or at least named for what it is. Nudge 1.

## 6. Advisor insight: make the first four seconds on the phone hers

The track's cleanest technical finding is also its best story opportunity. On a phone in a bright kitchen the cold open needs a visible first light within about four seconds and a first sound with energy above about 400 Hz, or she will think the file is dead. Both constraints are answered by her own morning, not by a generic kettle or latch. In a Pushtimarg home the day begins with a lamp and a bell: the small ghanta rung at the home shrine, the diya lit before anything else is touched. A bell sits exactly in the band a phone speaker passes (the sibling's model cost a temple bell under 3 dB), and a diya flame is the one light that reads on an LCD under a window. So let the phone cut's first sound be *her* shrine bell (recorded in her house when she is out, D-17, W-10 holiday capture list) and its first light the flame she lights, drawn or photographed by hand (L0 to L2, never AI). The venue cut can open darker and quieter because the hall is dark; the phone file gets the same opening with Trim P and the bell one step forward. Then the lock-screen still, which the track already restricts to her script line, becomes the still of that lamp with the end-card words beneath it in Gujarati, checked against a printed source. The constraint becomes the first thing in the film that could belong to no one else. Verify with the Pushtimarg sprint that the morning bell and lamp are indeed her household's practice before building on it; if her morning starts another way, use that.
