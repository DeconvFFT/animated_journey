## 7. Advisor insight

**Let the consent tiers design the architecture: one film, two doors.**

`allowed_use` now has four values, and the natural reading is that every value below `hall` is a loss: a story the public cut cannot carry. Turn it round. Build the film so that the hall cut is complete in itself (the public spine: found voice, documents, places, the maker's own "I remember" fragments, and whatever Tier 2 voices are cleared for the hall), and so that the private-link version is the same film with rooms added: the chapters, voices and photos whose `allowed_use` is `private-link` or `family-only`. In the game-world language the charter welcomes, these are doors that open only with the family key. The mechanic means something true: some of a life is for everyone, and some of it is for the people who were in the room.

Three things follow. The consent ask gets easier and more honest, because "for the family version only" becomes a real answer that earns more yeses than a hall-or-nothing choice. The public premiere stops being the place where the whole life has to fit, which protects her dignity (charter Principle 5) without cutting anything from the family's keepsake. And the inventory you are already building is the architecture: sort rows by `allowed_use` and you have the door plan. Decide at the vertical slice whether the extra rooms are a longer cut, an interactive companion, or both. The runbook's veto window then has a graceful outcome besides "cut it": she can move a room from the hall to the family key.
