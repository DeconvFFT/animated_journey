## Advisor insight

The honesty convention has three legs: one material, said once, answered by the real thing. The third leg has a live version now that the premiere is a public room. Tower answers its actress with the real Claire Wilson on camera; this film can answer its last reconstruction with the real object *in the room*. If the kitchen table, the recipe box, the sewing machine or whatever her chapters are built around is rebuilt in her material on screen for the whole film, then at the final cut to black the actual object is already standing under the screen, lit for the first time. The strangers in the room get the "this happened to a real person" moment that Tower engineers in two seconds; the family recognise the object before anyone explains it; and she walks up to something of hers rather than to a screen. It costs nothing, has no AI in it, cannot be mistaken for a photograph, and it gives `film-story` its last shot and `surprise-ops` its hardest covert errand (research follow-up 7). It also keeps the film honest in the one place a tribute is most tempted to cheat: the ending.
