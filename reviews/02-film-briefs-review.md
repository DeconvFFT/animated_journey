## Part 0 — How the three new facts change the plan

### 0.1 "Theatrical" is a quality-per-minute bar, not a runtime

The charter's wording is right: "crafted to a standard that could screen in a theatre." It does not say feature-length, and the maker's phrase "a feature film of her life" should be read as ambition about *quality*, not a commitment to 70+ minutes. Short films screen in cinemas every week; a 14-minute film that holds a 10-metre screen is theatrical. A 90-minute film that sags is not.

My prior, which `production-scale` must test with evidence and `film-story` must test against the story: **12–18 finished minutes to the bar is the defensible default for one learner with a job plus an agent team, with material gathered only after pipeline lock and a two-week dead zone over the holidays.** Twenty-five minutes is reachable only with 20+ hours a week, heavy asset reuse, and a vertical slice approved by the week-9 gate (2026-12-20). Forty-five and ninety minutes are not credible on this calendar and I would like the researcher to show me the evidence either way rather than take my word. Leads to verify: solo and near-solo animated features (for example *Away*, reported as one person over several years; *Flee*, *Memoir of a Snail*, *My Love Affair with Marriage*, *Rocks in My Pockets*) took years with teams; the Academy's short-film ceiling is 40 minutes; many festivals treat 40–60 minutes as a "mid-length" category that is hard to programme.

Consequence for the schedule: the theatrical bar adds a *finishing* phase that the 8–12-minute plan did not have: score, sound mix, grade, master, DCP, verification, tech rehearsal. Proposed backward schedule (the orchestrator owns it; the `production-scale` researcher should stress-test it):

- Weeks 1–4 (to 2026-11-15): Learn. These five notes approved; style bible and pipeline locked at a gate.
- Weeks 5–9 (to 2026-12-20): covert gathering begins the moment the pipeline is locked (per review 01, 1.6); treatment and slot-based beat sheet; vertical slice (one chapter at final quality including sound and grade) approved at G-slice.
- Weeks 10–11 (2026-12-20 to 2027-01-02): holidays. Gathering only. No production targets.
- Weeks 12–15 (to 2027-02-06): production. **Picture lock Saturday 2027-02-06.** If the slice slipped, the runtime drops one scenario, never the polish.
- Weeks 16–17 (to 2027-02-20): score and mix, grade, master, DCP (if any), captions, deliverables, verification.
- Week 18–19 (2027-02-21 to 27): the surprise-ops rehearsal calendar as written (Mon 22 Feb playback rehearsal; Wed 24 room brief; Thu 25 and Fri 26 cold starts). Buffer is spent on fixes, not features.

### 0.2 The public room turns the "shared cut" into a "public cut"

`surprise-ops` designed a shared cut (6–10 minutes) for a room of relatives and a private cut (20–30 minutes) for her. A mostly public gathering changes the test for the shared cut from "would she tell this to this family" to "**would she tell this to strangers**." That is stricter, and it is the right default under Principle 5. Three consequences:

1. Hard-chapter defaults tighten: anything marked HOLD or PRIVATE on the decision sheet is OUT of the public cut, full stop. The private cut keeps its 20–30 minutes if the material and the hours exist; otherwise it is the public cut plus her-turn material gathered after the reveal (surprise-ops Step 12).
2. Rights tighten: a public screening is public performance and public exhibition. Music needs sync *and* performance clearance; people who appear need releases (see 0.3 for the paradox this creates); Minecraft footage becomes a live question (0.6).
3. Story tightens, in a good way: the first two minutes must introduce her to people who have never met her, which is exactly the discipline that stops the film becoming a family in-joke reel. `film-story` must solve the "who is she" problem explicitly.

### 0.3 The sole keeper: what collapses, and what replaces it

The playbooks assumed a second human: a proxy or "two keys" for hard chapters (surprise-ops Step 9), a co-keeper for slips, a trusted relative for the slice review, test viewers over 60 and a child (review 00). The maker has said no. Honour that, and redesign rather than pretend:

- **Hard chapters: one key plus three guards.** (a) The maker fills the decision sheet exactly as written; (b) this advisor reviews every sheet and flags, with the right to say "not in the public cut" but never to force inclusion; (c) a **72-hour cooling rule**: a hard chapter's inclusion decision is made twice, three days apart, and both must be yes; (d) **default OUT for the public cut on any doubt**, with the "gift after the gift" (her-turn conversation on about 2027-03-06) as the mechanism that lets her put it back. Substituted judgement ("what would she choose, and what have I seen her do that tells me") stays the standard. `surprise-ops` needs this delta at its next round.
- **Fresh eyes without insiders: screen to strangers-to-her.** The public audience is mostly strangers, so the right test audience is people who do not know her and are not connected to the family (the maker's own friends or colleagues who will never meet her, or a local film club). This protects the surprise *and* tests the actual room. `cinematic-quality` must deliver that protocol; `production-scale` must schedule it (week 17).
- **The releases paradox.** A public screening wants likeness and voice releases from people who appear; asking for a release reveals a film. Resolution, to be researched not assumed: releases gathered under the *truthful partial pretext* already in `surprise-ops` ("a family film that may be shown at a gathering on 27 February; your voice and photos may appear") when a contributor records or lends material; for people who cannot be asked without revealing the project (people in old photos, the estranged, the dead, minors), the public cut uses only what is cleared or low-risk, and the cinema or online cut waits for written releases gathered *after* the premiere, including hers. `theatrical-delivery` owns this.
- **Throughput drops.** Nobody scans, drives to a lab, or records a relative but the maker. `production-scale` must model one pair of hands.
- **Slip protocol.** The co-keeper step in surprise-ops ("tell your co-keeper within an hour") becomes "write it down and tell the advisor at the next session"; everything else stands.

### 0.5 Resolution and frame rate: decide in week 2, not week 16

`agent-pipeline` fixed 1920×1080 and proposed 24 fps. A cinema DCP is 2K (2048×1080) or 4K; a 1080p master fits a 2K flat container with small pillarboxing or a tiny scale. That is probably fine, but it must be decided now because every agent-rendered hero shot is a function of the chosen frame size, and 4K costs roughly four times the render time and disk. `theatrical-delivery` must give the evidence; my lean is **2K-safe 1080p at 24 fps**, with stills prepared at 4K-equivalent so a later 4K up-res of photo-based shots is cheap, and code-rendered shots re-rendered only if a 4K screen is confirmed.

### 0.6 Minecraft at a public gathering

The dated excerpt says: personal use is relaxed; "share with the community" is treated as commercial; footage must not be "displayed or projected publicly within physical locations" (its examples are commercial venues); in-person events are capped at about 150 participants; the Minecraft name may not be the title; the guidelines "may change." A mostly public gathering with projected footage sits in exactly the undefined zone. Default until `theatrical-delivery` reports: **original voxel visual language (own assets, own palette), Minecraft present by reference and affection only (she knows the game; the film can nod to it) and never as title, textures or captured footage in the public cut.** The researcher re-reads the Usage Guidelines and the EULA, diffs against the excerpt, and names what would unlock real footage.

### 0.7 Rubric additions for these five tracks

- **Craft / usability:** "theatrical" means every per-shot claim is testable on a projected 2K frame at viewing distance, not on a laptop. A checklist item without a pass/fail test is not a checklist item.
- **Ethics:** add "public": would she be comfortable with strangers seeing this; are the people who appear cleared for public exhibition or scheduled for post-premiere release.
- **Accuracy:** legal and rights statements carry a jurisdiction and the words "not legal advice", and end with questions for a local rights professional. Loudness, colour and format numbers name the standard or the document they come from.

### 1.1 `film-story` — Writing a biographical animated film: structure, narration and runtime

**Refined brief.** Not "how to write a biopic." Design the writing process for *this* film: a subject who cannot be interviewed and must not find out; material (photos, home video, letters, relatives' recollections gathered under truthful partial pretexts, found recordings of her voice) that arrives *after* the pipeline is locked and unevenly by decade; a sole maker who is also the narrator-candidate; an audience of mixed ages who mostly do not know her, in a public room, with her present; a premiere that is also the end of the film. Study the exemplars for their *structural devices* (frame story in *Up* and *Big Fish*; interview spine in *Flee* and *Waltz with Bashir*; stand-ins for the unphotographed in *The Missing Picture*; epistolary structure in *Mary and Max*; wordless life cycle in *The Red Turtle*; rotoscoped testimony in *Tower*; time and memory in *Boyhood* and *The Tree of Life*; the ending of *Robot Dreams*; the song as spine in *Coco*), and separate what we borrow (structure, pacing, restraint) from what we cannot (invented events, composite characters, words put in a real mouth; Principle 4). Add films made *by a child about a parent* and films about an absent subject, because that is our stance (leads to verify: *Stories We Tell*, *Tarnation*, *Dick Johnson Is Dead*, *My Architect*, *Minding the Gap*; find animated ones). Build on `story.md` (Up timing, chapter structures A/B/C, through-line method, drawers) and `animated.md` (disclosure convention, time-passing devices); do not redo them. The writing process must be two-pass: a treatment and a *slot-based* beat sheet that can be written before the material exists, then locked chapter by chapter as material arrives, with the vertical slice written first. The runtime recommendation is a joint decision with `production-scale`: argue from the story and the room using the same four scenarios (12, 25, 45, 90 minutes) so the orchestrator can reconcile the two notes. Deliverables: (1) a treatment template with slots and a "what I do not yet know" column; (2) a beat-sheet template for a life film whose rows carry beat, time, chapter, device, evidence source, label-ladder level, consent status, hard-chapter flag, narration source and music cue; (3) a narration-strategy matrix; (4) a runtime recommendation memo with reasons per scenario; (5) a one-page brief on the "who is she" problem (the first two minutes for strangers).

**Must answer.**

### 1.2 `cinematic-quality` — What makes animation theatrical, and how AI-assisted work avoids looking like AI

**Refined brief.** Define the quality bar for a film that is photo-forward (real photographs at ladder levels L0–L4), uses one hand-made material for everything that was never photographed (L5, per `animated.md`), is generated mostly by agents (SVG/CSS animation, code-to-video, Blender headless, ffmpeg) and refined by the maker in alpha Craft apps, and will be projected at 2K to a public room. Two halves. *Craft fundamentals as testable checklists*: art direction and a style bible; colour scripts and how to check the final grade against one on a projector; model sheets and turnarounds, which for us means a per-age reference board for the stylised version of a real person (6, 25, 55) derived from photos; layout and virtual cinematography for stills-based and 2.5D shots; lighting; motion quality (24 fps, holds on twos, easing families, timing charts, settle and hold; `agent-pipeline` already bans uniform easing, so deliver the *test* for it); compositing finish (grain, depth of field, atmosphere, light wrap, edge treatment on cut-outs) with "how much is too much" and projector-specific cautions (grain plus compression, banding at 8-bit, thin-line aliasing, type too small). *AI tells and fixes specific to our pipeline*: face drift, flicker, morphing, plastic skin from upscalers and face restorers, warped hands and text, over-smooth motion, uncanny eyes, the generic "AI aesthetic" in lighting and composition, with cause, detection method, practical fix, and the cases where the technique is simply banned under Principle 4. ### 1.3 `production-scale` — How much film can one learner plus an AI team finish to the theatrical bar?

## Part 3 — Advisor insight

The public room is not a complication to manage; it is the film's first reader. Build the film for the stranger in row seven, the person who has never met her and will decide in two minutes whether they want to. That discipline fixes most of what goes wrong in family films: the in-joke that needs explaining, the montage that assumes you already love her, the hard chapter that is really about the maker. And it gives the premiere its true shape. Her experience that night will not mainly be watching the film; it will be watching a room of strangers meet her and, if the film is good, come to care about her. That is the gift: not "look what we remembered," but "look how many people now know you." Every gate from the treatment onward should ask the stranger's question first and the family's second.
