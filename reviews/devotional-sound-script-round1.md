## 2. The Hindu-only rule (D-28) and respect, checked

Applied with care, and in the right spirit of "flag accurately, the maker rules":

- **Nothing Mughal, Islamic or Christian enters as a motif.** Ghazal and qawwali are out; khayal, sitar, sarod and santoor are out as showpieces; the Ragamala pictures are out while the raga-by-hour idea is kept as a label; raga names that carry Persian-court attributions (Yaman, Miyan ki Malhar, Todi) never go on screen; Desh is avoided for its political and Brahmo associations ("her devotion, not a position", review 04 §0.1); Gurbani, Sikh kirtan, Jain and Buddhist recordings are "dharmic, not Hindu; ask"; Tagore and Nazrul are kept out of the Shakta line. Every exclusion carries an origin note and a source.
- **"Shared" is handled as a category, not a loophole.** The harmonium and the tabla, which are in almost every Hindu bhajan room, are neither stripped nor slipped in: each has an honest origin note, `maker_rule = ASK`, a one-line question for the maker, and silence in the render until ruled. The D-31 update adds the one fact that matters for her: one (tagged) page puts the harmonium inside her own sampradaya's temple music, which makes the question sharper, not softer. Good.
- **A tradition-internal respect rule was found, not imposed.** The same page says her sampradaya's custom keeps the bansuri out of the sanctum as Krishna's own emblem; the artifacts turn that into "do not buy a stock Krishna-flute bed; ask", labelled UNVERIFIED. That is exactly how a flag should work for a family whose first deity is Bal Krishna (D-31).
- **Sacred text is treated with care.** No AI voice of any devotional text; no aarti captioned until its author is read; no raga name printed without a source; strings copied from the source with the source's transliteration, or marked TODO; a verse card is two lines in her script and the room's language, never a three-line stack; IAST kept off the screen for a mostly non-Indian room.
- **Voices.** D-05 is read strictly and correctly: a temple's chanting crowd, a radio, a vendor and a kirtan upload are living voices; only instrumentals qualify before April; the one PD vocal candidate (a 1906 record) is routed through W-46 and the T10 consult rather than assumed clean.

Three soft spots, none an ethics failure, each a nudge:

1. **The maker's own A/B can produce a deliberately broken render of sacred text.** Fixture README command 3b says "one cue, her script's strings", and the Gujarati fixture cue holds the aṣṭākṣara mantra, ૐ, the names of three of her deities and a Vedic line. The committed exhibits avoid this; the instruction does not. Nudge 2.
2. **The flag `CHURCH_COLONIAL` says more than the sources do.** The harmonium's source says reed organs arrived "possibly with missionaries or traders"; the Carnatic violin is a European instrument with no church channel shown. Under a rule that names *Christian* motifs, a flag that reads "church" where the evidence reads "possibly" overstates, and review 04 asked for accurate flags in both directions. Nudge 7.
3. **The commission brief opens "I am making a short private tribute film."** The premiere is a mostly public gathering (D-08) and the same brief later asks for public-exhibition rights. D-18 is "omit, never lie". Nudge 1.

## 6. Advisor insight: give the film her tradition's clock and her tradition's drum

The note found, almost in passing, the two most personal sound ideas in all four Hindu tracks and then left them in a table. First, her sampradaya keeps time by eight darshans, from Maṅgalā before dawn to Śayana at night. A Pushtimarg household's day already has a shape that is hers and that a stranger in the room can feel without being told: the film's morning is Maṅgalā (the bell, the lamp, the conch), its midday is Rājabhoga (the kitchen set, W-34, where the food is made before it is offered), its dusk is Sandhyārati, its end is Śayana (the lamp carried away, the room going quiet). Key the `time_of_day_or_season` field and the module turns to those names in the sound sheet (never on screen), and ask the maker one question on Sun 25 Oct: which of the eight does her shelf actually keep, and at what hour does she ring the bell. Then the maker records S-02 at that same hour in the empty house, so the room tone under the bell is the light and traffic of her real hour, not a generic dawn.

Second, the drum of her tradition is the pakhawaj, flagged `NONE` and PD-source-free, the one melodic-rhythmic instrument in the whole sheet with no origin question and a direct line to haveli kirtan. If any instrumental cue is ever made (April, after TD-L2c), a slow dhamar or rupak pulse on a pakhawaj under a Sa-Pa drone is the honest sound of her world; a sitar or a flute is someone else's. Write that into Template H's brief as the first instrument option, and into the April plan, so the only commissioned sound the film ever carries is the one her tradition would recognise.
