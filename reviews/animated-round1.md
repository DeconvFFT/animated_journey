## Critical issue (must fix before approve)

**The playbook's production pattern contradicts the charter's production approach.** `CHARTER.md` (Known facts, updated today) says: Windows PC; Claude desktop app is memory-heavy so heavy work goes to the cloud; "agents generate, apps refine"; the ArtCraft Crafting Apps (PhotoCraft, VectorCraft, FilmCraft, EffectCraft, LightCraft, SoundCraft) are the refinement layer; every step needs a proven fallback and nothing critical may depend on one alpha app. `playbooks/animated.md` § "How to do it, step by step" says "We do not yet know which free editors are installed on your PC," then writes steps 7–9 as hand work in GIMP/Krita, Resolve Fusion, Blender and Inkscape, with no step that an agent runs and no mention of the Craft apps or the cloud. A maker following it would spend the Learn phase installing and learning five desktop GUIs on a constrained machine. Fix: nudge 1. The research note is not at fault (its own open-items list defers ArtCraft equivalents to the craft tracks); the scribe wrote from a stale charter.

## Advisor insight

Pick the reconstruction material from *her* hands, not from a style menu. The notes rightly say every unphotographed scene should be made in one hand-made material; Panh's river clay worked because it was the clay of the place he was reconstructing. For this piece the equivalent is whatever she herself makes or made: if she knits, the missing years are built in her wool and stitches; if she cooks, in her recipe cards and handwriting; if she gardens, in pressed leaves and seed packets; if she sewed the children's clothes, in those fabrics. The `story` track can find this out from relatives without tipping her off, and often the physical material already exists in the house. It makes the reconstruction honest (the hand visibly shows), personal (no other life would be built from this), and self-disclosing (the family recognises the material before any card explains it). It also quietly settles the "era look" question, because her materials changed with the decades and carry their own dates. Nothing in the notes contradicts this; it is the Missing Picture's logic applied to a living person who must not know yet.
