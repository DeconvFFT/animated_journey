## 8. Advisor insight

The hero-shot cap is not only a cost control; it is the film's thesis statement, and the plan should treat it as one. At 12 minutes with the levers adopted there are 96 shots and about five class C hero shots in the whole film. Five. Those are the only five moments where the camera leaves the photograph and moves freely through a built space, so the list of five is, in effect, the film's argument about which moments of her life deserve to be inhabited rather than remembered. Decide that list on paper before the slice, with the stranger's question first ("which five moments would make someone who has never met her lean forward"), and record it in `chapters.csv` as a `hero_moment` column so the choice is an artefact and not a leftover of whichever shots happened to be built. Then make the kitchen the only space the camera can ever enter: every hero shot returns to the one set, at a different hour and a different decade, and the film never explains why. Strangers will feel it before they can name it: this is the room she was most herself in. That gives the reuse program a meaning it does not have as a time-saver (and in Base it saves no time at 12 minutes), it satisfies Principle 2 (the device says something true), and it protects the schedule exactly where F3 says it is most exposed, because one well-built set rendered five times is the cheapest possible hero plan.
