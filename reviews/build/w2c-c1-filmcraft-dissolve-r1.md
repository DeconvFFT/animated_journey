## 2. The ruling

**Is amending the mark after seeing the result acceptable?** Yes, as a correction, and this package did it the way a correction should be done. The first wording (the average of the encoded values) cannot be met by any compositor that blends in linear light: to hit the encoded average in red the linear weight would have to be 0.70, in blue 0.33, and no frame does both. The gate review showed that from the first run's envelope; this run adds the stronger fact that the whole exported ramp is linear in linear light. The tolerance did not move (2 levels), a second criterion was added rather than removed, the first run's envelopes are kept in `first-mark/`, the generated verdict says in its decision section which mark the PASS rests on, the SP1 row calls it "a correction of an unpassable test, not a lowered bar" and names who recommended and who adopted it, and the 0.8.0 fallback's envelope was left alone with a note instead of being quietly rewritten. The driver's own docstring records the amendment and its date.

**Is `T2.dissolve_steps` a fair criterion?** Yes. It asks for at least 11 distinct levels over a 12-frame window that starts one frame before the first blended frame, which allows one pair of equal frames and fails the pair-stepping the single-frame probe showed (6 or 7 levels, as the stand-in's `stepped` defect reproduces). FilmCraft's export gives 12, the maximum the window can hold, so the margin of one is the window's, not FilmCraft's. The check reads a decoded H.264 centre patch, so it says nothing about the edges of the frame or about photographs; the report says so.

**The R8 outcome.** With T2 and T4 passing, R8 as written allows "drafts and chapters" in the container. The package applied it as "drafts" in the playbook, the skill and the SP1 row, because `ROADMAP.md` row 136 keeps the FilmCraft chapter pass on the PC and D-12 keeps the Craft apps as refinement tools with a fallback. I agree with that narrower reading: nothing in the plan depends on container FilmCraft renders, the PC chapter pass is the maker's own step, and a wider scope would need a decision the maker has not made. The generated verdict quotes R8 verbatim ("drafts and chapters"); nudge 1 asks for one parenthesis there so the three texts say the same thing.

**One chapter, one dissolve route.** Measured, not asserted: the three routes land on two different mid-tones 29 levels apart in red. The rule is in the playbook (Step 6, decision 3, pitfall 18), in step 16 of the staged pipeline skill with a pointer from step 9, and in the motion skill's quality bar, which until now carried the unpassable first wording and would have failed a correct FilmCraft dissolve; staging that fix was outside the named list and right to do.

## 5. Nudges (none blocks the approval)

1. **`verdict.py`, one parenthesis on the outcome line** (next package that touches SP1): after "agents may render drafts and chapters with the Craft CLIs in the container", add "(in this plan: drafts; `ROADMAP.md` row 136 keeps the FilmCraft chapter pass on the PC)", so the verdict, the playbook and the skill give one scope. Regenerate and `assemble.py check`.
2. **Record the probe-versus-export difference as a finding.** The single-frame `render --seconds` probe still shows pairs of equal frames (`alpha_by_frame_encoded_red`: -2 and -1, 1 and 2, 4 and 5) while the export does not. One sentence in `research/craft-motion.md` (near line 381, where `render --seconds` is the validation step) and in the SP1 "does not say" bullet: use the export, never the single-frame probe, to judge dissolve smoothness. Otherwise a later agent will "find" the stepping again.
3. **Key the amended-mark paragraph on the evidence, not on a criterion's presence.** In `verdict.py`, the paragraph names "the first run of the same pin (01:20 UTC)" and "28.50 levels" whenever the newest T2 carries `T2.dissolve_steps`; a T2 run on a later pin (0.9.1) would print it unchanged and wrongly. Emit it only when `first-mark/t2_<app>_<tag>.json` exists for that tag, and read the deviation from that file.
4. **The display-time reword in `mark_text()`.** Rewording a recorded envelope's mark text at display time keeps the stale check quiet, but the generated verdict then differs from its evidence by one phrase. Once the orchestrator's baseline re-cut (gate nudge 4) lands, prefer an allowlist entry for the 0.8.0 envelope and drop the regex, or append "(wording as recorded)" to the reworded cell.
5. **Make `--defects-only` idempotent.** It appends its 12 lines to `tests.jsonl`; run twice, or after a stale part 1, the file holds duplicates and `make_summary.py` double-counts. Drop existing `fake.defect_*` lines before appending, and refuse to run when `tests.jsonl` is missing or holds no part-1 lines.

For the maker's eye at the first real dissolve (not a nudge to this package): a linear-light dissolve is uniform in light, not in perceived brightness, so its last frames into a dark picture move fastest (the ramp's final encoded step here is 35 levels). That is correct behaviour, and the "one chapter, one dissolve route" rule is what keeps it consistent; the Saturday dissolve line and gate G4's eye on a dark-to-dark dissolve are where it gets looked at.
