None of these is in the playbook's collision table; Annakut carries the wrong date for her state in both documents.
- Template F1's row for her state is the thinnest in the table: floor-art names "UNVERIFIED", one song "lead, unread", no rite names beyond the pregnancy rite, no mention of Navratri garba (Maa Ambe is on her list, D-31) or of a sampradaya-specific rite.
- Template A has no row for a sampradaya initiation (in her tradition a child's *nāma-nivedana* and the later *brahmasambandha*, with the tulsi *kanthi* as its object: a real rite she is likely to have had and an object that may have followed her through every home, W-32). - Template C has no line from her own tradition: the eight-syllable *śrī kṛṣṇaḥ śaraṇaṃ mama*, Vallabhacharya's *Yamunāṣṭakam* (Yamuna Maharani is on her list), the *Madhurāṣṭakam*; the one Gujarati bhajan named is "lead, unread". ## 6. Advisor insight: her tradition already has a day-clock

The note borrows the Ragamala idea (a mood, a season, an hour per painting) and flags its Mughal-court lineage. Her own sampradaya has the same device with no flag on it: the daily *seva* divides the day into eight named darshans from dawn (*mangala*) to night (*shayan*), each with its own hour, dress, food and raga (UNVERIFIED in detail; for the sampradaya research track). Template H already asks when she lights the lamp morning and evening (Q16). Key the wrapper and the three modules to her lamp hours and the eight-darshan day, and the kitchen set rendered "at a different hour each time, never explained" (W-34) becomes a structure the room feels and she recognises. The film's native key, vātsalya, stops being a word we borrow and becomes the stance her tradition takes toward a child every day; and a lamp lit at the right hour on a phone screen at arm's length is the simplest shot in the film.
