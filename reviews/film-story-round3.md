## 8. Advisor insight

Change the question you ask strangers at the week-17 test screening. Do not ask whether they liked it, or whether they could follow it; ask each of them, before they say anything else, **"If you met her at the reception afterwards, what would you ask her?"** Write the answers down verbatim. If the film has worked, the questions will be specific and hers: about the ladle, the street, the saying, the thing she kept. If it has not, the answers will be compliments ("that was lovely") or questions about the maker ("how long did this take you?"), which is the self-centring failure measured in one line. The test is cheap, it needs no insider, and it measures the film's actual purpose rather than its polish: a film about a person succeeds when strangers leave wanting to talk to her. It also gives surprise-ops something it has been missing. The reception is the part of the evening that asks the most of her, because a room of strangers now knows a version of her life and she has to stand in it. If the film has planted good questions, the strangers come to her with those questions, and she has something to do that is not reacting to a film: she answers, corrects, tells the rest of the story. That is the gift after the gift happening in the room, on her terms, in her own voice, and it is the first chapter she tells herself.
