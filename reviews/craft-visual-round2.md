## 4. Critical issue (blocks approval)

**C1. The LightCraft TIFF strip/tile limit is missing from the playbook and the skill.** Research R1 item 4 and R4 establish, from issues #2198 (open, critical, 2026-10-17), #1983 and #2090 and from `crates/codecs/src/tiff_codec.rs` being identical at v0.8.0 and v0.9.1, that LightCraft cannot open a TIFF whose single strip or tile exceeds 128 MiB ("malformed TIFF data: decoder limits exceeded"); the fix PR #1989 is open with a review finding. The researcher's own table shows an 8x10 inch print at 600 ppi in 16-bit RGB (172.8 MB) fails if the scanner writes one strip, and a 3923x5877 file in the wild did fail. Playbook Step 8 item 1 ("Scan to 16-bit TIFF… use the scanner settings from `playbooks/ai-imagery.md`"), Exercise 5 and Checklist 4, and skill step 13, say nothing about it. Scanning is the one step in this project that happens once and covertly (albums are borrowed and returned), so the strip layout has to be right before the first real print goes on the glass, not discovered in the pilot. The fix is nudge 1; it is three sentences per file plus one `exiftool` check.

## 8. Advisor insight

The pilot already measures the mean RGB of each print's unprinted paper border, because that border was white the day the print was made and its drift is the print's age. Do not throw that number away after white-balancing. Record it per batch (the batches are already clustered by film stock and year) and let it drive the film: the measured cast of a decade's borders becomes that decade's title colour and the tint of its SVG chapter art (written into the rig as `data-*` so it survives VectorCraft), and the LightCraft preset for that batch neutralises only part of the drift, so a 1978 print still looks like 1978. The palette of the whole piece is then derived from how her actual photographs aged, not chosen from a swatch; that is a device that passes the charter's "must mean something" test, it is fully scriptable, and it costs nothing beyond a column that Template C already has.
