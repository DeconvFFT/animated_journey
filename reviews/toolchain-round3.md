## 8. Advisor insight

The two `r2_param.py` numbers are not only a feasibility check; they are the film's first editorial constraint, and the plan should treat them that way. At about 11 seconds per 1080p frame on four cores, a 30-second parallax shot costs more than two hours of the maker's machine overnight; a plain Ken Burns move costs seconds. That scarcity is a gift, not a limitation. A cinematographer with twenty minutes of film stock chooses where depth is worth spending, and the audience feels the choice without knowing it was made. So once Exercise 5 returns the real number, do the arithmetic once and fix the budget in the story documents: perhaps four or five parallax shots in the whole film. Then spend them only at the moments when her point of view on her own life changes, the places where, in the round-2 note, the camera changes hands. Everywhere else the photographs stay flat, as photographs are. When the room finally feels a picture open into depth, it will be because the life did at that moment, and nobody will be able to say why it moved them. The render log becomes the shot list; the slowest tool in the stack decides where the film breathes.
