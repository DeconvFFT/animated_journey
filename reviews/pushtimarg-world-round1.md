## 3. Critical issue (blocks approval)

**C1. PR18 "register separation" over-reaches from Thakorji's seva to her whole house, and would forbid Ganesh and Lakshmi together at Chopda Pujan.** Research T2 PR18, playbook T2 PR18 and core principle 7, skill Guardrails "Reverence" and Procedure step 7 all read: "Krishna seva, Shiva, Harsiddhi, Ambe, Ganesh and Lakshmi each get their own frames and chapters; never one thali, one lamp or one cloth for two." The source is the FAQ's anyashraya doctrine, which is about a Vaishnav's faith in deities other than Krishna; it says nothing about Ganesh and Lakshmi sharing a Diwali thali, and in every Gujarati Hindu home they do: Chopda Pujan is Lakshmi Maa with Ganesh Dada, one lamp, one thali, the account books between them (review 05, 0.2 already names "the Diwali Chopda" as the household's festival form). Maa Ambe's garbo at Navratri likewise sits with the household's other pictures. As written, PR18 is an agent check (`agent; maker`) that would fail the most Gujarati frame the film can have, and the skill would enforce it before the maker has answered Q14. The rule also carries source numbers as if sourced when the thali/lamp/cloth clause is the researcher's inference (J). The true line, and the one the sources support, is between **Thakorji's seva articles** (jhari, banta, seva thali, vastra, the mandir's cloth) and every other puja: those never share a thali, lamp or cloth with another deity's worship (Seva Q35 is the analogue for Mahaprabhuji's picture). Among the other deities, the film shows what her house does (Q14), and the Chopda frame is welcome. Fix in all three files (nudge 1).

## 8. Advisor insight: the first sound of the film

W-36 opens the film on "black and one true room sound from her kitchen", before any picture. In a Pushtimarg home the day's first act is Yamunaji's: fresh water poured into the jhari and the Yamunashtakam said over it before anything else is done ([1], F8). If Q7 says the home keeps Yamunajal, the truest room sound this film can open on is **water poured into a brass jhari in the dark**. To a Pushtimarg Vaishnav in the room it is Mangla itself, and the whole tradition is present in it: Yamunaji first, permission asked, nothing shown. To everyone else it is water in a kitchen before dawn, which is also true. It is an object sound (D-05 clean), the maker can record it with their own hands in an empty kitchen (no recording of her, D-17), it costs nothing to clear, it needs no card and no deity on screen, and it survives a phone speaker better than any pakhawaj. If the same sound returns under the ending beside the maker's hum, the water line the note rightly refused to fake (the Gulf of Khambhat is not the Yamuna) is carried by the one water that really is hers. Test the jhari sound on the phone fixture before G-SLICE-0; if Q7 is no, the insight is void and nothing else changes.
