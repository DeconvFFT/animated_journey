## 8. Advisor insight

The album-look rule gives the film a structural device that costs nothing and that nobody will name as a device. The fade, cast and grain of a print are not a decade's; they are the fingerprint of whoever held the camera, bought the film, chose the lab and kept the album in a particular drawer. Her mother's albums, her own, her husband's, her children's phones: each has a different hand, and the sampled look will carry it. So when the look file is filled, record one more field, *who took these*, and then order the chapters so the audience feels the camera change hands at exactly the moments the point of view on her life changes, from being looked at as a child, to looking, to being looked at by her children. No caption says it; the colour does. And the convention for reconstructions falls out for free: anything that is not from a real print gets no album look at all, clean and unfaded, so the *absence* of grain is the honest mark the charter's Principle 4 asks for. The same pipeline, one extra line in a text file, and the film quietly tells you who loved her enough to take the picture.
