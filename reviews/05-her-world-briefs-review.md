### 0.2 How to read her list of eight

The list is a Gujarati Pushtimarg household's normal breadth, not a mixed signal. A Pushtimarg core (Bal Krishna as the form served at home, Yamuna Maharani, Mahaprabhuji) sits with the deities every Gujarati Hindu home keeps (Ganesh Dada first in any puja, Lakshmi Maa at Diwali and Chopda Pujan, Maa Ambe as the goddess of Navratri and of Gujarat's great shrine) and two who are *hers* in a particular way: Lord Shiva (Bhavnagar is a Shaiva landscape: Takhteshwar in the town, Nishkalank Mahadev at Koliyak in the sea, Gopnath on the coast) and Harsiddhi Maa (a Saurashtra kuldevi shrine on the coast near Porbandar, and the Ujjain shaktipeeth). The rule for the three tracks:

1. **One idiom per register, never blended.** The Pushtimarg core is drawn in the haveli's idiom (pichwai motifs, seva objects, haveli sangeet). The goddesses are drawn in the goddess idiom of her region (the garbo lamp, the Mata ni Pachedi register if it passes 2.3, the Jai Adhya Shakti arti), never as pichwai figures. Shiva appears as the linga and the sea, never as a figure in a Vaishnava register. Ganesh and Lakshmi appear as the household's festival forms (the Diwali Chopda, the Ganesh of the threshold).
2. **Her order is data.** The maker wrote Bal Krishna first and Shiva second. Do not over-read it, and do not re-order it into a sampradaya hierarchy on a card or in the credits.
3. **The maker's list wins over the brief's geography.** The computed briefs name Khodiyar Mata at Rajpara and Gopnath Mahadev as Bhavnagar places. They are settings only. Neither becomes "her deity" unless the maker says so.
4. **Marks follow the sampradaya exactly.** The Pushtimarg tilak is a specific urdhva pundra form and not the generic "U" the sibling note's deity sheet describes for Vaishnavas; the Pushtimarg track must obtain it from a Pushtimarg source and give the agents a drawn reference. A wrong tilak on a card or a drawn figure is the first thing a Vaishnav in the room notices.

### 0.3 Vatsalya bhava: the question that decides whether this film can speak Pushtimarg at all

In Pushtimarg the devotee is the parent and Thakorji is the child: vatsalya is Yashoda's love, offered by the devotee to Bal Krishna through seva (`hindu-frameworks` Finding 4 already carries the honesty note). A film by a child about a mother cannot claim that bhava for the *maker*: the maker is not Yashoda and she is not the svarupa. The presumption to avoid is any frame that puts her where Thakorji sits (a curtain opening on her face, a pichwai niche around her photograph, a jhula with her in it, a "she is our Yashoda" card) or the maker where Krishna sits.

The non-presumptuous reading, and the one I ask the track to test with sources and, if it can, with the words of practising Vaishnavs: **the film shows *her* vatsalya, not the maker's.** Her hands that dress and feed Thakorji at home are the same hands that dressed and fed her children; the film's grammar is already hands (W-33), so the echo costs nothing and claims nothing. The deity is never on screen for it; the seva objects and the gesture are. The track's first must-answer is whether devotees would read even that as fitting or as taking a liberty, and the deity policy (O-42) is answered on the result.

### 0.4 The eight darshans: a clock for the day, not for her life

The ashta-yama seva (mangla, shringar, gwal, rajbhog, utthapan, bhog, sandhya arti, shayan: names and order to verify) is the most tempting structure on offer and the most dangerous. Mapped to life stages, the present day lands on shayan, the putting-to-sleep: in front of a mostly public room and a 50+ woman that is a sentence nobody wrote on purpose. Mapped to a day, it is already what the plan does: the kitchen is the only built set, rendered "at a different hour and decade each time, never explained" (W-34). The lean, for the track to confirm or refute: **the darshan is the hour of the kitchen render and the cue map's time of day** (sandhya arti light for a dusk chapter, mangla light for the cold open), never a chapter title, never a life stage, never a card. If a darshan name appears anywhere on screen it is because she keeps that darshan at home and the maker says so.

### 0.5 Two planks of the plan that her world bends

- **W-34, the kitchen as the only built set.** A Pushtimarg home has two rooms that matter: the rasoi and the home mandir. We cannot afford a second set (D-24), and we should not build the mandir: a built, generic shrine around a deity we may not show is the worst of both. Lean: the kitchen stays the one set and gains its Pushtimarg truth (on utsav days the kitchen *is* a seva kitchen: the sakhdi and anasakhdi distinction, the bhog cooked before anyone eats, the Annakut mound); the home mandir appears only as her own real photographs or the maker's photograph of the real shelf under deity policy P3, if O-42 allows, and otherwise not at all.
- **W-32, the object clock stands under the screen on the night.** A seva object cannot be a stage prop: Thakorji's vastra, the jhula, the singhasan, the seva thali are not candidates, however daily they are to her hands. The object clock is a domestic object (Part 2, 2.4), and the seva objects live in the style bible and the chapter openings, never under the screen. The surprise-ops reveal run sheet gains the line "the object under the screen is never a seva article".

### 1.3 Watch-outs

1. **The svarupa as a character.** Any moving, blinking, speaking, walking or morphing Shrinathji, Bal Krishna, Mahaprabhuji or Yamunaji, by hand or by AI, is out of the public cut, whatever the quality (D-16's spirit, `hindu-visual` T4). ### 3.3 Watch-outs

1. **Copyrighted recordings of public-domain songs.** "Vaishnav Jan To" and "Jai Adhya Shakti" are old texts whose every famous recording is owned; the common tune of a Gujarati "folk" garba is often a named composer's. No cue is cleared by being a bhajan or a garba. Composition status and recording status are separate columns, always.
2. **Voices.** A sung arti, a kirtan, a garba crowd, a temple loudspeaker, a radio at 6 a.m.: each is a recorded living voice and out of the premiere cut (D-05), and most carry a label's master as well. The premiere's sound is objects, rooms, the sea and the hum. Design chapters to that, not to a bhajan the April version may add.
3. **The harmonium and the sarangi.** The harmonium is in most Gujarati bhajan rooms and in many havelis today, and carries the sibling's `CHURCH_COLONIAL` flag; the sarangi and the shehnai carry court flags. Flag accurately, put the question to the maker (O-51 if she herself sings with one), and do not strip or slip either.
4. **Mixing kirtan traditions.** Gaudiya or ISKCON kirtan, Sikh kirtan (dharmic, out), Marathi abhanga or a Hindustani khayal are not haveli sangeet. Name the tradition of every cue; "kirtan" alone is not a value.
5. **Raga claims.** A drone in a scale is not "Raga Bhairav"; a credit that names a raga the recording's metadata does not carry is a tell and an error relatives will catch. The sibling's rule stands.
6. **Capturing at a haveli.** Recording inside a haveli at darshan time is intrusive, often forbidden, and means telling someone (D-02). Bells struck by the maker's own hand in their own home, or a CC0 bell as a labelled stand-in, cost nothing and offend nobody.
