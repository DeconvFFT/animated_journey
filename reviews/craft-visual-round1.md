## 3. Critical issue (blocks approval)

**C1. The pin mechanism cannot be executed as written, and the baseline it pins to is already stale.** Three documents instruct the maker to "switch the Launcher's updater off" (notes §1 pin rule; playbook Step 3 with a TODO for the setting; skill "Ethics and privacy guardrails"), but the Launcher's README documents no such switch (row 15). At the same time the notes name v0.9.0 / v0.12.0 / v0.8.0 as "newest tags today", while v0.10.0 / v0.13.1 / v0.9.1 exist (row 14). A maker who installs through the Launcher this week will get the newer builds and be silently updated afterwards, while the log, the acceptance-test expectations and the pitfalls describe the older ones. The fix is a few sentences (nudges 1 and 2), but until they land, the "pinned alpha app with a proven fallback" promise in the charter is not real.

## 7. Advisor insight

Treat restoration as a visible gesture of care rather than a correction to hide. The ORA hand-off already carries named layers; make the first layer of every restored still `L00_asfound`: the print exactly as it came out of the album, scanner border, foxing, thumbprint and all. In the piece, let a few chapters open on `L00_asfound` and resolve to the restored layer over a second or two, in her decade's colour, not a neutral "fixed" look. This does three things at once. It satisfies charter principle 4 honestly (the reconstruction is shown as a reconstruction, by the same gesture that shows the original). It turns the LightCraft pilot's "mean RGB of the paper border" from a QA metric into part of the visual language, because the borders stay in the frame. And it says something true about the maker: that someone sat with these prints, one by one, and brought them back. No new tool is needed; it is one extra layer name in `make_ora.py`, one rule in the touch-up sheet ("never paint on `L00_asfound`"), and one per-decade `.lcpreset` instead of one for all.
