## 3. Critical issue (blocks approval this round)

**C1. Yamuna Maharani is treated as a river and the Yamunashtakam as free in tune.** Three places, one fault:

- `research/gujarati-devotional-sound.md` T1-06 and `playbooks/gujarati-devotional-sound.md` Template B T1-06 give `composition_status = PD_text_and_tune (recited; no composed tune)`. In Pushtimarg homes and havelis the Yamunashtakam is *sung*, in the family's or the haveli's tune, and on records in tunes that may belong to a named composer. "No composed tune" is an unsourced claim, and `PD_text_and_tune` would let a sung Yamunashtakam recording look clear in the April column when only its text is. The text is PD by Vallabhacharya's dates; the tune is `unknown` until a specific rendering is placed, exactly as the note treats every other sung PD text.
- Playbook Template A row A6 fills the "meaning" cell for Yamunā with "River goddess", quoting Wikipedia's gloss, where every other row honestly says TODO(research). To a Pushtimarg devotee Yamunaji is not glossed as "the river goddess"; the maker's own word is "Yamuna Maharani" (D-31). A card or a gloss that reduces her to the river is the kind of line a Vaishnav in the room will hear, and the brief (05, 1.2 item 8; Part 4 rule 2) asked for her to be treated as devotees expect.
- The skill inherits both (Procedure 3 "Copy exactly", Exact texts) without the correction.

Fix: tune `unknown`; meaning cell TODO(research) with "address her as the maker does (Yamuna Maharani, Shri Yamunaji); never gloss as 'the river goddess' alone"; the Yamunashtakam card stays as pushtimarg-world has it (from the family's printed copy, or no card).

## 7. Advisor insight (what would make the film more hers)

The paper the track found confirms something the premiere's constraint accidentally shares with her tradition: **Mangla, the first darshan of the day, is the one service with no rhythm instrument at all: a conch is blown before it, a drone is held, and the deity is seen plain, "simple and unadorned"** [3]. The premiere cut, stripped by D-05 and D-07 to objects, rooms and a hum, is not a poorer version of the April film; it is a Mangla. Build the cold open on that without ever saying it: her own shankh blown once by the maker's hand in the empty prayer corner (S-A4, phone PASS), then one sustained sound (not room tone; something above 400 Hz that can hold like a drone, which the track can find), first light in the kitchen rendered at the 05:30 hour, no rhythm, no card, no name. A Vaishnav in the room will know what the first thirty seconds are; everyone else will see a conch and a window. O-46 (does she keep mangla at home) decides whether this is her morning or only her tradition's, and the maker's answer is the only approval the shot needs.
