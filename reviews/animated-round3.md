## Advisor insight

Decision 1 ("one hand-made material for everything that was never photographed") is still framed as an aesthetic choice between cut paper, clay and blocks. Make it biographical instead: the one material should be the material of her own hands. If she sews, the reconstructions are cloth, thread and pins; if she cooks, they are dough, flour and the cutting board; if she gardens, pressed leaves and seed packets; if she kept the household books, ruled paper and her kind of pen. This does three things at once that no other choice does. For the strangers in the room it still reads unmistakably as a reconstruction, which is the honesty convention's whole job. For the family it reads as her before a word is spoken, so the "visibly reconstructed" marker becomes a portrait rather than a disclaimer. And it hands `surprise-ops` a cover story that is true: borrowing her scraps, her tin of buttons or her recipe cards for "a family project" produces exactly the by-product the round-2 Q7 protocol asks for. It also sequences the decision correctly: the material cannot be chosen until `story`'s texture inventory says what her hands do, which is the charter's "story before software" applied to the one choice in this track that is most tempting to make early. The Missing Picture's clay is powerful because clay is what a child's hands could have made in that place; ours should be powerful for the same reason.
