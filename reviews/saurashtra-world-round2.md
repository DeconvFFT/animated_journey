## 8. Advisor insight: date her life by her calendar

Where the maker knows when something in her life happened, let the card give it the way her house would say it, "Maha sud Pancham" or "two days after Uttarayan", in Gujarati script with Gujarati digits, and keep the Gregorian date in the caption track for the room. Nothing is invented: the tithi of a known date is arithmetic from a panchang, which the note already knows how to read for Bhavnagar by geoname id. Every Gujarati Hindu in the room reads the card at once; everyone else reads the caption; and she sees her own life told in the calendar she actually keeps, which no generic "Indian themes" film has ever done. It costs one Drik day-page per card and one check by the native reader (O-62), and it keeps the film's clock hers from the first card to the last.
