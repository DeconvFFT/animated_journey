## 2. Reverence and the Hindu-only rule

- **Applied to motifs, not people.** W-66 and O-68 say so in writing and the plan page explains the harmonium case in one honest sentence. Good.
- **One overreach by classification.** W-66 puts "Jain, Buddhist, Swaminarayan, Parsi and Adivasi items" in one "flagged and never used" list and ROADMAP §13's saurashtra row puts Swaminarayan beside "Islamic" under a Hindu-only gate. Swaminarayan (and the Gaudiya, ISKCON and Dwarkadhish items in W-72's never list) are Hindu; they are out because they are not hers (D-31), which is a different reason with a different owner (the maker may overrule it in a line; D-28 he may not). Say which reason each exclusion rests on.
- **One rule that disagrees with itself.** The pushtimarg playbook's PR4 reads "Never photograph her Thakorji, chitraji or any picture of hers without her knowing." The register's Codes paraphrase it as "nothing in seva is photographed, touched or handled without her knowing"; ROADMAP §4.4 and PLAN rule 3 repeat the wide form and name the jhari; then W-61's first shrine moment photographs "the home mandir's closed doors ... with the jhari in its red cloth and the lamp outside", and W-70 and O-58 say seva objects enter "by sight, untouched, on an unsynced phone". The maker can read either page and believe the plan backs them. The honest line is the playbook's: the svarupa, every picture and anything inside the mandir are never photographed without her knowing; seva articles outside it may be photographed by sight, untouched, and each such photograph is a G5 flag (which O-48 already says for a wall or room photograph). If the maker as her proxy prefers the wide rule, then the shrine moment is drawn in her house's line, and W-61 should say so. Either answer is fine; the three documents must give the same one.
- **Everything else holds up**: the darshan clock as a day clock with its header as a rule; the hichko bare and empty with the camera in the room; Yamunajal filled, never emptied; no garland on a photograph of a living woman; no sanctum interior; no feet toward a linga; the invasion stories out by default with the maker's one-line yes; "Mataji" never for her; vatsalya as her household's own bhava shown through hands and objects.

## Advisor insight

The merge got the hard thing right: it refused to let her tradition become the film's structure (festivals open or frame a module, the darshan table is a day clock, the mandir's doors appear once) and kept the structure the strangers need (one place, one object, one turn). The two places it wobbles are both the same mistake in miniature, a careful rule restated more strongly than its source until the stronger form contradicts the plan that depends on the weaker one. The fix is not more rules; it is one sentence per rule, copied verbatim into the three documents, which is also what will let one learner follow this at ten hours a week.
