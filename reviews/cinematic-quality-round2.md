## 4. Critical issue (blocks approval)

**C1. MO8's "or 0.25 absolute" branch passes a shimmering push.** `research` D1 row MO8 and Q5 "A related finding"; `playbook` T1 row MO8 and step 9; `SKILL.md` step 7 and the per-shot checklist. The rule reads "no more than 1.3 times a sub-pixel reference render of the same move, **or** 0.25 absolute until calibrated on the slice (J)". FQ2 (M-C7b, three real prints, 51 clips) measured that relative roughness falls as the push gets faster while `zoompan`'s pixel jitter does not: at a 10% push `zoompan` direct from 4K read 0.094–0.098, under 0.25 and under the old 0.15, with centre jitter unchanged at 0.31 px. The note says so in its own words ("A relative test would pass a shimmering fast push from a 4K source") and then leaves the rule standing with a TODO. My reproduction today on a 4% push read 0.274 for `zoompan`, within 0.024 of the line. With "or", any push faster than about 6% passes the absolute branch regardless of the reference. The 1.3× ratio branch is sound (Pillow floors 0.03–0.08 on real prints; `zoompan` 4–20×), but it is optional as written. D4/T5 item 24 bans `zoompan` for finals, so the practical risk today is in the other renderers MO8 exists to audit (a CSS or GSAP transform that snaps to whole pixels, a Craft-app export, Blender with pixel-snapped camera keys): an agent following the skill would mark them `pass`. Fix in nudge 1. This is the only item that blocks; everything else below is a nudge.

## 8. Advisor insight

The strangers' written sheet asks question C: "In three sentences, who is she?" Treat those answers as a deliverable, not a QA artefact. Across S1 and S2 the maker will hold six to eight short portraits of her written by people who have never met her, before they knew anything but the film. That is the stranger in row seven speaking in advance, and it is the most honest test the film can get of Principle 3 (the life lives in the texture): if three strangers independently write "the kitchen table" or "the way she laughs at her own jokes", the texture is carrying; if they write "a mother who worked hard", the film is still a eulogy. Use the sentences as a G4 check (do the strangers' three sentences name a specific object, place or habit, or only a role?), and then keep them. After the reveal, in the "gift after the gift" conversation, the maker can hand her the sheets: the first portrait of her ever written by people who had never met her, and proof that the room came to know her. The sheets are anonymous, written for the maker, and reveal nothing about the viewers, so this costs nothing in privacy and nothing in the surprise.
