## 3. Music, sound and scripts

### 3.1 The music route (W-44, re-read for her household)

The spine stands, with four corrections and one new first option:

1. **The premiere's honest sound is objects, rooms, the sea and the hum.** A sung recording is a living voice under D-05, so W-44's "a relative's public-domain performance" means **instrumental only** before April; a sung aarti, kirtan, garba crowd, temple loudspeaker or 6 a.m. radio is an April column. No design depends on a bhajan before April; a beat that wants one is written as silence plus one struck bell.
2. **The hum is the MOTIF, on an open vowel.** The phone model fails a closed-mouth hum (−6 to −9.5 dB at the 500 Hz corner) and passes an open vowel ("aa"); record both takes in week 1, keep both in `originals/`, use the open take. **A hum is not free:** the hum-case table decides by Mon 14 Dec (HC-1 a Gujarati halardu from oral tradition or Meghani's *Halarda*; HC-2 a Narsinh Mehta pada, text PD, tune unknown until the arranger is placed; HC-3 a Pushtimarg kirtan, a dhol or a family pada, hum it and claim no raga; HC-4 a named twentieth-century composer such as Avinash Vyas, owned to 2044 or later: not in the premiere without a written answer, so silence plus kitchen sound instead; HC-5 a film song, HC-6 an album tune: the same). No raga name is claimed or printed anywhere.
3. **The one sound that is hers rather than the temple's or a label's:** the dhol songs of Gujarat, sung by lay women at daily work, at births and at weddings, outside the temple liturgy (devotional-sound-script N11; Mallison 1986). W-44's first source after silence is HER_TUNE_HUMMED, so ask the maker on Sun 25 Oct, under O-41/O-51: *"Which song did the women of the house sing at a birth or a wedding you were present for, and can you hum it?"* If the maker can, that is the motif: a melody sung at the maker's own birth or at a family wedding, carried by the maker's voice, with its first line as a two-line Gujarati verse card copied from a printed source. Two guards: confirm it is traditional, not a film or album setting (HC-3 versus HC-4); copy the card text from print, never from memory.
4. **Instruments and forms under D-28, with the maker's rule pending (ASK = silence in the render until ruled, due Mon 14 Dec):** out by default: sitar, sarod, santoor, khayal, ghazal and qawwali, the Ragamala idiom; ASK: harmonium (a mid-19th-century colonial arrival, "possibly" by missionaries, and at the same time named in her sampradaya's temple music by three sources), tabla, bansuri (its exclusion from the sanctum rests on one tagged page and is contested), tanpura, shehnai, ektara, Carnatic violin; in if hers: shankha, ghanta, jhanj, manjira, pakhawaj (no court use found), dhol. **No raga name on screen or in credits**, since several carry Persian-court attributions on the pages read; where a label is unavoidable use Carnatic Kalyani or none.
5. **If any instrumental cue is ever commissioned (April, after TD-L2c, name-free, under a signed s.19 letter):** first option a slow dhamar or rupak pulse on a pakhawaj under a Sa-Pa drone.
