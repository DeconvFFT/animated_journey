## 1. North star

We are making a short animated documentary about one woman's life, told by the people who were there, for a room that mostly has never met her, with her sitting in it. It is a film first: 1920x1080 at 24 fps, a real sound mix, a proper master, projected on 27 February 2027 at a public gathering and kept for twenty years on a drive she can open. It should feel the way a good biography reads: specific before it is sentimental, quiet where it could be loud, and unmistakably about *this* person within two minutes, because every device on screen is drawn from her own archive and habits. The as-found prints with the writing on their backs, the one object that followed her through every home, the cutting rate that slows when her life settled, the handwriting of whoever labelled each decade's photographs, the chapters no one but she can tell, left visibly unfinished: these are the film's grammar, and none of them would work for anyone else's mother. Game-world language is welcome only where a mechanic says something true (a chest that will not open, a room that opens only with the family key), never as decoration. Real memories stay real: AI restores, restyles, animates and connects her real photographs and the real voices of people who agreed; it invents no events, no faces and no voice. The surprise is not a constraint we hide; it is the film's structure. The premiere cut is the question her life poses to a room of strangers; the archive cut, finished with her in the weeks after, is the only version whose last word is hers. Success test (review 00): she recognises herself in details nobody else would have thought to include and says some version of "you remembered that"; a mixed-age room follows it with no instructions; it still plays in 2047.

## 2. What we learned (ten lessons that change how we build)

1. **"Pipeline locked" is a process lock in week 4, not the week-9 slice.** The lab calendar, the surprise-ops plan and the production-scale schedule model all need material from week 5. So the L1 process lock is Saturday 2026-11-14, tested on synthetic material; covert collection starts Sunday 15 November. The slice gate (Sunday 20 December) keeps its quality role. Everything that needs no gate (locating, protecting, listing found voice, lab emails, the maker's own practice) starts now. — `playbooks/surprise-ops.md` Decision 0; `playbooks/story.md` Track A/B; `ROADMAP-ADDENDUM.md` §4.

2. **The surprise's one hole is the film's structure.** The voice the film most needs cannot be recorded, so we stop pretending otherwise: an open chapter in her name, three locked rooms built from the inventory's "things only she can confirm", and a coda slot reserved in `timeline.json` for her own voice recorded after the lights come up. The premiere cut ends on its own; the archive cut is finished with her. — `playbooks/film-story.md` Template 8 and Decision 5; `playbooks/format.md` Step 7; `playbooks/story.md` Pitfall 9.

3. **The film's look comes from her archive, not from a style menu.** Every print enters as found (`L00_asfound`) and is restored in front of the audience as a gesture of care; the backs of the prints are the caption and title system; the album's measured border cast, not a decade palette, is the grade, and it changes when the camera changes hands; the one hand-made material for everything never photographed is the material of her own hands (wool, recipe cards, pressed leaves, fabric), which the story track learns before the material is chosen. — `playbooks/ai-imagery.md` "When not to restore" and Step 10; `playbooks/toolchain.md` Step 9; `playbooks/craft-visual.md`; `playbooks/animated.md` Decision 1; `playbooks/cinematic-quality.md` T2.

4. **Teach the grammar once, then break each rule exactly once.** Three honest textures (a still clean photograph; a paper reconstruction that wobbles on twos with a hand in frame; a slow push toward one detail) are keyed in the first minute. Hold length tracks the gap since the previous photograph; cutting rate tracks how settled her life was; one "still still" per chapter is a photograph she took. The single place each rule bends is the turning point of her life. — `playbooks/creator-threads.md` Part D and principle 3; `playbooks/games.md` principle 5; `ROADMAP-ADDENDUM.md` §1.4 item 4; `playbooks/cinematic-quality.md` step 14.

5. **One verb, one meaning, designed for a room.** Points, badges, timers, fail states and precision gestures are out. "Place" (from *Unpacking*) becomes an editing rule: the one surviving object is the only transition device, same framing, new room, new decade. The swap-the-subject test is the filter: if a device would work unchanged for another family, it is decoration. Interactive forms go only to the optional keepsake, with one click, auto-advance and a host pause. — `playbooks/games.md` principles 1–4, 8; Decision 9.

6. **Agents generate, apps refine, nothing critical depends on an alpha app; scripts travel, photos stay home.** Route A (drawn, seekable scenes) renders in the container; Route B (photo moves) and every full-size scan render on the PC from the same script; each photo set carries a `cloud` level, `none` by default, which only the maker raises. The Craft apps (PhotoCraft, VectorCraft, LightCraft, FilmCraft, EffectCraft) touch copies in `work\` folders under pinned portable zips; SoundCraft is learning-only. ffmpeg and ImageMagick sit under everything; Kdenlive or Resolve Free is the proven fallback editor. — `playbooks/agent-pipeline.md` Steps 1–14; `playbooks/toolchain.md` Decision 5; `ROADMAP-ADDENDUM.md` §1–2.

7. **Runtime follows the maker's hours and the material, and the slice measures it.** No published source gives maker-hours per finished minute for this kind of film; the 100–150 hour figure was judgment. The production-scale model says 12 minutes fits at about 14–20 hours a week only with the five levers (hero shots at 5%, 8 shots a minute, bought or templated finishing, photo triage, front-loaded gathering); at about 10 hours a week plan 8–9 minutes. The slice's measured `h_slice` decides the runtime on 20 December; kill-or-cut dates are written down now. — `playbooks/production-scale.md` Steps 1–3, 9–10, T2, "Kill-or-cut"; `playbooks/film-story.md` Decision 1.

8. **"Theatrical" is quality per minute, and the stranger in row seven is the first reader.** Lock the ten style-bible decisions before the slice; every per-shot claim has a pass/fail test on a projected frame; the first two minutes must answer "who is she" for someone who has never met her; the only fresh eyes compatible with the surprise are strangers-to-her, tested once on the slice (about 20 December) and once on the locked cut (week 17). The sole maker needs the 48-hour review, blind A/B and the decision log because there is no editor to be hard on them. — `playbooks/cinematic-quality.md` Steps 2, 15–16, T2, T7; `playbooks/film-story.md` Template 5; `reviews/02-film-briefs-review.md` Part 3.

9. **A public room means cleared for public screening, on every copy, and the venue decides the master.** Sync and master licences for every copy that carries a pre-existing recording (venue file, private link, archive, companion), plus public performance for the showing; "royalty-free", a venue's PRO licence and a personal streaming subscription authorise none of that. The venue is asked in writing before Sunday 15 November; its answer, not a date, decides whether the venue master is the 1080p24 H.264 file or a 2K Flat DCP derived from the ProRes master. People in the photographs who cannot be asked without revealing the film appear in the public cut only where cleared or low-risk; written releases, including hers, come after the premiere and before any cinema or online showing. — `playbooks/theatrical-delivery.md` T1, T2, TD-1–TD-8, Step 11; `playbooks/music-sound.md` Steps 6, 10–11; `playbooks/format.md` Decisions 8, 12 (with the round-3 corrections in Section 9 below).

10. **Honesty labelling is intrinsic, not a caption.** Six levels (L0 scan to L5 reconstruction) travel in the file name, `steps.csv`, XMP and the end-credit cue sheet; if it looks like a photograph it is a photograph, possibly restored, and anything invented is drawn; no synthetic voice of any real person, living or dead, even with consent; no AI animation of a real face; no face work on the dead without the family's yes; no model trained on her face, because she cannot consent to it. Because the audience sees the as-found print a second before the restoration, the label is something they watched happen. — `playbooks/ai-imagery.md` principles 1, 6–7 and Step 10; `playbooks/animated.md` Pitfalls; `playbooks/cinematic-quality.md` Decision 9; `playbooks/story.md` Decision 10.

## 4. The chapter model

**The skeleton: wrapper plus modules.** The film is a wrapper (cold open about 1:15, ending about 1:45) plus modules of about 3:00. Twelve minutes is the wrapper plus three modules; fifteen is the wrapper plus four; nine (the H≈10 fallback) is the wrapper plus two. Each module is one *told-by* chapter with a visible card between it and the next, so a guest who steps out loses one chapter, not the plot. (Reconciliation: film-story's 12-minute row of four 2:15 chapters is superseded by the 3:00 module, which production-scale and the surprise-ops shared-cut plan both use; the scribe patches film-story Template 4.)

**Eras as biomes, chapters as rooms, one object as the survivor.** Inventory the whole life first (every milestone, in the story inventory CSV), then *tell* the chapters that explain who she is, and give texture equal weight (review 00 §1.1). Map a 50+ year life this way:

| Layer | What it is | How it is chosen | Game-world reading (only if it means something) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Eras (biomes)** | Five placeholder spans: 0–12, 13–22, 23–35, 36–49, 50+ (cinematic-quality Decision 14). Each era has its own colour strip sampled from its own prints and its own "who held the camera". | By the archive: the look changes where the photographer changes (her mother, her, her partner, her children), not where a decade does. | Biome boundaries are where the light changes. No era above about 25% of runtime. |
| **Chapters (rooms)** | Three to four told-by modules, each with one teller, one place, one object, one device, one turn. | From the milestone triage (Turn, Motif, Scene, Voice, Dignity, Variety) and the through-line test; lock in order of evidence richness, not calendar order. | A room is where she kept that decade: each chapter opens on the as-found frame ("this is where she keeps 1988") and the album opens. |
| **The survivor (the object clock)** | One real object that followed her through every home (the ladle, the clock, the chipped bowl), found by the story track from relatives. | It is the film's only transition device: every chapter ends on it in its room, the next opens on it, same framing, new room, new decade; the final cut lands on it where it sits today, and the real object stands under the screen at the premiere. | *Unpacking*'s "place" made passive for a big screen; one empty slot stays for the keepsake. |
| **Milestones** | 25–40 one-off events. Treatments: dramatise (45–120 s, needs a scene with a sensory detail and a voice), glimpse (3–10 s), mention (a line on a map or list; 15–25 fit in a mention layer), hold (left out on purpose). | Six tests per row (story Step 13). The first hard beat comes no earlier than a quarter to a third of the way in. | The mention layer can be a map that fills in, if her life widened geographically; otherwise it is a list. |
| **Texture** | Repeated patterns with one concrete sensory detail each: a Tuesday, a saying, a smell, the route to school, how she ate standing up. | Texture rows from relatives, documents, found voice, and the maker's own hundred-day "I remember" practice (source R-maker, confidence C). Quota: every chapter has one texture sequence and the grey decades (about 35–55) are not read as empty. | TUESDAY interludes of 30–40 s; the still-still shot is a photograph she took. |
| **Locked rooms** | Three visibly unfinished places built from the inventory's "things only she can confirm" list. | Picked by the maker as proxy from the dark stretches of the coverage strip (`years_covered`). | A chest that will not open; a room with the light off; a relative saying "Nobody knows. Only she does." She answers at the reveal if she chooses; the archive cut gains it. |
| **The open chapter** | A held card, 6–10 s: "Chapter [N]: [Her name]. To be told by her." | Always, at any runtime (film-story Decision 5), unless the maker as proxy decides it does not suit her. | The one Vault slot that opens once and only with its own key. |
| **Two doors** | `allowed_use` per shot: `hall` for the premiere cut; `private-link` and `family-only` rooms added in the family-key cut; her cut holds what the proxy judged true but not for a crowd. | From the consent ledger; the veto window's graceful outcome is "move this room from the hall to the family key" rather than cut it. | Some of a life is for everyone; some is for the people who were in the room. |

**Density check.** At 12 minutes for a 55-year life the film spends about 13 seconds per year (film-story "How dense"); replace 55 with her real age in film-story Exercise 2. Scarce images get long holds; the recent decade gets a burst or one Tuesday, never a whole decade; no era above about 25%; say the gap out loud once ("No photographs survive from these years").

**Who is she (the first 90 seconds).** Black and one true room sound; her name said by a relative, name and relation on screen; one object in use, hands not face; the first laugh from a loving voice or her own found remark; the contract card in the maker's hand ("[Name], told by the people who were there. What is drawn is marked."); the title last. Exit test after two minutes: a stranger can say a name for her, one thing she does or keeps, what she is like to be around, who is telling them, how the film works, and why they are watching (film-story Template 5).
