# Roadmap addendum: how the theatrical-film bar changes the plan

*Fable advisor, Sunday 2026-10-18 (week 1 of 19; premiere Saturday 2027-02-27). Written for the orchestrator to merge into `ROADMAP.md`. Covers the five film tracks: `film-story` (approve), `cinematic-quality` (revise, one open issue carried in §3.6), `production-scale` (approve), `music-sound` (approve), `theatrical-delivery` (approve).*

**Baseline this addendum changes.** `ROADMAP.md` v1 and `ROADMAP-ADDENDUM.md` v1 both exist (read today in full). The roadmap already carries most of the five tracks' dates, gates and decisions; this document does not repeat them. It does three things: (1) applies the maker's answer of today, "for now only me, and I will be the only one on this project", which the five playbooks and skills already assume but `ROADMAP.md` §8 Decision 2 and `ROADMAP-ADDENDUM.md` §3.3 do not; (2) adopts the five tracks' advisor insights as named spec, column and calendar changes with their costs; (3) names, section by section, what in the two roadmap documents changes. Judgment is marked (J). No web page was opened for this document. Every tool fact below comes from the five playbooks, their research notes and fixtures, read today.

**The one-paragraph version.** The theatrical bar is quality per minute, not minutes, so the honest film is a 9-to-12-minute animated short built to a cinema standard, with 12 as the plan if the maker can give about 16 hours a week and 9 as the written fallback, decided by the measured slice on Sunday 20 December. "Only me" quietly designs two films: before the premiere the public cut cannot carry the recorded voice of any living person, so the premiere cut is the film as strangers meet her (what she kept, her own found voice if one recording passes, the maker's hands and a few cards, sound from her world), and the version delivered on Saturday 24 April 2027 is the same locked picture with the voices turned on by relatives who have now seen it. Everything else in this addendum follows from those two facts: the spine is an object cast from her present and traced backwards; hands are the grammar; the hand-made material is whatever her own hands made; the five hero shots are the film's argument and all happen in one kitchen; the sound is built from a tune she gave the maker and from the room the audience is sitting in; every removable appearance has an understudy before lock; and the strangers' first question at both screenings is "what would you ask her?".

## 2. The film's spine

### 2.1 Cast the object before the story

The roadmap's object clock (§4 "the survivor") is "found by the story track from relatives". Under only-me, cast it from **her present**, this week, with no relative asked: the kitchen table, the keyring, a shopping list in her handwriting, the chair she always takes, the sound of her front door. The maker photographs the candidates on an unsynced phone, in the light they live in, then traces the chosen object backwards through the albums as they arrive. Rule R2 (`film-story` principle 5): the first and last thirty seconds are the same object seen twice; the stranger in row seven ends the film knowing one true thing the family has looked past for years. The object is also the one thing a surprise cannot spoil, because she walks past it on the morning of the premiere, and it stands under the screen that night (surprise-ops run sheet, already in `ROADMAP.md` §5 Stage 9). Cost: one week-1 task and one `object_clock` row in the inventory. The slice can start in week 5 on evidence already in hand.

### 2.2 Hands are the grammar

`film-story` Template 5 already says "one object in use: hands, not face", and the collector stance has the maker appear as hands and handwriting. Extend it to a rule for the whole film:

- **Every chapter opens on hands holding the thing that chapter is told through** (the photograph, the letter, the object), filmed in the kitchen where it lives. Before the premiere these are mostly the maker's hands and, where a relative showed the maker an album under the true sentence in §0, that relative's hands and the pages (Tier B, no face, no voice).
- **Whoever's hands hold the picture is telling.** The public room gets one consistent rule and no caption is needed; the April version (§2.5) adds the voice of the same hands.
- **The last hands on screen are the maker's, setting the object down.** The film never shows her face as a device; the one face the room is looking at is already in the room.
- Hands carry age, work and tenderness that a stranger reads instantly, and they have no face-release problem.

Cost: a `hands_owner` field (relation only) on each chapter's opening shot in `chapters.csv`; a filming note in the album-session protocol (phone on a stand, hands and pages only, the person's face never in frame); one line in the style bible's camera grammar ("the camera may hold on hands; it never searches for a face").

### 2.3 Structure: wrapper, three kept rooms, five inhabited moments

The skeleton stands as `ROADMAP.md` §4 has it (cold open about 1:15, ending about 1:45, modules of 3:00, the open chapter card, three locked rooms, the still-still per chapter, `allowed_use` two doors). Four changes:

1. **Kept-by, not told-by, until April.** Each module is one place, one object, one device, one turn, opened on the as-found frame ("this is where she keeps 1988") and on hands. The `teller(s)` field in the chapter template becomes `teller (April version)` and `material (premiere)`.
2. **The archive's density sets the pace.** `material_in_hand_pct` in `chapters.csv` is a portrait, not only a metric: the decades with the fewest photographs of a mother are often the decades she held the camera or held everyone else. Dense decades cut quickly (about 12 shots a minute, many stills); sparse decades hold long on a single visibly reconstructed L5 image in her material (4 to 6 shots a minute); the film says the gap out loud once, plainly ("there are almost no pictures of her from these years; she was behind the lens"). Add a derived `shots_per_min` column to `chapters.csv` so the pacing decision is an artefact. This turns the schedule's largest problem (uneven material) into the film's most honest device and saves hours exactly where the model says they are scarcest.
3. **Five hero moments, decided on paper before the slice.** At 12 minutes with the levers there are about five class C shots in the whole film: the only moments where the camera leaves the photograph and moves through a built space. They are the film's thesis about which moments of her life deserve to be inhabited rather than remembered. Choose them with the stranger's question first ("which five moments would make someone who has never met her lean forward"), record them as a `hero_moment` column in `chapters.csv`, and make **the kitchen the only space the camera can ever enter**: one Blender set, rendered five times at a different hour and a different decade, never explained. One well-built set rendered five times is the cheapest hero plan there is (`production-scale` Decision 7 lite program: title system plus one set, about 8 hours), it satisfies charter principle 2, and strangers will feel before they can name it that this is the room she was most herself in. At 9 minutes it is four moments.
4. **The leitmotif comes from the maker's blind line-up.** `cinematic-quality` A3 (five of five, twice, 48 hours apart) records "what feature tipped the choice". Read it the other way round: the one or two features that survive all three age boards (6, 25, 55: the parting, the tilt of the head when she listens, the hand she rests her chin on) are the maker's own unspoken knowledge of her face, written down for the first time. They become the film's visual leitmotif: the hand-made material always gets them right, and era changes cut on a match of the same tilt of the head across forty years, the most honest "same person" device there is (it answers strangers' question E without a caption). Nothing is invented; the features come from her photographs. After the reveal it gives the maker one true sentence: "this is how I always knew it was you."

### 2.5 Two films: the premiere cut and the version of Saturday 24 April 2027

The only-me rule has designed two films, and the roadmap should say so. The **premiere cut** is the film as strangers meet her: the maker's hands and cards, her own voice where it passes, photographs, text, sound from her world. The **April version** is the same locked picture with the voices turned on: each relative who has now seen the film and signs release 4d (sent Sun 28 Feb to Sat 6 Mar) records one memory *in response to the film they just watched*, in their own kitchen, knowing exactly what the film is; her own coda, recorded only with her spoken yes after the her-turn conversation of Sat 6 Mar, closes it. Technically it is cheap: picture locked, only the DX stem and the mix change, `ROADMAP.md` already holds the 24 April delivery. Ethically it is cleaner than anything gathered under a pretext. And it gives the premiere its shape in reverse: the public room gets the film about her; she later gets the film in which the people who love her answer it. One line in theatrical Step 12, one consent-ledger row per voice, and a note in `music-sound` Step 14 and `film-story` Step 6 that the dialogue stem has a second life. The understudies of §5.4 become the places where a voice can speak over an absence rather than a face.

**Changes to the roadmap.** `ROADMAP.md` §1 North star ("told by the people who were there" becomes "told, at the premiere, by what she kept and by her own voice where it can be heard; told again in April by the people who were there"); §2 lessons 2, 7, 8 and 9 (one sentence each on only-me); §4 the whole "Chapters (rooms)" row, the "Who is she" paragraph and the template fields `teller(s)`, `consent`, plus the new `hands_owner`, `hero_moment`, `shots_per_min`; §5 Stage 1 (sessions, not interviews), Stage 5 (the slice's "a voice line" becomes "a sound line and, if it passed, one HER-PUBLIC line"), Stage 10 (the April version defined); §8 Decision 11 re-opened as above.

## 3. The quality system

### 3.1 The style bible: ten decisions, two of them now answered from the house

`cinematic-quality` T2 locks ten decisions before the slice (G1, by Sun 15 Nov). The completeness critic found a sequencing inversion: decision 2 (the hand-made material) is "due Sun 13 Dec after the texture inventory" yet locked at G1. Resolve it the way the track's own advisor insight does: **the hand-made material is whatever her own hands actually made over fifty years** (dough, thread, a garden, a ledger in her handwriting, a blackboard, a mechanic's rag), and the maker can find it in the house in week 1 without telling anyone. Ask the maker one question now: *what did her hands do most in each era?* The answer fills decision 2 at G1 with no placeholder, and decision 6 (the reconstruction marker) writes itself: the disclosure convention becomes characterisation ("the parts nobody photographed are shown in the stuff she made"). Three problems close at once: the swap test passes by construction; the generic-AI aesthetic collapses because the material is scanned (L0/L1 texture) and no model has a prior on her tablecloth; and the grain-and-paper system (decision 8) takes its paper from the backs of her prints and its texture from her material. Decision 4 (typography): OFL fonts only (§5.3). Decision 5 (camera grammar): add the hands rule (§2.2) and "the camera may enter one room only, the kitchen". Decision 9 (titles): the Card A slips traced in VectorCraft, Inkscape as fallback.

### 3.2 The colour script

Unchanged in method, named here so the orchestrator schedules it: one frame per beat from the animatic, three swatches each (dominant, secondary, accent), laid in a strip whose widths are running time, a temperature line above and a light row below; about two hours per era; frozen at G1 for the slice (`cinematic-quality` step 3). The palette is six colours or fewer per era sampled from her L0 scans and the measured `border_rgb` of each batch (`ROADMAP-ADDENDUM.md` §1.4 item 2), never a decade swatch; the look changes where the photographer changes. Numbers: ΔE00 within 6 for a shot, 4 for skin, 3 between shots in one scene. Grade once to Rec.709 / BT.1886; Trim V (lit venue: lift 2 to 4 IRE, mid-gamma +0.05 to +0.10 as starting values) is set on site at TD-L2b (by Sun 17 Jan); Trim C stays identity until a real cinema.

### 3.3 The per-shot checklist (T1) and the chapter check

Every shot brief carries its label (L0 to L5), a `because:` (reveal, link, isolate, time or hold), an ease family, `mirrored: false`, its lens and its source photos; a shot with no reason fails. Then: MO1 spec check (no framework default ease; no `linear` without `drift: declared`; no ease above 40% of a chapter's moves); the pixel audits of §3.6; finish of two global layers or fewer with the finish-off test and baked seeded grain of at least 48 frames; type at 5% of picture height, x-height 2.5%, strokes 2 px, captions `FontSize=23`, everything inside 5% margins; the maker's eye checks (eyes, mouth, hands, text, reflections, cut-out edges on three backgrounds at 200%, the AI-tells sweep T9). Chapter level: in every run of eight moving shots at least three move families, none above 40%, entropy at least 1.5 bits; holds at least 35% of photo shots; one picture-led silence of 3 s or more; shot-length coefficient of variation at least 0.4; no finishing layer on more than 80% of shots. The maker alone signs likeness (five of five, twice, 48 hours apart), and the features that tipped the choice are logged as the leitmotif (§2.3).

### 3.4 Dailies with Fable

The roadmap has the gates; it does not have the daily rhythm. Define it (J):

- **Every production day** (W5 to W9 for the slice; W12 to W16): the agents post one review pack per shot that moved (contact sheet, strips, face and type crops, a preview of 20 MB or less), scored twice by a fresh-context critic. The maker watches the day's packs **with sound on for five to ten minutes and no more**, drops markers only (red cut, yellow hold, green breath, blue wrong photo), and approves or rejects nothing until 48 hours have passed.
- **Fable reads** every pack the critic scored under 4 twice, every pack flagged for template risk, every shot that touches a face or a hard-flagged beat, and the first three briefs and first style-frame set of each chapter. Fable never judges smoothness or sound from stills.
- **Every Sunday**: burn-down, `hours.csv`, `status.csv`, and a style-drift check of the week's approved frames against the frozen G1 reference frames, so week 17 is judged against week 1 on purpose; one decision-log entry per lock (T8).
- **Blind A/B, squint, distance, half and double speed, sound-off and picture-off** passes before any chapter master (G4).

### 3.5 Test screenings S1 and S2

Keep both sessions with strangers-to-her (S1: three strangers on the slice about Sun 20 Dec; S2: three to five on the locked cut in week 17, Mon 8 to Sun 14 Feb), on a redacted copy, under the truthful partial pretext in `cinematic-quality` T7, with the cold self-read as the fallback and Fable's rubric pass mandatory in the slot if strangers are declined. Strangers are not insiders: they learn that a short film about one woman exists, never who, never that it is a surprise, so this does not touch the only-me rule (`film-story` Decision 7). Three changes to the protocol:

1. **The first question on the sheet, before anything else: "If you met her at the reception afterwards, what would you ask her?"**, written down verbatim. If the film has worked, the questions are specific and hers (the ladle, the street, the saying, the thing she kept); if not, they are compliments ("that was lovely") or about the maker ("how long did this take you?"), which is the self-centring failure measured in one line. It is cheap, needs no insider, and measures the film's purpose rather than its polish. It also closes a gap in surprise-ops: the reception asks more of her than the film does, because a room of strangers now knows a version of her life; if the film has planted good questions, strangers come to her with them and she has something to do that is not reacting: she answers, corrects, tells the rest, in her own voice, the first chapter she tells herself.
2. **Question C ("in three sentences, who is she?") is a deliverable, not QA.** Across S1 and S2 the maker holds six to eight short portraits of her by people who never met her. Use them at G4 as the check on charter principle 3 (do the sentences name an object, place or habit, or only a role like "a mother who worked hard"? the first means the texture is carrying; the second means the film is still a eulogy). Then keep the anonymous sheets and hand them to her in the gift-after-the-gift conversation: the first portrait of her ever written by strangers, and proof the room came to know her.
3. Add one viewer over 65 and, if the maker can arrange it, one teenager with a guardian to S2 (the completeness critic's point; the success test promises a mixed-age room).

Pass rule unchanged: at least 80% identify the reconstructions (D) and one person across ages (E); no unread text (F); no chapter above two tell-marks per minute.

### 3.6 The open issue in `cinematic-quality` (round 3: revise), carried here
