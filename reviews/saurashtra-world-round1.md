## 2. The Hindu-only rule and reverence (D-28, D-31; review 05 Part 4 rules 2 and 3)

**What is right.** The rule is applied by origin and community in every craft row (bandhani, gharchola and patola FLAG on the maker, not the use; Rogan and Ajrakh OUT as practised; "Kutch embroidery" named community by community; Pithora flagged Adivasi; Palitana flagged Jain and never described as a setting; Talaja's caves flagged; Swaminarayan named as another sampradaya). The Pushtimarg names are handled as devotees would expect: "Mahaprabhuji" throughout, "Thakorji article" as never an object clock or prop, the Hindola as a seva swing distinct from the household hichko, no Shrinathji or Yamunaji image proposed by this track (correctly left to `pushtimarg-world`), and Khodiyar Mata kept as a place until the maker names her. Rama and Ayodhya are kept out because they are not on her list of eight, which is D-31's "personal before generic", not a political judgment. The saathiyo is prepared in two versions and decided by nobody but the maker. The political-room rule (T2) is written once, is short, and would survive a hostile reading.

**What is wrong (the critical issue).** In two places the note applies the rule by *look* instead of by origin, which its own first principle forbids, and both are copied as defaults into the playbook and the skill:

1. **The Dwarkadhish dhwaja.** T1 row P06, T2's Dwarka row ("a held close-up of the sun-and-crescent emblem" is OUT), Reverence case 14 and the playbook's Template A, Reverence table and case 14 all treat the moon on Dwarkadhish's flag as something that "can read as a crescent to a mixed room" and so "keep it in motion at a distance, never held; ask." The sun and moon on that flag are unambiguously Hindu by origin and meaning (the page the note itself cites says they signify that Krishna is present while the sun and moon exist). D-28 asks for flags where *origin* is ambiguous; this origin is not. A devotee of Dwarkadhish, and a Pushtimarg family for whom Dwarka is a pilgrimage, would find "hide the flag because it might look Islamic" a wrong note, and it quietly gives a mixed room's possible misreading authority over her deity's own symbol. The fix is small: PASS on origin; keep "in motion, at a distance" only as the T2 composition rule that already covers every Dwarka shot; delete the crescent caution and the "ask".
2. **The word "Maa".** Q7, T7, Reverence case 11, the playbook's Principle 7, Template F, Checklist 1 and case 11, and the skill's step 10 and checklist all rule that calling her Maa on a card "sets her beside Maa Ambe, Harsiddhi Maa and Lakshmi Maa" and is "a presumption", with "Pushtimarg and goddess devotees" named as the people who would object. The source cited gives "mother" as the first sense and the goddess suffix as the third; millions of Gujarati Hindu children call their mother Maa with no presumption in anyone's ear, and no source is given for the objection. Review 05's presumption warning (0.3) was about placing her where Thakorji sits or her face inside a pichwai niche, not about a child's word for mother. The default as written pushes the film away from the plainest word in it. The fix: whatever her children call her is the word (O-49 and O-61 already ask); the only rule is a layout rule (a word for her never shares a frame with a goddess's name or epithet); delete the unsourced "who would object" line.

Neither item is a research error; both are a rule applied one notch too hard. Because the rule's exact application is what the brief asked me to check, and because the two defaults sit in all three files, this is the one critical issue. It is a half-hour fix.

## 7. Advisor insight: put the camera on the hichko

The note found the one piece of Gujarati furniture that is both daily and *hers by use*: the hichko, the living-room swing she sits in every evening (H01). It is listed as an object-clock candidate "if it travelled" and an opening hand ("a hand on the chain"). It can be more than that. A swing is a camera move built into the house: a slow pendulum, 2 to 3 seconds a period, that nobody has to animate and that passes the Florence test because it is literally where she sits. Shoot the cold open and the ending from the seat of her hichko (the maker's own unsynced phone, objects only, D-17): the kitchen doorway swinging gently into and out of frame, the chain's creak as the first true room sound (W-36 asks for one), the same seat and the same creak returning before the open chapter card. The hichko's pendulum becomes the wrapper's only camera grammar; the object clock keeps its job as the only transition (W-32); nothing is added to the structure. If O-58 says there is no hichko, nothing is lost. If there is, the film breathes at the rhythm of her evenings, and every Gujarati in the room will know the sound before they see the swing.
