## 6. Advisor insight

**Teach the audience the film's grammar in its first minute, then break it exactly once.** This track has, almost by accident, produced a complete and honest visual vocabulary with three textures: a real photograph held still and clean (this happened); a paper print that wobbles on twos with a hand or a table in frame (we made this to show you something we have no picture of); and a slow push toward one detail (look here). Most tribute films never tell the audience how to read them, so every effect reads as decoration and the room cannot tell memory from reconstruction. Open with a deliberate key, like the legend on a map: one still, one wobbling paper reconstruction, one push-in, each on something small and true from her kitchen, no captions needed after that. Then ration the rules absolutely, so that the single place the film breaks its own contract carries the weight of the whole piece: the first time a real photograph is allowed to move at all is the turning point of her life, and the room will feel it before it knows why. This costs nothing, it is built entirely from the three scripts that now pass their self-tests, and it is the exact opposite of reel grammar, where everything moves and therefore nothing does.
