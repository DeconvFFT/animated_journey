## Summary

1. **The premiere date is a Pushtimarg utsav.** The sampradaya's own tippani lists Sat 27 Feb 2027 as *Shri Nathji ko Patotsav* [3]. A haveli handout calls Patotsav "a Mahotsav in all Seven gruhs" [47], and the Vasant–Dol "khel" season puts it on about day 17 of 40 (Vasant Panchami Thu 11 Feb is day 1) [50 (D)]. `hindu-frameworks` Template D currently marks 27 Feb "minor" [R2]. It is not minor for a Pushtimarg household. The haveli handout and a Goswami's printed lectures say the day recalls Shrinathji's coming to Gusainji's house, the Satghara at Mathura, not the installation at Nathdwara [47] [64]. Ask the maker (Q1); the film must not borrow the day's meaning.
2. **Painted pichwai is a warm-season thing.** Kay Talwar (independent scholar) writes that painted pichvais "never appear in the winter season nor do they adorn the shrine on Annakut ... nor Janmashtami" [32]. The haveli's own look in the spring "khel" season is white with colour only at Gwal [8]. So of the brief's three windows (Diwali–Annakut, Dhanurmas, Vasant) none has a painted pichwai in the sanctum: Diwali–Annakut has a jewelled hanging, Dhanurmas none, Vasant is white. The only painted ones in our calendar are Sharad Purnima (Sun 25 Oct) and Gopashtami (Tue 17 Nov). The idiom must follow that or a devotee will feel it is off.
3. **The "central niche" is a physical cut-out.** In the sanctum "the cloth is cut where Shrinathji stands" and "in the temple, Shrinathji himself is never painted into the pichwai" [35]; "the hanging is in the form of an inverted U with an opening" [32]. The niche is the svarupa's place, so a niche around a person is exactly the presumption to avoid. Borrow the border, the bands and the sky; never the opening.
4. **A picture can be a svarupa.** Pushtimarg keeps three kinds of svarupa: standing (*thada*), baby (*lalan*) and *chitraji*, "painting or picture of Thakorji", which is dressed and adorned like the others [7]; a family's picture of Shrinathji can be "pusht" by a Goswami and then served daily [2]. And "the eyes come last ... opening the eyes is the pran pratishtha of the picture" [35]. So a framed picture in her home may not be décor, and drawing, animating or blinking a svarupa's eyes is the line this film does not cross. My lean on photographing her Thakorji without her knowing is firmly no.
5. **Vatsalya: the sampradaya's own stance is servant-of-the-parents, not parent.** Its FAQ says seva is done "by keeping bhaav of Daasaanudaasatv" and "on behalf of Nand-Yashoda and Mahaprabhu Shri Vallabhacharya" [2]; the parent-bhava is "an internal and unobservable personal factor" [1]. The Nathdwara descriptions do give the *darshan moods* a parental colour (Mangla "bala bhava, the feelings of a parent concerned about a child's well-being"; Sandhya Aarti "matrubhava") [12], while the season of the premiere is described as madhurya, not vatsalya (D) [50]. Three checkable rules follow (F1): no seat, acts not bhava, offered first.
6. **The eight darshans work as a day clock and are traps as a life clock.** Table T3 gives the names, hours, moods, poets and raga families, the light in Bhavnagar at each hour, and what each would wrongly imply as a life stage; the header forbids that mapping. The anti-monotony rule is checkable: kitchen hours must not rise through the film in darshan order. A home does not keep all eight: the sampradaya's own routine lets a household do "one time, two times or three times a day", and a person who goes out to earn may do Mangla to Rajbhog and then put Thakorji to rest [1].
7. **The utsav table (T5) differs from Drik in three places and is inconsistent with itself in two.** Dhanteras: tippani Sat 7 Nov, Drik Fri 6 Nov. Annakut: tippani Mon 9 Nov, Drik (Delhi) Tue 10 Nov, while the same site's festival page says "Kartak Sud Ekam". Prabodhini Ekadashi: tippani Sat 21 Nov only (the Vaishnava day Drik calls Gauna), Drik Fri 20 and Sat 21 [45]. And from 24 Dec the tippani's printed sud/vad words are inverted against the moon (11 Feb prints "Magha vad 5" for Vasant Panchami, which is a Shukla day). Dates and festival names are usable; the printed fortnight words are not. None is resolved here.
8. **Baithaks: Saurashtra has twelve, Bhavnagar none.** Mahaprabhuji's 84 baithaks include nos. 55 to 67 in Saurashtra and Kutch: Morbi, Jamnagar, Jam Khambhalia, Pindtarak, Mul Gomti, Dwarka Harikund, Gopi Talav, Bet Shankhoddhar, Junagadh, Prabhas (Somnath, "Triveni Nadi ke ghat"), Madhavpur (Kadam Kund) and Delvada [29]. No Bhavnagar entry in the address list or the Wikipedia table [28]. (A search digest claimed "No. 43 is at Bhavnagar"; I opened the list: no. 43 is Lohagarh, Goa. The claim is false.) The water-side ones (Prabhas, Dwarka, Bet Dwarka) are where her place and his lineage can share a frame.
9. **The Pushtimarg tilak is not the generic Vaishnava "U".** Wikipedia, citing Brill's Encyclopedia of Hinduism, gives the Vallabha form as a "double vertical red line which is rounded at the base" (the U read as Krishna's lotus feet) [24]; a lay blog agrees and assigns a *single* red line to the Rudra/Vishnuswami line [26]. Both are weak; one sampradaya print (the Trust's 2002 English Manual) confirms a "'U' shape vermilion mark" but not the double line (FU3). Spec in T8, flagged for the maker's own sighting.
10. **Yamunaji is present in a home as water.** The jhari holding Yamunajal, "usually covered with a red cloth", is "an essential part of the seva" [8]. The water line of her life (Koliyak, Somnath's shore, Gomti ghat) cannot carry Yamunaji's meaning without a label and need not; the truthful bridge is a real bottle of Yamunajal if the home has one (Q7). Yamunashtakam verse 1 is given in Devanagari, Gujarati script and IAST (T9); no card is recommended.
11. **Open-access pichwai is thinner than it looks.** Cleveland's 1937.454 (anonymous, 1825–50, Nathdwara) is CC0 and the best candidate, but its centre is the svarupa, so the film could only hand-trace the cow border. Cleveland's 2018.153 by the head of the Shrinathji temple's painting department, who died in 1931, is flagged "Copyrighted" by the museum although Indian term (life plus 60, if published in his lifetime) ended in 1991; AIC's 20th-century Annakut pichvai is not public domain; the Met's Nathdwara paintings are a Subhash Kapoor gift (OUT: the donor was convicted in India in 2022, FU7); a "Festival of Cows" pichwai at the Met is attributed "Deccan, Aurangabad (?)" and a Hyderabad pichvai at AIC is Deccani (both D-28 flags). Table T6.
12. **Chitrakars and motion.** GI no. 748 "Nathdwara Pichhwai Painting" was certified 31 Jul 2023, valid to 11 Apr 2030, holder Mewad Art and Craft Sansthan [36]. The Artists of Nathdwara organisation (founded 30 Dec 2014; 100+ pichvai artists) names its three heads [34]. I found **no documented adaptation of pichwai to motion, titles, brand film or projection** after four searches (adaptations found are textile and fashion); the film writes its own rules (T1).
13. **Her list of eight is the normal breadth of a Gujarati Pushtimarg household:** the Krishna seva core, the deities every Gujarati home keeps (Ganesh, Lakshmi, Ambe) and two that are hers in a particular way (Shiva, Harsiddhi). The film records her house; the sampradaya's published view of other deities is in E33 and is never commentary. The one rule that follows is about Thakorji's seva articles, not her house: they stay apart from other deities' puja, and the other deities are shown as her house keeps them (PR18; Q14).
14. **Three layers need a D-28 flag.** The shringar vocabulary (kulhe, jama-vagha) has a Mughal-court layer: the sect's own history page admits the court's influence on its taste in dress [10], and a low-reliability blog adds a patron's Mughal-style outfit (D); sampradaya hagiographies often frame Vallabhacharya as saving Hindu religion "from the savage attacks of Muslim rulers" [15] and tell Shrinathji's journey as a flight from Aurangzeb [12]; and the Deccani golden pichvai belongs to Gujarati merchants of Hyderabad worked by Hyderabadi artists [33]. The film shows none of these (PR22).

## Findings by question

### F0. Pushtimarg in brief (for the agent that has not read it)

- **Founder and shape.** Shri Vallabhacharya (Mahaprabhuji, 1479–1530) founded Pushtimarg ("path of grace", Vallabha Sampradaya, philosophy Shuddhadvaita); his descendants, above all his son Vitthalanatha (Gusainji), built its liturgy [18]. Followers are prominent in Rajasthan and Gujarat [18].
- **Seva, not worship.** "Service offered out of love and devotion to God"; its unique features are "Raag, Bhog and Shringar" [6]. It is done to a *svarupa* treated as a living child: fed, dressed, sung to, put to rest [18]. There is "no fixed quantum of materials or strict rituals ... mainly based on pure love towards the sevya swaroop"; a householder serves "according to limited time, place and facilities" [1].
- **Svarupa kinds.** *Thada* (standing, e.g. Shrinathji), *Lalan* (baby, e.g. Balkrishnaji, Navnitpriyaji), *Chitraji* (painting or picture), and, "occasionally", footprints, handprints, handwritten scripts and footwear of Mahaprabhuji and Gusainji [7]. A home svarupa is accepted ("pusht") by a Vallabhkul Goswami [2].
- **Haveli.** Pushtimarg temples are called havelis, each considered a "'home' of the deity"; the eight daily darshans are the times he "gives darshan ... when he is relaxing in his sitting room. Usually these times coincide with a meal" [6]. Shrinathji is at Nathdwara (Rajasthan), installed there in 1672 [12] (the Wikipedia page on Nathdwara painting says 1670 [21]; the older story of the journey from Braj is not told in the film, PR22).
- **Everything offered first.** "Everything, including material possessions, are first offered to god and than enjoyed as his prasad" [6]; "a pushti-devotee is required to maintain his entire life style by taking/using only those items which are offered to Lord (Samarpit)" [1].
- **Yamunaji and the first meeting.** "Yamunaji is the adi-guru. She initiated Vallabhacharya in the marg" [6]. The Yamunashtakam is the first of his sixteen Shodasha Granthas [11].
- **Initiation and marks.** Brahmasambandha is given by a descendant of Vallabha; a Vaishnav wears two tulsi malas [16] and a tilak (T8).
- **Not in this note:** the sampradaya's internal houses (seven gruhs, whose rituals "are also varying among the main seven houses ... in some aspects" [1]) and whose tippani her haveli follows (Q1).

### F1. Vatsalya without presumption

**How the sampradaya's own pages describe it, and who holds it toward whom.**

- Four bhavas are named: dasya, sakhya, madhura, vatsalya; "Vatsalya bhava treats Krishna as if he were a child" [18] (S, Wikipedia citing Bennett and Barz; no sampradaya page ranks them in what I read). The Mangla darshan's mood at Nathdwara is "bala bhava, the feelings of a parent concerned about a child's well-being"; the Sandhya Aarti darshan's "predominant mood is matrubhava (motherly love)" [12] (S, temple-linked blog, 2013). A fan portal puts Yashoda into the narrative of Mangla, Shringar and Sandhya ("Mother Yashoda conducts aarti ...") [14] (S, independent portal).
- **The sevak is not Yashoda.** The FAQ: "In Pushti-bhakti-sampradaay seva is performed by keeping bhaav of Daasaanudaasatv ... we do service of Shri Krishna who is the son of Shri Nand-Yashodaji ... We do not have Adhikar (qualification) like Vraj Bhakta ... Therefore, we do Seva of Shri Krishna on behalf of Nand-Yashoda and Mahaprabhu Shri Vallabhacharya etc." [2, Seva Q20] (S). A devotee-run blog that cites the temple and PushtiDham puts it as "Mother Yashoda is the role model for worshipping Shri Krishna as a child, but both these arise from the foundations of devotional servitude" [17] (S, secondary).
- **The bhava is inward.** Mahaprabhuji's rule is to treat Thakorji "as divine characters of Shri Krshna as infant, child, boy", and "an emulation sentiment of Vraja-devotees is an internal and unobservable personal factor" [1] [2] (S). What shows are acts: the sampradaya lists *padasevana* as "to clean the place of Krishna-worship, wash His dresses, utensils, paintings, making His ornaments, preparing flower necklace, food etc." [1] (S).
- **The premiere's season is not vatsalya.** For the 40 Vasant–Dol days "the significant bhav ... is Madhurya" and the head-ornament *kulhe* is not offered because it "is offered with the Bhav of Vatsalya" (D, a search digest of Pushtimarg pages that I could not open) [50]. If true, a vatsalya echo in a February film is out of season for a haveli; the film is not a haveli, but the point stands that bhava is the sevak's season-by-season inner stance, not a mood to stage.
- **Precedent for likening a living mother to Yashoda.** None found in sampradaya text. Adjacent evidence: pads cover "lilas as they would have been in every season, every time of the day and every range of emotion" and are sung "in the presence of the God during seva, either in the haveli or at home" [6]; "lay Gujarati devotees, particularly women, sing the dhol song tradition" [18]; the sampradaya's daily routine has the sevak "offer respects to parents, elders and others according to status" before Mangla [1]. Yashoda Jayanti (Fri 26 Feb 2027, Drik per [R2]) is not in the sampradaya's tippani [3], so the film does not hang on it. The common speech habit of calling a mother "Yashoda" is outside my sources: the maker's own ear decides (Q5).

**What a practising Vaishnav would find fitting or presumptuous (derived from the above; no practitioner asked).** Fitting: hands doing the same *acts* in both kitchens; the quiet; the order of offering; no svarupa on screen; mother honoured as a mother (respects to parents are in the sampradaya's own morning routine). Presumptuous: any frame that makes her the parent *of the svarupa* or the svarupa *herself*; showing bhava as an expression (a rapturous face turned to a picture); a card that says "Yashoda" or "Maiya" about her; a child's plate and a bhog thali match-cut as the same object.

### F2. The eight darshans as a day clock

Names, order and the mood of each come from the Nathdwara booklet text as reproduced by the temple-linked blog [12] and a haveli site [15]; hours from the independent fan portal [14] with the blog's "around 3.30 p.m." [12]; ragas from [12] (poets and a first raga list) and [14] (a second list); the home routine from [1]. Other readings of the hours exist and differ by 15 to 45 minutes: [52] (read first-hand in the follow-up) gives Mangla Aarti 05:45–06:30 and Shayan Aarti 19:30–19:45, and [51] (also read first-hand) gives Mangala 05:15–06:00 and Shayan 18:50–19:30, so each hour in T3a now names its source line (FU6); the fan portal itself says "exact opening times can fluctuate by 10-15 minutes" [14]. Treat every hour as indicative and as Nathdwara's, not Bhavnagar's.

**What the sources say that changes how the clock is read.**

- Two darshans are explicitly parental in mood (Mangla, Sandhya Aarti) [12]. That makes them the two most dangerous to hang on a person (F2 cautions in T3).
- After Rajbhog "the Lord retires for three hours: Anavasara" [12]: the doors close. Closed doors are a true, quiet state of a haveli and of a home.
- **Shayan is not held for half the year.** "This darshana is usually not held for the six months, from Chaitra Shukla 1 to Ashvin Shukla 9" [12] (S, single source, 2013; the fan portal [14] and the other two timing pages [51] [52] state no break, and no Nathdwara source of this year confirms or denies it: FU9). On the tippani's own labels that period ends at Dashera, Tue 20 Oct 2026 [3]. So, if that 2013 statement still holds, the whole film window (21 Oct to 27 Feb) is in the half in which Shayan darshan is open, and "Shayan as the present" would be a statement about the very season of the premiere.
- **A home keeps fewer.** The sampradaya's home routine runs Mangla, Shringar/Gwal, Rajbhog, "Seva Anavsar", Utthapan with Bhog-aarti, Shayan; "one time, two times or three times a day, one can perform seva"; those who must earn may "follow rituals from Mangla to Rajbhog and then make Shri Thakurji retire to bed" [1]. A haveli FAQ adds that aarti at home is only for a household that serves sakhdi, and only at Mangla, Rajbhog and Shayan [16]. The simplest form is "Mishri Seva", "also known as Bhav Seva", done under the "agnya" of a Vallabha Balak: "a vaishnav wakes up Thakorji, serves mishri and Jariji, then puts Thakorji to sleep" [16 Q68] (S). Which darshans she keeps at home is Q3.
- **The Vasant–Dol season is the premiere's season and it re-keys the day**: white soft furnishings and pichwai, colour play at Gwal, gold and enamel jewellery rather than pearls [8] [2, Seva Q41]; "Shri Mastak" kulhe not offered (D) [50].

### F3. Pichwai as an idiom, not as a niche

**What a pichwai is, per the people who paint and study it.** "Pichvai, literally meaning 'displayed at the back', is a painted or decorated cloth, that is suspended behind the Krishna svarup in a Pushtimarg haveli. They can be painted, printed, woven or embroidered" [34 (AoN)]. In the Shrinathji sanctum the hanging is "an inverted U with an opening for the life-sized child Shrinathji"; "in other temples and private shrines, an unperforated rectangle of cloth" [32]. Raja Ram Sharma (master painter trained under the temple's head artist; via Rooftop, a commercial platform, 19 Aug 2026): "in the temple, Shrinathji himself is never painted into the pichwai. The svarup is the murti. The cloth serves it ... In the bazaar, the pichwai must carry him" [35]. A lay site adds that "due to the time it takes to create painted pichois, they are not used as often as most people think" and that in homes pichois "are by necessity small" [8].

**What can be borrowed for a film about a person, and what cannot.**

**Palette rules, and what the phone does to them.** The palette is **not** to be sampled from a museum scan. I measured Cleveland's CC0 1937.454 (a faded textile; Cleveland: "usually faded and worn"): about 46% of its pixels are saturated, but the most saturated vermilion, orange and blue clusters come out as #853E34, #744D37 and #354145, dull browns and greys. Pairing rule (J, my WCAG contrast arithmetic on the starter hexes): any line that carries meaning and any border stroke needs at least 3:1 against its ground; cream on indigo is 10.8, gold on indigo 5.7, vermilion on cream 4.2, but vermilion on indigo 2.6, leaf green on vermilion 1.2, gold on cream 1.9 and saffron on cream 2.1 all fail and may meet only as areas, never as 4 px lines. The phone (hindu-visual F3 [R1]): the dense lotus and cow repeat "goes to mush" below a 6 px repeat unit; meaning-bearing line 4 px, decoration 3 px, dots 6 px; equal-brightness colour pairs fail after chat recompression. My proposal for a cow border: silhouette height no less than 48 px at 1080p so that legs, horns and tail are 6 px features (J; to be proved on the phone chart before G1).

### F4. The Nathdwara chitrakars today, and motion precedents

- **Who paints (names only where a page confirms).** Nathdwara painters live in "Chitron ki gali" and "Chitrakaron ka mohallah" and work under a master [21] [20]. ### F6. Reverence norms for the svarupa and Thakorji

**May Shrinathji's image be reproduced at all?** Yes in practice, and the sampradaya does it itself: pilgrim paintings of the haveli [12]; a gallery of devotees' home svarupas "kindly sent in by" their owners [9]; Nathdwara painters selling "Shrinathji ka shringar" [34]. I found no sampradaya page that forbids reproduction. But a picture "pusht" by a Goswami is *in seva* ("Chitra Swaroop-Shrinathji had been pusht by H.H. Goswami ... We do seva from Mangala to Shayan") [2, Seva Q35], and the shringar guide treats a *chitraji* like any svarupa: it is dressed in cloth that is "slipped on, covering the edges" or in flat clothes "tailored to look like dhoti, char-vagh, gherdar", and jewelled with "malas and flat shrungars for the head", all stuck on with lagau [7]. So the same print can be décor in one house and a living child in another. **The film cannot tell which a picture is by looking**, which is why the rule is not "do not mistreat it" but "do not put it on screen unless the maker knows and her answer allows" (PR2, PR4).

**May it be animated?** I found no sampradaya or Nathdwara statement on animation or AI images of Shrinathji (one search on 2026-10-19 returned nothing on any such dispute). The practice that speaks to it is the painters': "The aankh is made last ... opening the eyes is the pran pratishtha of the picture, the establishing of life in it. Until the eyes are made, it is a painting being worked on. When the eyes open, Shrinathji is present in it" [35]. A blink, a gaze that follows, or a morphing face is that act performed by software. **Lean: never** (PR1, PR3). Sibling precedent: Puri's January 2026 police complaint over AI deity video [R1 F5].

**Cropped, placed low, dissolved, shown without the season's shringar.** No sampradaya ruling on crops, placement or dissolves; the sibling's R2, R4, R5 stand and the film shows no svarupa anyway. "Without the season's shringar" is not merely irreverent but *wrong*: "the shringar tradition of Shrinathji is fixed for all three hundred and sixty five days ... the vaga, the dupatta, the mukut or the pagdi, the garland, whether a lotus mala or another, the kamal in the hand, the chhadi, even whether the feet are bare or in moje, all of it is fixed by the tithi and the utsav ... The mistakes come from those who only copy pictures of pictures" [35]. An agent cannot draw him correctly; only a hereditary painter who "goes to the temple that day" can (PR2).

**Is photography inside the haveli forbidden?** At Nathdwara phones and cameras are barred: "Mobile phones, electronic cameras, smart watches, leather belts/wallets, and luggage are strictly prohibited inside the temple premises" (independent portal [14]); "Mobile phones, socks, and shoes are not allowed ... lockers near the gate" (Wikipedia, uncited sentence [23]). The Board's own notice is unread (site not retrievable). **Do the temple's darshan photographs carry a copyright claim?** Not found; none of the three pages read says. Treat any darshan photograph as the temple's or the photographer's: out, as in the sibling's R17.

**What do devotees do with a Thakorji image on something to be discarded?** No ruling found (the sibling found none either, [R1] F6). The one practice on record: havelis "discard damaged items of seva" such as worn soft furnishings, which is why old painted pichois reach the art market [8]; a pichoi is a backdrop, not a svarupa. Rule (J): no deity image or seva article is ever shown torn, burned, binned or on packaging, and the production prints nothing with one.

**How do families treat the home Thakorji's privacy?** No norm found against photographing; the practice on record is consent (owners sending photographs to a gallery [9]) and quiet: seva is kept up "without disturbing his Deity, his family and his neighbor" [1]; the home mandir is "the Nikunj of Gopijan", and "both doors of the temple represent the eye lids of Shri Swaminiji (Radhaji), who will grace us to open the eyes. This way we get darshan of Thakorji through the eyes of Swaminiji" [16 Q81]; seva pauses for a birth or death in the family (sutak) and at other times [16 Q98]. **May the maker photograph her Thakorji without her knowing? No**, on reverence (the doors) and on D-02 and D-17; the maker's one allowed photograph is of her *pichwai or picture only if O-48 and O-52 allow, in a way she knows about*, which a surprise makes hard: so the default is none.

### F8. Yamuna Maharani

- **Iconography.** Agni Purana (via Wikipedia): "black in complexion, standing on her mount, the tortoise, and holding a water pot in her hand" [27]; lotus as symbol [27]. In Pushtimarg: "Shree Yamunaji is always seen in clothes and ornaments similar to Shreenathji" [15]; shown "wearing the 'mukut-kachani ka shringar', offering a lotus garland to the Lord" and, at a marriage ceremony, in a special head-dress, the *sahra* [9]; "favorite fourth queen of Shri Krishna" (*krishna-turya-priya* in verse 3) [2] [30]; "the Beloved of ShriNathji ... literally fluid form of Bhakti" [15]. So: dark, kurma, pot and lotus, and in the haveli's own pictures dressed like Krishna. Never a Ganga-like white goddess.
- **In daily seva.** Seva begins "after obtaining the permission of Shri Yamunaji, Shri Acharyacharan, Shri Prabhucharan" and the Yamunashtakam is recited with the morning water ("Pour fresh drinking water in Jhariji and offer it to our Lord and recite Yamunastak") and at Rajbhog [1]. "Jhariji, containing Yamunaji's water, is an essential part of the seva. It is usually covered with a red cloth" [8]. Yamunaji is "adi-guru" [6].
- **The Yamunashtakam text.** Nine verses (eight plus the verse of results), colophon "iti shrivallabhacharyaviracitam" [30]. Devanagari text: sanskritdocuments.org "Yamunashtakam 3", encoded and proofread by DPD, 24 Feb 2017, volunteer text "to be used for personal study and research" [30]. Roman text on the sampradaya's FAQ [2]; the two agree on verse 1. **No printed sampradaya edition was read**, so the edition is a web e-text: UNVERIFIED against print. Beware other hymns titled Yamunashtakam (by Raghunathji, Devakinandana, Vanamali Shastri [31]; and a Shankaracharya hymn with a Kalindanandini refrain): they are not his. Verse 1 in three scripts is T9. **Recommendation: no Yamunashtakam card**; if the maker wants one, she holds the family's printed copy and the card is checked against it (hindu-visual R19).
- **Can the water line of her life carry Yamunaji's meaning without a card?** No, and it need not. Yamunaji is a specific river of Braj, who "holds the position of ... intermediary" between Shree Goverdhannathji and his devotees on a haveli's own page [15]; the Gulf of Khambhat, the sea at Somnath and the Gomti are other waters, and a film that lets them stand in for her would assert something the tradition does not. What is true and quiet is the jhari with its red cloth in a hands shot (T4 H7). A real bottle of Yamunajal in the home is the only honest bridge between the two waters (Q7). The Yamunashtakam's fifth verse has her water meeting Ganga's: if the maker ever wants a confluence image, Prabhas's Triveni is a place where that image is literally true of three rivers (Hiran, Kapila, Saraswati: UNVERIFIED), not of Yamuna.

### T8. The Pushtimarg tilak as a drawn reference (the sibling's generic Vaishnava "U" is not it)

**What the sources say.** Vallabha Sampradaya: "double vertical red line which is rounded at the base ... This 'U' shape represents Krishna's lotus feet ... materials ... derived from saffron plants" (Wikipedia citing Narayanan, Brill's Encyclopedia of Hinduism Online) [24]; the same form, "joined" lines read as the oneness of jivatma and paramatma, with the *single* red line assigned to the Rudra/Vishnuswami line and read as Yamuna Devi, in a lay blog of 2011 [26] (probably derived from the same source). The general Vaishnava mark in the sibling and in Lochtefeld is "two or three vertical lines ... intercepted in the middle by an elongated U" [25] [R1 F5]: **a different mark** (the pan-Vaishnava urdhva pundra, which in other lineages is usually white or yellow: J). Both of those Pushtimarg statements are weak. The follow-up (FU3) read two sampradaya prints: the Sri Vallabhacarya Trust's English Manual of 2002 [65] names the signs of a Vaishnava as "Tilaka and Kanthi ('U' shape vermilion mark and Tulasi garland)", which supports the U and the vermilion, and a Goswami's Gujarati lectures [64] say the devotee's tilak is made from prasadi kumkum and is the mark of the Lord's feet. Neither says "double line", "rounded base" or anything about women, so those details still rest on [24] and [26]. The same lectures say the chhapa stamps (shankha, chakra, gada, padma) of the Ramanuja and Madhva sects have lapsed in Pushtimarg [64]. A sampradaya page confirms only that a tilak is applied daily, "twelve Tilaks, six impressions" [1] and that Gokulanatha "defended the sect's right to wear tilaks and tulsi mala" [18].

**Drawn reference (J, from the text above; to be checked by the maker against a real forehead or picture).**

```
    |   |      two vertical strokes, red (kumkum or saffron-derived), from the hairline
    |   |      to just above the bridge of the nose
    |   |
     \_/       joined at the base by a round curve: a "U" with NO central stroke
```

Proportions are my own: stroke 4 px at 1080p (the floor), centre gap about three stroke widths, height about three times the width. Minimum drawn size 28 by 20 px; below that do not draw it (a 4 px stroke is lost on a small face and the mark would read as a smudge). By the sources read: not white, not a leaf, not a central stripe, not three horizontal bands. Women's forehead mark: no Pushtimarg print or page read describes one (FU3); ask (Q6). If the maker's family shows a different form, hers wins and this table is amended.

## Reverence notes, exclusions and ambiguous cases

Rule applied (D-28, review 04 section 0.1): name the origin honestly, flag, default **out**, put the exception to the maker in one line, never present a shared motif as Hindu to get it past the rule. "Hers beats generic": if a flagged item is on her actual cloth, in her actual picture or at her actual haveli, it is hers and the maker decides that line. Numbering continues the sibling's exclusion list (E1 to E26 in [R1]).

**Safer patterns, in order of preference (the brief's own list, checked against what I found).** (1) Seva objects shown by hands (T4). (2) Motifs that are not a figure: the moon disc, a lotus-pond band, a cow border, a kadamba or banana leaf, a lamp, a jhari with its red cloth. (3) A closed door, a closed parda, an empty doorway. (4) Her own picture or pichwai as a still, only if she knows and O-52 says yes. (5) *Darshan stills from public-domain sources*: **none recommended**: every open-access Nathdwara pichwai I found centres the svarupa, so only a border could ever be traced (T6). (6) A painted svarupa: only a hereditary painter, only by written licence, only the day's shringar (PR2).

## Questions for the maker

Each is answerable from your own knowledge or by looking at the house, none would tip her off if seen, and none asks you to write anything identifying into the repo (answer "a haveli in town", not a name). They extend O-46 to O-54 in [R4].

1. **Q1. 27 February is Shrinathji's Patotsav, a mahotsav for her sampradaya.** Does she keep it, and does it clash with the evening or the day? Which haveli's tippani does she follow (the seven houses can differ by a day)? *Why it gates:* the premiere's meaning and logistics; the film must not borrow the day.
2. **Q2. Which day does her haveli keep Annakut:** Mon 9 or Tue 10 Nov? And Dhanteras: Fri 6 or Sat 7?
3. **Q3. What does she serve at home:** a standing form, a baby form, a picture, none; and is a picture dressed? Which darshans does she keep (all, Mangla to Rajbhog, or Mishri Seva)? Does she serve sakhdi (that needs her Acharya's permission) or anasakhdi and milk items only? *Why:* the kitchen's truth (VR-3), T3b and T4.
4. **Q4. Is there a pichwai, a Shrinathji picture or a Mahaprabhuji picture in the house now, and is any of them "pusht"?** Nothing in the seva space, and no picture or pichwai of hers, is photographed without her knowing (PR4, D-02, D-17); the default is none. A wall or room photograph is the maker's call as her proxy and is logged as a G5 flag. *Why it gates:* PR4; O-48 and O-52.
5. **Q5. What does she call Thakorji, Yamunaji and Mahaprabhuji, and what do her children call her?** Does the idiom of calling a mother "Yashoda" belong to her family's speech, or would it ring false? *Why:* T7 and VR-1.
6. **Q6. Tilak and kanthi:** what mark do the men of the family wear (the double red line of T8, something else, none), does she wear a chandlo, does she wear the two tulsi malas?
7. **Q7. Does the home keep Yamunajal in a jhari?** (the only honest bridge to the water line).
8. **Q8. Which of these do her hands actually do:** stitch a small cloth for Thakorji, string beads, thread flowers, draw a saathiyo or rangoli, make bhog? *Why:* style-bible decision 2 (O-06, O-40).
9. **Q9. Does the family go to a Saurashtra baithak** (Prabhas, Dwarka, Bet Dwarka, Madhavpur, Junagadh or another)? Bhavnagar has none in the lists I read.
10. **Q10. Hagiography:** may every invasion or Aurangzeb narrative be left out of the film entirely (the default)?
11. **Q11. Which of these does she keep as hers:** Dhanurmas, Uttarayan on the 14th or the 15th, Dev Diwali, Yashoda Jayanti?
12. **Q12. Hatri and phool mandali:** does her haveli or her home do either? A hatri is a small decorated shop, with scales, dry fruits and sweets, in which Thakorji is seated for the Diwali days; a phool mandali is a bower of flowers built around his seat as a manorath (both defined from a Goswami's printed lectures [64], FU1). The film leaves both out until you say.
13. **Q13. The sakhdi, the offering, the kitchen:** is it true in her kitchen that nothing is eaten before it is offered, and that onion and garlic are never used? If not, the film shows what is true, not what the sampradaya asks.
14. **Q14. Her list of eight beside Krishna seva:** does she keep Lakshmi Pujan, Ganesh, Shiva, Harsiddhi and Ambe in the same house as Thakorji's seva? Nothing asks her to change; it tells me how her house places them: whether Thakorji's seva has its own room or shelf, where the other deities sit, and whether Ambe is kept as garba, as the Pushtimarg Navratri, or both (PR18; practice evidence in FU8).

### FU1. Hatri (hatdi) and phool mandali (review item 1)

Both are defined in one printed source, [64], Gujarati lectures by a Vallabhkul Goswami on seasonal, festival and manorath seva. "Printed page" is the PDF page minus one. I translated the passages from the page images (S).

- **Hatri** (also written Hatari, hatdi), printed pp. 151 to 152. Nandrayji's cattle-herding made him "half vaishya" (and his kinship to Vasudevji half kshatriya, which is why Dashera is kept), so "by vaishya connection the Diwali-hatri utsav is also celebrated"; "in the days of Deepavali Thakorji is seated in the hatri, and scales, dry fruits and sweets are set before him"; the hatri pads have Thakorji tell everyone to buy goods from him ("not from Dau-dada", that is Balaram) at a fair price. So a hatri is a small stall or shop set up for the Diwali days, with the svarupa seated in it as the shopkeeper and scales, dry fruits and sweets laid out. That matches the lead and the sampradaya's festival page ("Hatari should be decorated" [4]). Nothing read says what a household hatri is made of or whether her household sets one up: UNVERIFIED for her house.
- **Phool mandali** (printed ફૂલમંડલી, phool-mandli), printed pp. 111, 143 and 251. P. 111, in a critique of showy seva: "the flowers in Thakorji's phool-mandali are tied so that they show towards those who come for darshan", while Thakorji "seated inside sees only the stalks of the flowers and the thread"; it is grouped with the Annakut display. P. 143: it is the lecturer's example of a manorath (a devotee-sponsored special seva) that a child of a house may do, after which Thakorji keeps that manorath's utsav each year. P. 251: it is listed with the hindola and the palna as a decoration, and "such manoraths can be done in our house". So a phool mandali is a bower or enclosure of flowers built around the svarupa's seat as a manorath, grouped with the swing and the cradle. Its season is not stated on any page read, so the first pass's "summer" lead stays UNVERIFIED.
- **Adjacent, same lectures (S, printed p. 258):** in the Holi days a household makes a kunj (a bower of a few trees and creepers) and lets Thakorji play there; in summer a platter of water with a tortoise and flowers is set before Thakorji for the Yamuna-bank lila. Not applied anywhere.
- For Q12: both can now be put to the maker in one line each. Both stay out of the film until she answers.

### FU3. The Pushtimarg tilak and the women's mark (review item 3)

- **A sampradaya print for the U and the red.** [65], the Sri Vallabhacarya Trust's English "Manual of the devotional path of Pusti" (Mandvi-Kutch, 2002; its imprint gives www.pushtimarg.net), section "4- Signs Of Vaisnava", PDF p. 67: "external signs like Tilaka and Kanthi ('U' shape vermilion mark and Tulasi garland)". That supports a U-shaped mark in vermilion. It does not say double line, rounded base, men or women. (S)
- **What the Gujarati lectures add** [64] (S): the devotee applies a tilak because "our Krishna wears a tilak" (printed pp. 45 to 46); the tilak all year is made from prasadi kumkum, the kumkum first used to mark Thakorji at the big utsavs (p. 116); the tilak is the sign of the Lord's feet, worn on the forehead with the tulsi mala at the heart (pp. 175 to 176); and the chhapa stamps (shankha, chakra, gada, padma, as in the Ramanuja and Madhva sects) have lapsed in Pushtimarg, revived only lately by some Goswami balaks on certain occasions (p. 150). So no chhapa stamps on a Pushtimargi body.
- **The men's double red line** is therefore still Wikipedia citing Brill [24] and the 2011 lay blog [26]; the sampradaya print confirms the U and the vermilion only. T8's drawing (two strokes joined by a round base, no central stroke) is consistent with the print and goes no further than it.
- **The women's forehead mark:** no Pushtimarg print or page read describes one. The McGill thesis [66] has nothing on it. UNVERIFIED; Q6 stays with the maker.

### FU4. What Shri Nathji's Patotsav commemorates (review item 4)

- **The Baltimore handout [47], read in full today (S)** (the first pass's HTTP 403 did not recur with a browser user agent): "Meaning of 'Paat' is to place the swaroop of Lord in Sinhasan. - **The Goswami's printed lectures [64] say the same and say what it is not (S):** printed p. 132, Shrinathji was seated on the pat in the devalaya on Akshaya Tritiya, but Pushtimarg does not celebrate the Patotsav then; "we celebrate Shrinathji's Patotsav on Phalgun vad 7, because on that day Shrinathji came to Shri Gusainji's house and accepted his lineage as his servants", leaving the temple's decorum for the decorum of a Goswami's house. Printed p. 222: the Akshaya Tritiya installation is "a historical patotsav", not the one celebrated in Pushtimarg; Shrinathji came to Shri Gusainji's Satghara on Phalgun vad 7 and stayed there until Narsimha Chaturdashi; Shri Vrajrayji said no utsav in Pushtimarg could be greater.

### FU8. How a Gujarati Pushtimarg household places Thakorji's seva beside its other deities (review item 8)

No source states how a Gujarati household arranges pictures of Ganesh or Lakshmi relative to Thakorji's seva (same room, same shelf, separate shelf). The practice evidence that exists:

- **Thakorji's space is its own.** McGill thesis [66], p. 84: because Shrinathji "is regarded as a member of the family", every Pushtimarg home may have "a space dedicated to the performance of daily worship", "a small corner in a room, or an entirely separate room"; p. 254: in upper-class homes "separate rooms constructed for domestic shrines and food preparation" mark status. The Goswami's home-mandir slides [67]: the nijmandir is Thakorji's own space, "even if it is just a small wooden cupboard", set so that it "can remain separate from the usual activities of the devotee and his family". (S)
- **The strict form keeps other deities' images out of the shrine.** [66] p. 56, n. 43: "orthodox Puṣṭimārgīs will not visit temples or sacred shrines dedicated to other deities. Most Puṣṭimārgīs also do not celebrate festivals like Navarātrī in their homes nor would they have images of other deities in their domestic shrines." A scholar's footnote about practice, not a survey, and not about her house. (S)
- **Navratri in Pushtimarg is the Swaminiji and ashtasakhi utsav, not Ambe's.** [64] printed p. 152: "we do not celebrate Navaratri as worship of the popular devi", because Thakorji has eight sakhis and one Swaminiji, nine devis: sprouts are sown, Swaminiji's utsav is kept with the ashtasakhi, and Dashera is Thakorji's victory. Whether her house keeps Ambe's garba, this, or both is Q14's to answer. (S)
- **Lakshmi and Chopda Pujan are in sect households despite the doctrine.** [64] printed pp. 150 to 151: Diwali Lakshmi pujan, ledger worship and new account books "continue even today in many places", "a laughable tradition has become prevalent in our Pushtimarg", which the lecturer calls plainly anyashraya, tracing its entry into the Goswamis' own houses to the sect's merchant followers who kept their accounts. A statement about sect history; never applied to her family (E36). The doctrine's own answers: Lakshmi Pujan on Dhanteras is anyashraya [2] Anyashray Q2; Ganesh Puja at a marriage has "no dosh" [2] Tenets Q10 (both in E33).
- **Net:** a range, from "no other deity's image in the shrine" (the strict form) to Diwali Lakshmi and Chopda Pujan in the same house (widespread by a Goswami's own account). The corrected PR18 rests on the doctrine, which concerns Thakorji's seva and a devotee's prasad, and on this practice evidence, which shows Thakorji's seva having its own space. It does not rest on any statement about Ganesh and Lakshmi sharing a thali, which no source forbids. Where her house falls is Q14.
