Hindu-visual track: dated licence and policy excerpts for image sources, read on 2026-10-19 (cloud container).
Each block says what was read, how, and what could not be read. Nothing here is legal advice, and nothing here is a full capture: the web-fetch tool returns short quoted fragments (about 125 characters at most) plus a summary, so the quotation marks below enclose only fragments the tool returned as quotes. Where a page blocked us (HTTP 403 or 429) the block says so and the terms are marked "search result only".
Rule for use: a licence on a museum's collection page is a statement about the museum's own photograph or record. Re-read the licence on the individual object page and save that page's text (T8 `licence_text_saved`) before any image enters a shot.

==== 1. Cleveland Museum of Art, Open Access (https://www.clevelandart.org/open-access) read 2026-10-19 via fetch tool
"High-resolution images and data related to the CMA's collection are made available under a" Creative Commons Zero (CC0) designation.
Content covered: "images, data, software, and documentation"; "includes more than half of the museum's collection".
Excluded when: the CMA has not yet created a digitized image or data; "the artwork is under copyright or has other restrictions from the artist, donor, or lender"; "the artwork is not fully owned by the CMA".
Attribution: CC0 works "do not require attribution or citation" (the museum suggests a citation format anyway).
Launch: "in January 2019, the CMA launched its open access initiative."
Object page read: https://clevelandart.org/art/1986.63 "Raja Sansar Chand of Kangra and courtiers", c. 1783, "Northern India, Pahari kingdoms": "Public Domain ... You can copy, modify, and distribute this work, all without asking permission."

==== 16. Copyright Act, 1957 (India) ====
16a. Section 17 text on Indian Kanoon (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1404402/; no "as on" date on the page) and the full Act (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1136195/; shows the 7 June 2012 version) read 2026-10-19. Fragments: "the author of a work shall be the first owner of the copyright therein"; clause (b) "in the case of a photograph taken, or a painting or portrait drawn, or an engraving or a cinematograph film made," "for valuable consideration at the instance of any person," "such person shall, in the absence of any agreement to the contrary, be the first owner"; proviso: for a work incorporated in a cinematograph film, "nothing contained in clauses (b) and (c) shall affect the right of the author in the work" referred to in s.13(1)(a).
16b. Section 19: "No assignment of the copyright in any work shall be valid unless it is in writing signed by the assignor"; the assignment "shall identify such work, and shall specify the rights assigned"; royalty and other consideration to be specified; rights lapse if not exercised within one year unless otherwise provided; "If the period of assignment is not stated, it shall be deemed to be five years"; "If the territorial extent of assignment of the rights is not specified, it shall be presumed to extend within India."
16c. Section 22: artistic works published in the author's lifetime: copyright lasts "until sixty years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the author dies." Raja Ravi Varma died 2 October 1906, so on this rule his copyright expired at the end of 1966 (public domain in India from 1 January 1967). Arithmetic is ours.
16d. Cross-check: the consolidated copy at https://copyright.gov.in/Documents/Copyright_Act_1957.pdf (PDF metadata 2018) was read on 2026-10-18 for research/sources/theatrical-delivery-round2-excerpts-2026-10-18.txt item 4 and gives s.17(b) in the same words. Currency after 2018 is UNVERIFIED; the consult (T10) confirms the current text.

==== 17. Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999, s.22 (https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/geographicalindicationsofgoods/22.php) read 2026-10-19; text has typos
Infringement by a non-authorised user who uses the indication so as to indicate or suggest "that such goods originate in a geographical area other than the true place of origin" (a), or in a manner that "constitutes an act of unfair competition including passing off" (b); sub-section 3 reaches use "in translation of the true place of origin or accompanied by expression such as 'kind', 'style', 'imitation'" for certain goods. The Act is about goods and misleading origin; it does not make a painting style owned. India Code PDF (not opened): https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1981/1/a199948.pdf.
