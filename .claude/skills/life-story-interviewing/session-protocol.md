# Interview protocol (one page, v0.1)

Standalone part of the `life-story-interviewing` skill. Moved from Recipe 3 of `research/story.md` in round 1 (2026-10-18). The slate line and the three-doors line are also inline in `SKILL.md`.

## One-page interview protocol

**BEFORE (a week out)**
1. Pick one stage (`question-bank.md`) and 5-10 questions; choose 1-3 prompts (object, photo, song, map) [2]. Reserve the rest.
2. Pre-talk (Tier 2 only, which under only-me means nobody before the premiere: this protocol applies to the April-version response recordings after release 4d, or to anyone TD-L2c brings in on Sun 3 Jan; `DECISIONS.md` D-03, D-06, S-10; not recorded; do not let them tell the whole story yet) [11]: why you are doing it; who will hear it, and the use in one sentence: "It was shown at a gathering on Feb 27 that was mostly public, and may be shared with family afterwards by private link; you can restrict any part."; that AI tools will be used to transcribe and tidy audio and may be used to restore or animate real photos but will not invent events or voices (charter); they can skip any question, stop, restrict a passage, review it before use, and withdraw it until a lock date [8]. Promise only what you will do [10]. Hearing and comfort: ask where they want to sit; speak up for hard-of-hearing narrators [11].
3. Consent: spoken consent on the recording plus the written release (4d for a relative's April-version recording; `DECISIONS.md` W-48), signed at the end if possible (many oral historians prefer signing after the interview so they can address anything sensitive) [8]. Before the premiere no consent card is read to anyone (S-10); album-and-hands sessions (W-49) take a spoken "fine" on the file and nothing more.
4. Length: 45-60 minutes with a break (Baylor average 60-90; frail or memory-affected narrators 30-45) [10][35]; book a two-hour slot with time for tea [11].

**SET-UP (10 minutes)**
5. Quiet, soft room; fridge and clocks off; phone on Do Not Disturb; lossless setting; plugged in [10][37]. Phone on a table, stand or folded cloth, between you, not in the hand [37]. One speaker: 5 to 6 inches from the mouth [37], or 6 to 12 inches [89]. Two speakers: the distance is inference, to be settled in Exercise 2. Second device if possible.
6. Test: speak, play back, adjust [10].
7. Slate on tape: names, full date, place ("narrator's home"), session number, topic [8][10].

**DURING**
8. Open with an easy present-tense or object question. Chronological order at the start, then wander [11].
9. One question at a time. Then wait. Count to ten in silence [10]. Nod; no "uh-huh" [11][12].
10. Follow up: "Tell me more", "And then what happened?", "What did it look/smell/sound like?", repeat their last words [3][36][60].
11. Ask for the scene, not the summary: "Tell me about one time."
12. Do not correct. Note contradictions to check later [10].
13. Jot unusual words and names to spell afterwards [10]. Watch for fatigue: fidgeting, repeating yourself, flat answers; wrap up [35].

**IF SOMEONE CRIES OR GOES QUIET** (assembled from [32][36][11][35]; ordering is my edit)
14. Stay. Say nothing for a few seconds. Offer a tissue.
15. Say something plain ("That's a lot."), not "How do you feel?" [32].
16. Offer three doors: stay with it, take a break, or move on [32]. Do not change the subject for them [36]; follow their lead if they ask to move on [11].
17. Keep recording unless they ask you to stop [32]; mark the time. Afterwards ask whether that passage should be kept, restricted or removed [8].
18. If they seem to be far away or overwhelmed, stop [32]. Finish with an easy present-tense question (what's for dinner?) (inference).

**CLOSE**
19. Ask a reflective question ("What was it like to talk about this today?") [5].
20. Say the recorder is still running and ask if that is all right, or stop it visibly. Never record someone who believes they are off-tape (OHA consent [8]; inference). Then ask the last question, S10.4 (subject) or R10.2 (relative); it is the likeliest place for an unguarded remark (inference). Thank them.

**AFTER (within 24 hours)**
21. Copy to two places; name files `YYYY-MM-DD_person_session##.wav`; never edit the original [10][38].
22. Make a time-coded index (every 5 minutes or less) and a list of spellings [10]. Run Whisper locally for a transcript [39][40].
23. Send a thank-you; give a date for when they will hear or read it [10][32].
24. Populate inventory rows with source and timestamps. Put anything hard in `drawer` and `consent_status`.
25. Review: Did they get tired? What did we miss? Next session's stage and prompts.
