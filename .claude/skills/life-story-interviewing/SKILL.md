# Life-Story Interviewing

**Reconciled with `DECISIONS.md` (2026-10-19, after the her-world merge); the register wins.** The maker is the only person on the project (only-me, D-03): nobody is briefed, nobody is read the consent card, and no inside-helper role exists. The gate for real gathering is P-LOCK, the process lock, Sat 14 Nov 2026; covert collection starts Sun 15 Nov (D-13, W-06, W-10). Her world sets the calendar and the languages: she is from Bhavnagar, Gujarati-speaking and a Pushtimarg Vaishnav (D-31), her Diwali week (Thu 5 – Sat 14 Nov) is pre-P-LOCK with no asks of any kind (W-10), and the family's languages are asked about at the sitting (O-10, W-57). Before the premiere the sessions are album-and-hands sessions under a true sentence, with a spoken "fine" on the file (W-49); relatives' voices are recorded in response to the film after release 4d and arrive in the April version (D-06). Tier 2 below is a dated placeholder that fires only if TD-L2c (Sun 3 Jan 2027) brought someone in.

## Purpose

Gather and shape the texture and milestones of one 50+ year life for the Life Journey piece, without inventing anything. The work produces three things. First, logged and timestamped source material. Second, an inventory of milestone and texture rows, each with a source, a confidence grade and a consent status. Third, a through-line that has passed its checks. The method is in `playbooks/story.md`. The evidence is in `research/story.md`. This folder is the procedure: this file, plus the standalone files listed under References.

## When to use

- Preparing a session, a question set or a relative briefing.
- Running a session, or reviewing its transcript.
- Setting up or logging a recording, or transcribing one.
- Building or updating the inventory.
- Choosing the vertical-slice chapter, or testing a through-line.
- A conversation turns to a hard chapter.

## When not to use

- Animation, narration writing, visual design or game mechanics. Other tracks cover those.
- Any direct contact with the subject while the charter says the project is a surprise.
- Clinical or therapeutic reminiscence. If someone is in distress, stop and point to appropriate help. This is a gift, not care.
- Filling gaps with scenes, dates, sensory details or quotes that no source contains.
- Sending private recordings or transcripts to a third-party service without the maker's explicit say.

## Procedure

1. Read the known facts in `CHARTER.md` before anything else. Surprise mode is fixed there (2026-10-18). If the facts have changed (for example, the subject now knows about the project), stop and ask the maker which mode applies.
2. Check the gate. The gate is P-LOCK, the process lock, on Sat 14 Nov 2026 (D-13, W-06); sessions start Sun 15 Nov (W-10). An earlier recorded Door A session with the consent script is itself a bringing-in decision and waits for TD-L2c (W-49). Track A work can run now: the relative list, the found-voice list and practice. Before the gate you may run only tests, with the maker's own voice or a practice partner who agrees. Do not schedule real sessions.
3. Write down the mode and the proxy. The proxy for hard chapters is the maker (charter, principle 5). Only the maker knows about the project; anyone else brought in is a dated entry in the people tab under playbook Decision 2, and the list reads "nobody" (decided, D-03) until TD-L2c (Sun 3 Jan 2027). In surprise mode the subject is not interviewed. Work through relatives, documents, objects and **found voice**: existing audio and video of her (voicemails, answering-machine tapes, home video, clips on relatives' phones). Found voice is a source class of its own, and in surprise mode it is her only route to her own voice. Ask every relative, as an ordinary family request (Tier 1, step 5), and never in her Diwali week, Thu 5 – Sat 14 Nov (W-10): "Do you have any video or voice message of her on your phone? Please don't delete anything." Log each item with its holder, format and rough date. Copy as soon as the relative agrees, by cable or direct transfer, never through a chat app (`surprise-runbook.md`, Found voice).
4. Choose one life stage and 5 to 10 questions from `question-bank.md` in this folder. Choose 1 to 3 prompts (object, photo, song, map) and keep the rest in reserve. Evidence for the prompts: smell cues bring back older, vivid memories, but rarely [41]. Songs from ages 15 to 30 work best, per [46]; we extend the lower bound to 12 to catch first records (inference). In one older-adult study about 70% of such songs evoked a memory, against 44% of other songs [46]. Before the premiere the maker asks the song question of themselves: the tune she sang to you, hummed in two takes (closed-mouth and open vowel), uncleaned, into `originals/` in week 1 (O-07, W-65); a relative's sung recording is an April item (D-05, W-44). Use six photos, not sixty, with two that are not celebrations [58]. The cultural life script expects the middle years (about 35 to 55) to be grey, not empty [47], so ask for their ordinary good texture as well as their events.
5. Ask each relative in one of two tiers. **Tier 1 (the default; not brought in; unrecorded):** no briefing about the project. Ask the found-voice question as an ordinary family request, and ask where the albums and papers are. Stories heard in normal conversation are written up afterwards by the maker as `R` rows with `consent_status = not-asked (surprise hold)`, and are never quoted in the film without later consent. **Tier 2 (void under only-me; a dated placeholder that fires only if TD-L2c, Sun 3 Jan, brought someone in):** brief the relative with the honest script below before any recording. Keep the pre-talk to logistics and consent, so the story is not told before recording starts [11]. Log a dated `told_on` line and a `scripted_line_if_asked` in the people tab. Do not invent a cover story that asks the subject to act on camera [30]. Tell the relative that remembering can hurt, and let them ask questions first [33]. Sort them into Door A or Door B on the people tab (`surprise-runbook.md`).

   **Relative script (Tier 2 only; honest; no cover story):**
   > "I'm making a piece about [NAME]'s life as a gift. It is a secret from [NAME] until [date]. It will be shown at a gathering on Feb 27 that is mostly public, and may be shared with family afterwards as a copy on their phones, with no online link until the releases close; you can restrict any part. I'd love 45 minutes of your memories; you can skip anything, and nothing you say will be used without your OK. Please don't mention it to [NAME] or anyone who might."

   Then the found-voice question, in the same conversation:
   > "Do you have any video or voice message of her on your phone? Please don't delete anything. Please don't forward it to me or post it; just tell me what you have and roughly when it was made."

6. Set up the room and the recorder. Use a quiet, soft room. Put the phone on Do Not Disturb, plugged in, set to lossless (iPhone) or at least 128 kbps (Android) [37]. Stand it on a table, a stand or a folded cloth, not in the hand [37]. For one speaker: 5 to 6 inches from the mouth [37], or 6 to 12 inches [89]. For two speakers the distance is inference, to be settled in Exercise 2 of the playbook. Run a backup phone [10]. Test: speak, play back, adjust [10].
7. Open with the slate, said on tape: names, full date, place, session number, topic. Say the place as "narrator's home", not a street address [8][10]. Template:
   > "This is [your name], recording [narrator's name] at the narrator's home, [full date]. Session [NN]. Topic: [topic]. The recorder is running."
8. Run the session. Before the premiere it is an album-and-hands session of 20 to 30 minutes (W-49); the full shape of 45 to 60 minutes, with a break, is for the April response recordings (D-06) [10][35]. Open with an easy, present-tense or object question. Go in order at first, then let the relative wander [11]. Ask one question, then wait, and count to ten if needed [10]. Nod; do not say "uh-huh" [11]. Follow up with "tell me more", "what happened then?", "what did it look, smell or sound like?", or repeat their last words [3][36]. Ask for the scene: "Tell me about one time."
9. Do not correct the narrator. Note each contradiction with its timestamp, to check later [10]. Note unusual names and words, and check spellings afterwards [10].
10. Watch for tiredness: fidgeting, repetition, flat answers. Wrap up [35].
11. If someone cries or goes quiet: stay, say nothing for a few seconds, and offer a tissue. Say something plain ("That's a lot."), not "How do you feel?" [32]. Offer three doors, in these words:
    > "We can stay with it, take a break, or move on. Your choice."

    Do not change the subject for them [36]. Keep recording unless they ask you to stop, and note the time [32]. Afterwards, ask whether the passage is kept, restricted or removed [8]. If they seem far away or overwhelmed, stop [32].
12. Close. Ask a reflective question: "What was it like to talk about this today?" [5]. Then say the recorder is still running and ask if that is all right, or stop it visibly. Never record someone who believes they are off-tape; that breaks the consent they gave [8]. If they agree, ask the last question, S10.4 or R10.2 from `question-bank.md`. It is the likeliest place for an unguarded remark (inference). Then thank them.
13. Within 24 hours: copy the original to two places and never edit it. Rename it `YYYY-MM-DD_person_sessionNN.wav`. Make a time-coded index, every five minutes or less, and a spellings list [10][38]. Run the transcript locally, so the audio stays on the maker's machine [39][40]. Run it in the language spoken (Gujarati first, D-31) and expect a human correction pass; a transcript in Gujarati script is read by the maker or the one native reader before any line reaches a card or a caption (O-62, W-75). Mark unclear words as unclear. Do not guess. The full after-session steps are in `session-protocol.md`.
14. Propose inventory rows from transcripts and documents only. Each row needs a source code, with a quote and a timestamp. Never invent a sensory detail or a date. Leave unknown cells empty. If two accounts conflict, make two rows and do not average them. The maker confirms the confidence grade and the consent status. Columns and rules are in `inventory-rules.md`; the template is `inventory-template.csv`. `allowed_use` is the widest screening the person has agreed to: `hall`, `private-link`, `family-only` or `none`. `source_type` names the kind of source (`session-S`, `session-R`, `album-hands`, `photo-print`, `document`, `found-voice`, `camera-roll`, `messaging`, `social`, `email`, `record`, `newspaper`, `genealogy`, `map`, `none`; `DECISIONS.md` W-58, the source-type matrix fills in the acquisition, consent and leak-risk route per type). The CSV header, for copying:

    ```
    id,type,date,date_precision,age,place,people,what_happened_or_repeats,objects,sensory_detail,recurrence,source,source_type,confidence,consent_status,allowed_use,drawer,treatment,motif_tags,who_can_verify,notes
    ```

    Source codes: `S` subject, `R` relative, `D` document, `O` object, `P` photo, `V` found voice.
15. Grade each row's confidence. A: a dated original record, and at least one person agrees. B: two or more independent people agree, with no record. C: one first-hand memory. D: second-hand, family legend, or conflicting accounts [79].
16. Check the texture quota for each life stage. Include at least six texture rows from at least four categories (morning routine, meals, transport, sound and music, home layout, sayings, money, pets, clothes, work, weekly rhythm, annual ritual). Include at least one texture row per decade each for a place, an object, a saying and a smell or sound. Aim for more texture rows than milestone rows (`inventory-rules.md`; inference).
17. Triage each milestone on six tests: Turn, Motif, Scene, Voice, Dignity, Variety. Give it one treatment. Dramatise needs a 45 to 120 second scene and a sensory detail. Glimpse is 3 to 10 seconds. Mention is a label, a date or a line. Hold means left out for now. Write one reason per decision that points to a test. The method and the treatment tiers are in `beat-sheet.md`. It is untested, so test it on the vertical-slice chapter.
18. Find the through-line. Harvest recurring verbs and objects, the narrator's own chapter titles [5][6], and traits named unprompted by two or more relatives with different scenes. Write three candidates. Each is one sentence, with three timestamped quotes and one counter-example.
19. Test each candidate. Own words: the key words appear in the transcripts. Independence: two witnesses with different scenes. Counter-example: you found one (if not, keep looking). Would-they-say-it: in surprise mode there is no insider: the maker answers from their own knowledge (O-11) [53]. No default redemption arc: the pattern is common, its link to well-being is mixed, and it is not a template [7].
20. Build a beat sheet: time, description, emotion, recurring image, what is withheld, and a mood graph [17]. Check the shape against the register: the public cut is 9 or 12 minutes (W-01, W-02), a wrapper (cold open about 1:15, ending about 1:45) plus modules of about 3:00 (W-04). The 25-minute budget in `beat-sheet.md` is superseded (D-06: the April version is the same locked picture with voices). Keep the first hard beat no earlier than about a quarter to a third of the way in [17]. The module internals are untested.
21. Set the consent checkpoints. Re-check every item's consent status and drawer at the rough cut (Sun 31 Jan) and at the lock (Sat 6 Feb). In surprise mode the first viewing is the maker's cold read (48 hours unopened, Fable's flags first). After the reveal, the subject has her say at the her-turn conversation (Sun 7 Mar 2027 after about 14:00, else Mon 8 Mar; Sat 6 Mar morning only if she does not fast; W-48), and nothing is shared beyond the room until she agrees; releases 4d to 4g still run Sun 28 Feb – Sat 6 Mar; relatives first see the film as the April response recordings (D-06) (inference) [8][32].

## Checklists

### Before a session
- [ ] Gate passed for this recorded session (P-LOCK Sat 14 Nov; sessions from Sun 15 Nov; no asks in her Diwali week); no early Door A session without a TD-L2c decision
- [ ] Mode and proxy written down
- [ ] Relative is Tier 1 (nobody is briefed under only-me); the true sentence is ready and a spoken "fine" will be captured; the found-voice question was asked (the Tier 2 honest script exists only if TD-L2c brought someone in)
- [ ] Stage, 5 to 10 questions and 1 to 3 prompts chosen; reserve list ready
- [ ] Level check done; backup recorder running
- [ ] Slate line ready; off-limits topics known

### During a session
- [ ] One question at a time; waited at least ten seconds once
- [ ] Asked for at least one scene
- [ ] No corrections; contradictions noted with timestamps
- [ ] Tiredness checked; break taken at 45 to 60 minutes
- [ ] Any hard moment: three doors offered, time noted

### After a session (within 24 hours)
- [ ] Original copied to two places, untouched
- [ ] Time-coded index and spellings list made
- [ ] Local transcript done
- [ ] Inventory rows added, each with source, quote and timestamp
- [ ] Hard passages in the right drawer, with consent status
- [ ] Thank-you sent; what was missed is noted

### Each inventory row
- [ ] type, date and date_precision set; ranges and anchors preferred over guessed exact dates
- [ ] source code with timestamp
- [ ] texture rows: one concrete sensory detail
- [ ] confidence graded A to D; conflicts split into separate rows
- [ ] consent_status, allowed_use and drawer set
- [ ] who_can_verify is a person

### Each hard chapter
- [ ] The maker has decided the chapter twice, 72 hours apart, after Fable's flag, and the drawer is right (D-04)
- [ ] Named third parties checked
- [ ] Told briefly: the hard fact in a line or two, then the person
- [ ] Words are the narrator's own; no AI-edited speech
- [ ] An exit route is built in

## Quality bar

| Area | Good | Mediocre |
|---|---|---|
| Question | "Walk me through a normal Tuesday in [year], from the alarm to bed." | "What was your childhood like?" |
| Session | 45 to 60 minutes; several sensory details; one scene told in full; silence allowed | 90 minutes of summaries; the interviewer filled every pause |
| Inventory row | Source, timestamp, one quoted sensory detail, confidence graded | "Warm family memories," with no source |
| Milestone decision | A treatment and a reason that points to a test | "Important, so dramatise" |
| Through-line | Three candidates; timestamped quotes; a counter-example found | A theme that suits the film we want, not the person |
| Hard chapter | Decided by one-key-three-guards, brief, in their own words, with an exit | A dramatised tragedy with music and no consent trail |

## Ethics guardrails relevant to this skill

- Never invent events, people, dates, quotes or sensory details. Mark every reconstruction as a reconstruction (charter, principle 4).
- Quote exactly. Do not AI-edit speech. Keep the narrator's own perspective [8].
- Do not solicit bottom-drawer material: secrets, trauma, the cause of an estrangement. If it is volunteered, log it as "bottom-drawer (do not use)" and move on [34].
- Do not cold-contact a relative who may have a difficult history with the subject. There is no insider under only-me: decide alone, and default to not contacting them [30].
- Do not use a cover story that makes the subject do something on camera [30].
- Keep recordings and transcripts local unless the maker gives explicit approval for a service. Do not use the StoryCorps app or archive for private material [4].
- Do not let the subject find out through group chats, shared photo libraries or shared devices (charter).
- AI disclosure (AI tools transcribe and may restyle real photos) goes into release 4d after the premiere, because nobody is read card 4a before it. No cloned or synthetic voice of any real person, living or dead, even with their consent (decided, D-16).
- Every voice and cue is cleared for a public premiere, or cut (charter, principle 7). Before the premiere the public cut carries no recorded voice of a living person except her own HER-PUBLIC clip, and only if it passes one-key-three-guards (D-05, W-45); the maker's own hum and at most one plain factual fragment are the maker's own voice under W-35 and W-44. Relatives' voices need release 4d and arrive in the April version (D-06).
- Never record someone who believes they are off-tape [8].
- Do not treat tears or anger as material. Ask before using any emotional moment [32].
- Respect breaks and stops. Do not push anyone to tell their story [34][35]. If distress is serious, stop and point to appropriate help.
