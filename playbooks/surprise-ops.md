## Why this matters for our life journey

Most of the material for the film is not in her house. It is with the people who shared her life: her parents, her brothers and sisters, old friends, a former colleague, a neighbour. It is also in albums, letters, slides and tapes that other people keep. To collect it, someone has to ask those people. Under only-me that someone is the maker alone, through low-key asks that reveal nothing (D-03). Asking carries a risk that she hears about it.

So the secrecy is part of the craft, and the charter sets its limits. Principle 4 says real memories stay real: nothing is invented, and no words go into anyone's mouth. Principle 5 says dignity comes first, even in a surprise. She cannot approve the film in advance, so anything she might not want shown stays out of the public cut until she has had her say (D-04).

## Core principles

1. **Omit, but never lie.** You may leave things out. You may not say anything untrue, and you may not record her in secret. A research-ethics rule on deception draws the same line: withholding details is different from misleading people [13]. This is our reading of that rule, not a legal test.
2. **Make every pretext real.** When you ask someone for help with a cover task, you actually do it, and she gets the result. You label the photos and give her a copy. You write the family page. A pretext that is not real is a lie with a delay (our inference).
3. **Apply the no-lie floor.** If she sincerely asks whether something is being made about her, you do not deny it. You can say yes and keep the details until the date. The "If she asks" lines under the truthful pretext cards show the words.
4. **Ask her directly only in truthful windows.** There are three. You can label and date old photos with her. You can hold a real family-history conversation that also covers her parents and grandparents. You can hold an announced family-stories recording at a gathering, where everyone, her included, can see the recorder [9][10][11]. Everything else comes from other people and from objects.
5. **Get a spoken yes, at the time, wherever a person is recorded.** Before the premiere nobody is read the consent card (card 4a; D-03, W-49). The only planned recordings are the album-and-hands sessions, filmed on the hands and pages under a true sentence with a spoken "fine" on the file, and an announced Card E evening. Relatives' voices arrive in the April version, after release 4d (D-06, W-48). The rule for recording her is: no recording of her without her knowledge and a spoken yes. The only planned cases are an announced recorder at a family-stories evening (Card E) and a wide shot of the room at the reveal, if she agrees in the quiet word. Decision 10 settles this as (b), and the register records it as D-17 (O-17 asks the maker to confirm it by Sun 1 Nov); write the choice into card 4a.
6. **The reveal is her first consent point, and she can change anything after.** Give her the review options: delete, restrict, redact, add clarifications, correct mistakes, or keep material closed until a date she picks [8]. Her decisions override ours.
7. **Default OUT.** If we are unsure about a hard chapter, it stays out of the public cut (D-04). You can add something later. You cannot un-show it (our inference). Anything HOLD or PRIVATE is out of the public cut, full stop; the her-turn conversation (Sat 6 Mar 2027) is the only way back in.
8. **Honest and warm, not raw.** Describe a hard person by trait, not by one incident. Follow each honest sentence with something kind [56]. Hint rather than detail. Do not invent a sunny ending. Use no synthetic voices [63]. Use no AI animation of any real face, living or dead (D-16), and no animated or voiced dead in the public cut (W-46) [64].

**Philosophy note.** Philosophers disagree on whether a lie to hide a surprise is acceptable. A search summary of Derek Parfit's 2002 Tanner lecture reports that he treats such a lie as innocent when the person would consent if asked [76]. We could not open the lecture (404), so we do not rely on it. A Leiden doctoral thesis separates surprise, as a reaction to the unexpected, from whether it later turns out good or bad [75]. The practical test is plain: would she consent? Only the maker can answer that, from their own knowledge (Step 1, O-11); there is no insider under only-me.

### Step 4. Make devices and accounts safe (week 1, then monthly)

Run the Mum-test: open each shared device and account the way she would, and look for project words. The checklist is in Templates. The main fixes:

1. **Scanning phone.** Turn off the shared photo library, and set the camera to save to personal [18]. Turn off partner sharing [19]. Check the scanner app's backup settings before the first scan [21][22].
2. **Notifications.** Turn off message previews on shared screens. iOS previews can be set per app to "When Unlocked" or "Never" [33 S] (TODO(research): check on your own phone). Signal has name-only and no-content notification options, but its page was not opened (TODO(research)) [80 S].
3. **Family calendars.** Joining an Apple family or setting up a Google family creates a shared Family calendar automatically [31][32]. Keep project dates on paper, or in your project account only.
4. **Smart speakers.** When a child asked for recent orders, an Alexa+ device read a parent's recent Amazon orders aloud, including birthday gifts [29]. Turn off personal results on shared devices [30]. Turn off item titles in shopping notifications, and disable voice purchasing [29].
5. **Your Windows PC.** Use a separate Windows account for the project. Keep project files on an external drive, in a folder that OneDrive does not back up. Check that Manage backup is off for Desktop, Documents and Pictures [25]. Set "Hide content when notifications are on lock screen" per app, and use Do Not Disturb [24]. Windows Recall exists only on Copilot+ PCs and is opt-in per user [23].
6. **Encryption.** Windows Home lacks BitLocker Drive Encryption and BitLocker To Go [26], but has Device Encryption for the OS and fixed drives, turned on automatically on first sign-in with a Microsoft account and not for a local account [112]. Device Encryption does not cover USB drives [110], so the external project drive is the real gap, not the whole PC. A separate project user created as a local account does not get Device Encryption by itself. Device Encryption is theft protection, not family-privacy protection [110] (inference). For the external drive, the notes name VeraCrypt 1.26.29, which is free [93][95]. Decided (Fable advisor, round 2): a file container, not a whole-device volume. A standard user can mount it, an accidental format prompt is avoided (that prompt behaviour is (M), general knowledge, UNVERIFIED here), and the one visible large file is named with the code name. An administrator installs VeraCrypt and creates the container; the project account mounts and dismounts it [97][98]. A mounted volume is visible to every logged-on user, so dismount before you leave the desk or switch users (inference from [98]). The notes' rule: use VeraCrypt only for the external drive, never the Windows boot drive. Before real material goes in, test mount, copy, dismount and unplug once (inference).

Gate: the Mum-test finds no project word on any shared screen, and each fix is written down.

### Step 6. Gather stories from people, low-key (from Sun 15 Nov, to about week 12)

Nobody is interviewed on the record, and nobody is told a film is being made (D-03, W-49). The sessions below are low-key conversations that reveal nothing.

1. Ask only people who would enjoy the visit. A reluctant relative derails the project [12].
2. Make each ask an ordinary family request that reveals nothing, for example "Do you still have that video of Mum from the wedding? Please don't delete it" (`story.md`, Tier 1). Do not send a contributor brief: Versions A to C in Templates are void placeholders that fire only if TD-L2c (Sun 3 Jan) brought someone in. Do not ask older relatives to use a website they do not already use [37].
3. Hold album-and-hands sessions (W-49): the album open, a phone visibly filming the hands and the pages under a true sentence ("I'm filming the album while you tell me about it"), a spoken "fine" on the file, the face never in frame. The picture is Tier B now; the voice on the file is Tier C and waits for release 4d after the premiere. See one person at a time, in a quiet place. Start with the oldest relatives. See siblings separately before any group evening, because their versions differ [9]. If her parents are alive, see each one separately, at their table, with props. Frailty and hearing matter [40][66].
4. Ask for scenes, not summaries. Broad prompts like "tell me about your childhood" get names and dates. Props such as albums, letters and photos draw out scenes, and so do open follow-ups [9]. Keep three prompts ready in your head; do not send them ahead, because a card tells the person a project exists. StoryCorps groups its question lists by relationship, and says to use the ones you like [5].
5. Keep it to 20 to 30 minutes. The notes cite StoryCorps for six to eight questions in about 40 minutes [1]. The 20 to 30 minute shape is our inference for a favour.
6. The recording is your phone, visible, on the hands and pages. Relatives recording their own voice letters is April-version work, after release 4d (D-06, W-48), not part of this step. For that later work the Oral History Association says audio-only is often the least stressful option, and calls a phone-based recording "likely the most secure remote option" [6]; video calls are a last resort [7]; on an iPhone, Voice Memos can share a recording as one .m4a file [17].
7. Ask the "only you" question once, with an exit (Templates), only if it can be said as ordinary conversation that reveals nothing. Otherwise keep it for the April response recordings.
8. Log each person's ask status (`asked-ok` for what was said openly, otherwise `not-asked (surprise hold)`), the drawer each piece came from (top, middle or bottom, after the notes' use of Brooker's drawers), their country (see Step 1.2), and `told_by_candidate` (yes, maybe or no, plus the chapter): could this be the one story this person tells, in their voice, as the spine of a chapter? The withdrawal date stays blank until the release letters go out after the premiere (W-48).

Gate: each session has a log line and a spoken "fine" on the file; nothing untrue was said; nobody was read card 4a or told that a film is being made.

### Step 7. Borrow and digitise without tipping her off (from Sun 15 Nov, to about week 13)

1. Choose items she will not miss. Look yourself; do not ask her. Items in weekly use are photographed in place or left alone.
2. Pick a window when she is reliably out, with at least twice as long as you need. Note the window (time out, expected back) in the borrow log; there is nobody to tell under only-me.
3. Photograph as found: the shelf or drawer, each album's cover and spine, and every spread before any page moves. This set is your order record and your first scan.
4. Do not mark originals. Keep loose items in a labelled envelope outside the album, and keep a paper list.
5. Log each item: time out, where it went, time back, and who handled it.
6. Return the items in reverse order, and compare them with the as-found photographs. Return them the same day by default.
7. Magnetic album pages: the adhesive can weld to prints and tear them on removal [55]. The notes assume the same for sticky albums, with no source (TODO(research)). So photograph each spread in place first, and remove only prints that lift easily.
8. Originals of legal papers, passports and certificates never leave the house. Photograph them flat, in window light, with no flash.
9. Time: according to one vendor, a phone takes about 15 seconds per print once you are in rhythm, and a flatbed takes 30 to 60 seconds plus handling [52]. A sheet-fed scanner is another option: one Epson model is rated at about one photo per second at 300 dpi, takes loose prints only, and publishes no 600 dpi speed [54]. Our estimate for six albums of 20 spreads with four prints each is 3 to 5 hours of phone work (inference). Time your own first album (Exercise 2). Scan settings and file naming are in `playbooks/ai-imagery.md` and are not restated here (the scan standard and tool are fixed in W-27).
10. A library may offer self-service digitising. One example takes VHS, MiniDV, 8 mm film, photos and slides in three-hour blocks, but Video8, Hi8 and Digital8 were unavailable [49]. Your location is unknown, so treat it only as an example.

Gate: every item has a log line, and everything is back or logged as out.

### Step 9. Decide the hard chapters with one-key-three-guards (weeks 12 to 17; decided: D-04)

1. Guard 1: fill one decision sheet for each candidate chapter (Templates). Keep the facts, the sources, and how sure you are of each.
2. Guard 2: Fable reads the sheet and writes a flag. Fable flags and never passes G5, the dignity gate.
3. Ask the standard question, and answer it yourself in writing: "What would she choose, and what have you seen or heard her say or do that tells you?" This is substituted judgment, which is the medical-ethics standard for someone who cannot decide [61]. Ask this instead of "what would be best for her".
4. Guard 3: the maker decides OUT, PRIVATE, SHARED-LIGHT or HOLD, with a reason, then decides again at least 72 hours later (maker-proxy-72h-1 and maker-proxy-72h-2).
5. Apply the rules. Default OUT on any doubt. HOLD and PRIVATE are out of the public cut, full stop. An item goes in only if both decisions say yes after Fable's flag has been read. If the maker is part of the story on an item, Fable's flag is read twice and the item defaults OUT (inference).
6. Check that nobody is left worse off than when you met them. This principle comes from a search summary of a study of 45 documentary filmmakers [62].
7. Keep private showings private. In a 2023 Hong Kong documentary dispute, a subject said she never agreed to public release [82]. A private showing does not stand in for consent to a wider release.

Gate: each candidate chapter has two dated decisions 72 hours apart, Fable's flag, and a reason; both are final by Fri 5 to Sat 6 Feb (O-35).

### Step 11. The reveal (Saturday 27 February 2027)

1. Four hours before the start, set up: test the sound, turn captions on, turn on Do Not Disturb, hide notifications, and close mail and chat [24].
2. Guests arrive by a route she will not see. Phones are silent. Older relatives sit near the speakers with a clear sight line.
3. A distractor brings her by a plan that is true. If she asks who will be there, do not lie.
4. The quiet word happens 20 minutes before the film. Then she chooses her seat and her companion.
5. Open with a welcome of 30 to 60 seconds, said by the maker: "This is a gift for [NAME]. There is nothing to say and nothing to do." (Under only-me the old "from all of us" line is dropped.) Then play the public cut.
6. At the end, hold silence for 20 seconds. Bring the lights up slowly. Do not cue applause. Say: "No speech. There's food."
7. Give a pause of 10 to 15 minutes, with food, soft music and her companion beside her. No toasts, no questions about the film, and she may step out.
8. Toasts only if she signals she is ready. Nobody has agreed in advance to speak under only-me, so nobody is called on.
9. Later, in a quiet corner, hand her the invitation card and, if she wants it, the keepsake (Decision 15): "This is yours. No rush." The April version follows (D-06).
10. The next day, send a short text. Ask nothing about the film.

Gate: she is not asked to speak, perform gratitude, or say what she thought.

### If you slip (any time; D-19)

1. Stop. Do not cover it with a lie.
2. Within 10 minutes, assess what she saw or heard, and whether it points at the project or only at "something".
3. Write it down within the hour. There is no co-keeper to tell under only-me.
4. Tell the Fable advisor at the next session.
5. Choose one path: carry on, if the truthful line is enough; a partial reveal ("There's a gift in progress; here's what you can know"); convert to collaborative, where she picks the chapters she sees; or stop. If she objects, stop and ask her later; the maker is the proxy. This is the notes' slip protocol (T8), which draws on story.md Recipe 4.
6. Fix the cause: the setting, the device, or the person. Log what happened and what was fixed.
7. If a relative you asked a low-key question slipped, thank them without blame, and ask exactly what they said. If a child blurted, stay calm and use the partial-reveal lines.

## Learn-by-doing exercises

Exercises 1 and 4 come before the pilot tape (Step 8, Fri 30 October 2026). Exercises 2, 3, 5 and 6 come before the first real album-and-hands session (Step 6, from Sun 15 Nov 2026). Times are our estimates, not sourced figures.

### Exercise 1. Mum-test one device of your own (45 to 60 minutes)

- **Goal:** find every place a project word could show up on a shared screen, before there is anything to hide.
- **Steps:** (1) Pick a device that others share with you, such as your own tablet, a speaker you own, or a shared TV. (2) Use a dummy code name, such as "Lantern test". (3) Search for it on the device and in its apps. (4) Ask any speaker "what's on my calendar" and "show recent orders". (5) Write each finding and its fix on one sheet. (6) Apply one fix, then test again.
- **Time estimate:** 45 to 60 minutes.
- **What good looks like:** a sheet with a finding and a fix for each device, and a second test that finds nothing. Use your own or a practice device, not hers. The real Mum-test on her home devices happens in Step 4, by the maker alone.

### Exercise 2. Time one album spread in place (1 to 2 hours)

- **Goal:** measure your own rate. No source in the notes gives a rate for photographing album pages in place, so the estimate is inference.
- **Steps:** (1) Use an album you own. (2) Set the phone on a stand near a window, no flash, at the highest quality setting. (3) Time the set-up, the cover and spine, then each spread. (4) Note what slows you: glare, curled pages, sticky corners. (5) Multiply to estimate 20 spreads. (6) Repeat with a second album and compare.
- **Time estimate:** 1 to 2 hours.
- **What good looks like:** a table of times with an estimate for a whole album, labelled as your own measurement. No figure copied from a vendor page.

### Exercise 3. Run a practice album-and-hands session (about 1 hour with review)

- **Goal:** practise the true sentence, the set-up, the spoken "fine", and the "only you" question.
- **Steps:** (1) Ask a practice partner who knows it is a practice (a friend, not a relative of hers) about their own album. (2) Send no prompt card; keep three prompts in your head. (3) Set up: Do Not Disturb on, phone on a stand or a folded towel aimed at the album and the hands, 6 to 12 inches from the nearest mouth. Say a test line and play it back. (4) Say the true sentence ("I'm filming the album while you tell me about it") and get a spoken "fine" on the file. Card 4a is practised on paper only; nobody is read it before the premiere. (5) Run the 20 to 30 minute shape: slate, warm-up, two to four prompts, the "only you" question, and the closer. (6) Within 24 hours, copy the file to two places, rename it `YYYY-MM-DD_[relation]-[initials]_kit01`, and write a time-coded index.
- **Time estimate:** about 1 hour, including review.
- **What good looks like:** a clean file with a spoken "fine" at the start; at least one answer with a place, a sequence and a sensory detail; no steering; the face never in frame; and an exit offered in the closer.

### Exercise 4. Get lab dates in writing (1 hour of work, plus waiting)

- **Goal:** send the two emails this week (by Fri 23 October 2026); the Oct 30 pilot depends on at least one reply. Learn to get the dates, terms and costs in writing, and check our ship-by table against them.
- **Steps:** (1) From the project email, write to two labs, one fast and one slow. (2) Send each the six-question email in Templates (Lab email and log). It asks for weeks to the files and to the return of the originals, closure and cut-off dates, the mould and sticky-shed policy, return shipping and hold time, the total price with shipping and tax, and, for audio, whether the lab takes answering-machine microcassettes and at which speed it plays them. Anyone with film also sends the optional third email, the Pro8mm quote request. (3) Save each reply in the project email. (4) Paste the two replies into the Exercise 4 log table in Templates. (5) Recompute the ship-by date for each lab from the formula in Templates, on the return of the originals, and compare it with the ship-by table.
- **Time estimate:** 1 hour of work, plus waiting for replies.
- **What good looks like:** two written replies with dates, costs and terms, logged in the table, and a ship-by date that matches the table or differs for a stated reason.

### Exercise 5. Cold-start a reveal test (about 1 hour)

- **Goal:** prove the playback chain works from a powered-off laptop, with captions, sound and notifications set.
- **Steps:** (1) Use a three-minute test file, not her film. (2) Power the laptop off. (3) Connect the screen cable, turn on captions, turn on Do Not Disturb, and hide notifications on the lock screen [24]. (4) Start the file from cold, and time the first frame. (5) Check the sound at room level with a listener who is hard of hearing, if possible [66]. (6) Walk the fallback ladder: second laptop, USB stick, audio-only version.
- **Time estimate:** about 1 hour.
- **What good looks like:** the first frame appears without fiddling, captions are readable, the sound is clear to the listener, and the fallback order is written on a card.

### Exercise 6. Write a decision sheet for a made-up chapter (45 minutes)

- **Goal:** practise one-key-three-guards on an invented case, never on her life.
- **Steps:** (1) Invent a chapter about a fictional family, and label it FICTIONAL. (2) Fill every field of the decision sheet in Templates, including the four questions. (3) Write the maker's first decision, a flag as a fresh-context critic would write it (standing in for Fable), and the second decision 72 hours later. (4) Let the outcome follow the rules: default OUT on any doubt, HOLD and PRIVATE mean out of the public cut, and a disagreement between the two decisions means OUT. (5) Write the treatment as a trait, not an incident.
- **Time estimate:** 45 minutes.
- **What good looks like:** no blank field, a disagreement produces OUT, and the treatment line is honest and warm.

### Album-and-hands session kit (per relationship; replaces the interview kit, W-49)

- **Before:** ask in person, as an ordinary visit, and check they would enjoy it [12]. Send no card and no prompts ahead, because that tells them a project exists. Say how long: "about 20 to 30 minutes; we can stop whenever you like".
- **Set-up:** quiet, soft room; fridge and clock off; Do Not Disturb on; phone charged; phone on a stand or a folded towel aimed at the album and the hands (the face never in frame), 6 to 12 inches from the nearest mouth; test line played back.
- **Shape:** 0:00 to 0:03 slate, the true sentence ("I'm filming the album while you tell me about it") and a spoken "fine" on the file; 0:03 to 0:08 warm-up ("Where are you sitting?" or "Which album is this one?"); 0:08 to 0:22 two to four prompts, with follow-ups such as "Tell me more", "What did it smell like?", "Then what?", and silence; 0:22 to 0:27 the "only you" question, only if it can be asked as ordinary conversation; 0:27 to 0:30 the closer.
- **Closer:** "Is there anything I should have asked?" "Is there anything you have said that you would rather I left out?" Keep recording until they leave the room, then say goodbye off tape [notes T1.0].
- **Do not ask:** "What do you love most about her?", "Was she a good mother?", "Why did she...?", or anything about money, health, romance, conflict or faith unless they bring it up. If they do, log it as `drawer: middle/bottom` and stop.
- **Children:** no recorded children before the premiere. An identifiable minor without a yes on file may not be in the public cut (W-46), and a guardian cannot be asked without telling them. Revisit with releases 4d to 4g after the premiere.

### The "only you" line

- Use it only if it can be said as ordinary conversation that reveals nothing; otherwise keep it for the April response recordings (D-06).
- Ask once, plainly: "You were the only person in the room for at least one story about [NAME]. Which one is yours?"
- If they cannot think of one, ladder, do not repeat: "When were you alone with her?" Then "Where?" Then "What happened next?"
- Sort it: "Would she tell this one herself, to a room?" "Is there a version you would be happy for the whole family to hear?"
- Always give an exit: "If this one is better kept between us, say so, and I will keep it out."

### Cultural and religious questions (questions, not rules)

1. Is 27 February her birthday, or a milestone with its own name or rites in her tradition? Does her community celebrate birthdays at all?
2. Does the date clash with a fast, a mourning period, a festival or an observance (Ramadan, Lent, a death anniversary)? Would the meal, timing or alcohol need to change?
3. Who is the head of the family, and should that person hear first, as a courtesy?
4. Who speaks first, sits first or is served first? Is it right for a child (the maker) to host, or to present to elders?
5. Are there norms about being photographed or filmed, about who may see her image, or about dress on screen?
6. May images, voices or names of the dead be shown? Are there rituals or warnings expected first? Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people may be distressed by images, voices and names of the dead, which can conflict with cultural prohibitions [65].
7. Which language or languages will the room speak? Do you need subtitles in more than one?
8. Which music is acceptable in her tradition, and are there times when music is not played?
9. Are divorce, remarriage, illness, miscarriage or debt taboo, shameful, or a source of pride when told well?
10. What is the right way to thank, and is a public speech expected or unwelcome?

None of these is a sourced rule for any culture. Do not apply any of them as an assumption. Answer from your own knowledge first (O-10, O-11); ask an elder only through a low-key question that reveals nothing, or after TD-L2c.

## Pitfalls

- **Smart speakers read orders aloud.** A child asked, and a recent order was read out [29]. Fix: turn off personal results and item titles on shared devices, and disable voice purchasing.
- **Family calendars appear by themselves.** Joining a family plan creates a shared calendar that every member can see [31][32]. Fix: keep project dates off it.
- **Shared libraries share everything.** Every participant can add, edit and delete; contributed items move out of your library; the camera can share automatically [18]. Fix: keep the scanning phone out of any shared library.
- **Partner sharing shares photos as they are backed up.** The partner can save them to their own account, and they can appear in Memories and search [19]. A report says the "other apps" option has been off by default since November 2024 [20 S]. Fix: check the setting, or use a project-only account.
- **Scanner apps back up without asking.** PhotoScan uploads depend on backup settings [21]. Photomyne's membership backs up everything to its cloud, and shares albums of more than five photos as a link [22] (vendor's claims). Fix: check backup settings before the first scan.
- **OneDrive backs up Desktop, Documents and Pictures** once switched on [25]. Fix: keep project files on an external drive, outside those folders.
- **Windows Home encryption gaps.** Device Encryption covers the OS and fixed drives, not USB drives, and it is not turned on automatically for a local account [110][112]. Fix: VeraCrypt on the external drive (Step 4.6), and check the PC's status in msinfo32 under "Device Encryption Support" [110].
- **Dropbox emails the requester on every upload** [34]. Fix: use the project email as the only address.
- **Notification previews show names and text** on shared screens [33 S]. Fix: set previews off for any shared device.
- **Mail previews.** The USPS Informed Delivery service shows letter images and package status to the account holder [77 S]. Fix: check who holds the account, and send project mail to an address she does not see.
- **Mail-in absences get noticed.** A mail-in lab may keep an item away for 4 to 10 weeks (the notes' estimate from lab turnarounds). Fix: send only what she will not miss. Film may be recycled after 30 days unless storage is bought [46].
- **Group chats leak.** A message can land in the wrong thread, and previews show on shared screens [35][36]. Fix: no project talk in any chat she is in.
- **Too many helpers.** One successful surprise video needed at least ten helpers [38]. Fix: nobody else is told (only-me, D-03), and every ask is low-key and reveals nothing.
- **Hidden objects get found.** A ring hidden in the oven was found while cleaning [36]. Fix: choose hiding places by asking who cleans or looks there.
- **Proxies are wrong sometimes.** In one review, surrogates matched preferences about 68% of the time [60]. Fix: one-key-three-guards, default OUT, and her say at the her-turn conversation.
- **Synthetic voices and animated faces of the dead.** A 2021 documentary used an AI voice of a dead person without clear consent, and the director disclosed it only later [63]. Fix: real audio, objects and places. No synthetic voices, no AI animation of any real face (D-16), and no animated or voiced dead in the public cut (W-46) [64].
- **Anecdote used as best practice.** The first-hand material is blogs, columns and a Substack. Reddit was not reviewed (TODO(research)). Fix: label every claim as anecdote or practice, and test the method on one trusted person first.
- **Long or crowded reveals.** Hearing loss is common among older viewers [66]. Fix: keep the public cut at 9 or 12 minutes, burn in captions, and seat older guests near the speakers.
- **Technical failure on the day.** Fix: two cold starts, two USB sticks, and the fallback ladder.
- **Vendor bias.** Capture ranks its own service first [43]. Fix: get quotes from several labs, in writing.
- **Holiday overload.** Fix: listen at holidays, and extract nothing mid-meal (holiday rules).
- **Staging a reconciliation.** Fix: never. The maker, as proxy, decides on any estranged person's place under D-04, default OUT.
- **Unverified law.** The US reporters' guide is a baseline, and it says it is not legal advice [14]. Apple's call recording is consent-by-design, with a repeating notice, and is available only in some regions [15]. Third-party call recorders may record silently [15]; this project does not use them. UK: a participant's own recording is unlikely to be unlawful interception under IPA 2016 s.3/s.4 (the notes' reading, inference) [117][118]; Germany: StGB §201 makes an unauthorised recording of private speech an offence with no participant exception, so a spoken yes is mandatory [119]; EU: GDPR Art. 2(2)(c) household exemption is likely but untested [120]; other countries and non-US cross-border calls UNVERIFIED (TODO(research)). Fix: ask a lawyer where it matters.
