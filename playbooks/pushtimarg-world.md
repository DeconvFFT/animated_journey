## Why this matters for her film

- **Her world is fixed by the maker (D-31).** Bhavnagar, Saurashtra, Gujarat. Mother tongue Gujarati. A Pushtimarg (Vallabh sampradaya) Vaishnav family. The deities of her life: Lord Bal Krishna, Lord Shiva, Yamuna Maharani, Mahaprabhuji (Shri Vallabhacharya), Harsiddhi Maa, Maa Ambe, Ganesh Dada and Lakshmi Maa. She keeps all the Hindu festivals (read as the Gujarati Vaishnav set, row by row under `kept_by_her`, O-39, W-77). Her phone is Android (D-31; D-27). Tradition first, pan-Hindu second.
- **The premiere falls on a Pushtimarg utsav.** The sampradaya's own tippani lists Sat 27 Feb 2027 as *Shri Nathji ko Patotsav* [3]. A US haveli's handout calls Patotsav "a 'Mahotsav' in all Seven gruhs of pushtimarg" [47] (S). The handout and a Goswami's printed lectures say the day recalls Shrinathji's coming to Gusainji's house, the Satghara at Mathura, not the installation at Nathdwara [47] [64]. The sibling's Template D marks the day "minor" [R2]. This track disagrees, and that row must change (see Changelog). Whether the day clashes with her household's observance is Q1.
- **The sampradaya's own rules are stricter than generic "Indian" motifs.** Painted pichwai belongs to the warm months and never hangs in winter, at Annakut or at Janmashtami [32]. The niche in the sanctum is the svarupa's place [35] [32]. A svarupa is served as a living child [18] [1]. Each of these is a line the film must not cross (T2).
- **Vatsalya (parental feeling) must be honoured without presumption.** The sampradaya says seva is done "on behalf of Nand-Yashoda and Mahaprabhu Shri Vallabhacharya" [2]. The film honours her as a mother. It does not make her the parent of the svarupa, and it does not make her Yashoda (VR-1).
- **Her list of eight is the normal breadth of a Gujarati Pushtimarg household:** the Krishna seva core, the deities every Gujarati home keeps (Ganesh, Lakshmi, Ambe) and two that are hers in a particular way (Shiva, Harsiddhi). The film records her house; the sampradaya's published view of other deities is in E33 and is never commentary.
- **The eight darshans give a kitchen clock, not a life clock** (T3 header; W-34). They can set the hour of light in a kitchen. They may never name a chapter, a card, an age or a stage of life.
- **Her hands are the film's grammar** (W-33). The sampradaya defines padasevana, the seva of acts, as "to clean the place of Krshna-worship, wash His dresses, utensils, paintings, making His ornaments, preparing flower necklace, food etc." [1]. Those acts open chapters. Finished seva articles never do (W-32).

## Core principles

1. **The svarupa is never a character.** No Shrinathji, Bal Krishna (the *lalan*, or baby, form [7]), Thakorji, Mahaprabhuji or Yamunaji figure moves, blinks, speaks, walks, morphs or is lip-synced, by hand or by AI (PR1). The eyes come last: "opening the eyes is the pran pratishtha of the picture" [35]. Never draw or open a svarupa's eyes (PR3).
2. **Acts, not bhava.** The inward mood is "an internal and unobservable personal factor" [1]. The film shows hands doing seva acts and never a rapt face (VR-2).
3. **A servant, not Yashoda.** No card names her Yashoda, Maiya or Lalan. Her love is called by her family's own word, and the maker chooses that word (VR-1; Q5).
4. **Pichwai is a frame, never a niche.** Use the border (cows and peacocks only), the horizontal bands and the night sky. The centre holds an object of hers or text, never a face and never an opening (T1 composition rules; PR5) [35] [32] [8].
5. **Season-correct idiom.** Painted pichwai appears only in the Sharad Purnima and Gopashtami chapters. Never in winter. The premiere's Vasant season is white malmal and cream, with gulal only as a puff over hands and table (W-72; T10; PR23) [32] [8].
6. **The clock is the kitchen's, not the life's.** A darshan hour sets the light in a kitchen. A darshan name is never a chapter, a card or a stage (T3 header; PR16).
7. **Offered first, and Thakorji's seva kept apart.** Nothing is eaten before the offering is made and removed. No onion or garlic in the kitchen set, as her kitchen keeps it (Q13; until Q13 is answered this is a flag, not a fail). Thakorji's seva articles never share a thali, lamp or cloth with another deity's puja; the other deities are shown as her house keeps them (Q14) (VR-3; PR12; PR13; PR18).
8. **No hagiography, and unknowns stay out.** No Mughal or Aurangzeb journey, no "saved from Muslim rulers" line, and no Mughal-court shringar shape on screen (PR8; PR22; D-28). Phool mandali, hatdi and any Dhanurmas practice stay out until the maker says her family keeps them (PR25; Q12).

10. **Log any overrule.** The maker may overrule one line at a time. Record it in the maker-overrules log (T1: date, entry, one line of her reason). Nothing else relaxes an exclusion.

## Learn-by-doing exercises

### Exercise 1. Season map

- **Goal.** Know which pichwai register each window allows, before any frame is drawn.
- **Steps.** Take the seven windows in T10 and put their dates from T5 beside them. Write the one register each window allows. Check that no painted pichwai falls in the Diwali or winter rows. T10 lists painted pichwai only for Sharad Purnima and Gopashtami.
- **Time.** 45 minutes.
- **What good looks like.** Each window has one register. The Diwali and Annakut row says "no painted pichwai". The winter row says "none". No card takes its pichwai from a window it is not in.

### Exercise 2. The kitchen clock

- **Goal.** Build the kitchen-hour list for a three-module outline, and test the monotony rule.
- **Steps.** Take the T3b hours: Mangla 05:30, Shringar 07:45, Gwal 09:20, Rajbhog 11:45, Utthapan 15:45, Bhog 16:45, Sandhya about 30 minutes around the local sunset, and Shayan 19:30. Assign a kitchen hour to each module. Check that the hours are not in darshan order across modules. Check that the last render is not a Shayan hour unless the maker chose it. Check that no card carries a darshan name.
- **Time.** 60 minutes.
- **What good looks like.** The hours across the modules are not in darshan order. The last render is not a Shayan hour unless the maker chose it. No hour is labelled as a life stage in any card.

### Exercise 3. The border on a phone

- **Goal.** Prove that a cow border reads on a phone, before the palette or the frame is locked.
- **Steps.** Draw one cow border at 1920 by 1080. Use a silhouette at least 48 px tall, a 4 px meaning-bearing line, 3 px decoration and 6 px dots (T1 phone block). Use Inkscape (the fallback named in W-38) or a hand drawing scanned at the same size. Show the frame full-screen landscape on an Android phone, then at upright chat scale (about 0.56). Count the legs, horns and tail.
- **Time.** 90 minutes.
- **What good looks like.** Legs, horns and tail each read as a feature. The repeat unit is at least 6 px, and no dense repeat "goes to mush". The border frames the centre and never outweighs it: the centre stays larger than the borders together.

### Exercise 4. Contrast check on the palette roles

- **Goal.** Confirm which colour pairs may carry a line.
- **Steps.** Take the eight palette roles in T1. Work out the contrast ratio for each pair you plan to use as a line or border stroke. A line that carries meaning needs 3:1 or more against its ground. Compare your results with the OK and FAIL lists in T1. Keep failing pairs as areas only.
- **Time.** 30 minutes, with a calculator or a contrast checker.
- **What good looks like.** Your results match the researcher's arithmetic (J) for the listed pairs. Any pair you add is marked OK or area-only. The final palette is re-sampled from her own prints (W-38 decision 1; O-24 at G1), not taken from the museum scan (F3 of the research note).

### Exercise 5. Making or tending

- **Goal.** Sort the home-seva objects so that each hands opener is a making or a tending, never an article.
- **Steps.** Sort T4 rows H1 to H12 into "makes" and "tends". For each, write what her hands actually do, but only where the maker has answered (Q8; O-06). Mark the decision-2 candidate as the making, not the article. Confirm that every row reads `object_clock_eligible: no`.
- **Time.** 40 minutes.
- **What good looks like.** Each making names a line or a stitch, not an object. Rows the maker has not confirmed stay TODO_maker and are not used.

### Exercise 6. The verse check (only with a printed copy already in the house)

- **Goal.** Check T9 verse 1 against a printed edition, or leave it out.
- **Steps.** Only if a printed Yamunashtakam is already in the house, in its ordinary place, and the maker has seen it in daily life: compare the three script lines of verse 1 with that page. Never ask her about it. If there is no printed copy, mark verse 1 TODO(research) and go no further.
- **Time.** 30 minutes.
- **What good looks like.** The IAST and Gujarati lines match the print letter for letter. Otherwise the mismatch is logged and the card does not go ahead. No card carries verse 1 without this check (PR21).

**Idiom rules (decision 11).**

- **Allowed parts of a pichwai-manner frame:** the gai (cow) and mor (peacock) borders; a lotus-water band; a cow band; a night sky with a dot field and a plain moon disc; banana leaf; kadamba; an episode grid of her own kept-by rooms only [35] [8].
- **Forbidden parts:** a central niche or cut-out; any svarupa figure; flanking gopis; a raas ring with a person at the centre; the *chaubis svarup* border (the 24 forms of Vishnu); the Krishna leela border; a jewelled hanging; embossed gold; the Deccani golden pichvai; any Mughal arch, jaali, dome or kulhe silhouette (PR8; E27 to E29).
- **Composition:** never more than two borders, and the centre stays larger than the borders together. The centre holds an object of hers or text. No figure group is arranged around a central figure. One sky per card, either night indigo or white, never both.
- **Motion:** the border never moves, and nor do the bands. Nothing with a face moves; that includes cow friezes sliding. Allowed: a camera push or pull up to 8% and a pan up to 5% of width over a still field (style-bible decision 5); leaf sway; a water ripple on a band; a lamp flame on an object; falling petals or a gulal puff over hands and table, never over a deity or a person's face; a cloth changed by hands as shot content, never as a transition. No dissolve, wipe, burn or shatter on any pichwai-manner card. Enter and leave by cut or by a held, lamp-lit still.
- **Gold and silver:** flat colour, no gradient, glow, flare or filter. Pearl dots only as drawn dots of 6 px or more.
- **Palette roles (design values, not sampled):** indigo night #1F2A55; vermilion #C23B2A; leaf green #5E7F50; saffron #E08A2B; chalk cream #EDE3CE; flat gold #C9A23C; flat silver #C4C8CC; lampblack #1B1A18. **The cold open and the Mangla reading (W-36, W-76).** The cold open's first act of her morning is a sound with energy above about 400 Hz and a visible first light within about 4 s, at the 05:30 hour (one flame against blue-black kitchen glass), with no rhythm instrument (the Mangla service has none: a held object sound, the hum's open vowel, or nothing), no card and no name. To a Vaishnav in the room it is Mangla; to everyone else it is a kitchen before dawn. No bell and no conch are needed for it to land. If the home keeps Yamunajal in a jhari (Q7, O-77), water poured into a stand-in brass vessel is the first candidate; the bell, if the morning bell is hers (O-46), is a household hand-bell outside the mandir or the maker's own; the conch is the maker's own or nothing (W-70).

**Monotony rule (checkable from `chapters.csv`).** The kitchen hours across modules are not sorted in darshan order, and the film's last kitchen render is not a Shayan hour unless the maker chooses it. A one-hour render may use any hour, and its name is never on screen.

### T8. The Pushtimarg tilak, as a drawn reference

**What the sources say.** The Vallabha Sampradaya's mark is a "double vertical red line which is rounded at the base ... This 'U' shape represents Krishna's lotus feet ... materials ... derived from saffron plants" (Wikipedia, citing Narayanan in Brill's Encyclopedia of Hinduism Online) [24]. A lay blog of 2011 reads the same form as joined lines, and assigns a single red line to the Rudra/Vishnuswami line [26]. Both are weak. Two sampradaya prints were read in the follow-up: the Sri Vallabhacarya Trust's English Manual of 2002 names the signs of a Vaishnava as "Tilaka and Kanthi ('U' shape vermilion mark and Tulasi garland)" [65], which supports the U and the vermilion; and a Goswami's Gujarati lectures say the devotee's tilak is made from prasadi kumkum and that the chhapa stamps (shankha, chakra, gada, padma) have lapsed in Pushtimarg [64]. Neither says "double line" or "rounded base", so those details still rest on [24] and [26]. The general Vaishnava mark, in the sibling and in Lochtefeld, is "two or three vertical lines ... intercepted in the middle by an elongated U" [25] [R1 F5]. That is a different mark, the pan-Vaishnava urdhva pundra, which in other lineages is usually white or yellow (J). A sampradaya page confirms only that a tilak is applied daily, "twelve Tilaks, six impressions" [1], and that Gokulanatha "defended the sect's right to wear tilaks and tulsi mala" [18].

**Drawn reference (J, from the text above; the maker checks it against a real forehead or picture).**

```
    |   |      two vertical strokes, red (kumkum or saffron-derived), from the hairline
    |   |      to just above the bridge of the nose
    |   |
     \_/       joined at the base by a round curve: a "U" with NO central stroke
```

Proportions are the researcher's own: stroke 4 px at 1080p (the floor); centre gap about three stroke widths; height about three times the width. Minimum drawn size 28 by 20 px. Below that, do not draw it: a 4 px stroke is lost on a small face and the mark reads as a smudge. By the sources read: not white, not a leaf, not a central stripe, not three horizontal bands. The women's forehead mark: no Pushtimarg print or page read describes one (Q6). If the maker's family shows a different form, hers wins and this table is amended.

## Reverence and what we leave out

### Reverence norms

- **Reproduction.** The sampradaya itself reproduces Shrinathji: pilgrim paintings of the haveli [12]; a gallery of devotees' home svarupas "kindly sent in by" their owners [9]; painters selling "Shrinathji ka shringar" [34]. No sampradaya page found forbids reproduction (F6). But a picture that a Goswami has pusht is in seva [2] Seva Q35, and a chitraji is dressed like any svarupa [7]. A print can be décor in one house and a living child in another. The film cannot tell which by looking. So the rule is not "do not mistreat it"; it is "do not put one on screen unless the maker knows and her answer allows" (PR2; PR4), which under D-02 means the April version at the earliest (W-61, O-64).
- **Animation.** No sampradaya or Nathdwara statement on animating Shrinathji was found. The painters' practice speaks: "The aankh is made last ... opening the eyes is the pran pratishtha of the picture ... When the eyes open, Shrinathji is present in it" [35]. A blink, a gaze that follows, or a morphing face is that act done by software. Lean: never (PR1; PR3). The sibling's precedent: a January 2026 police complaint over an AI deity video, as the research note cites it [R1 F5].
- **Shringar is fixed.** "The shringar tradition of Shrinathji is fixed for all three hundred and sixty five days ... The mistakes come from those who only copy pictures of pictures" [35]. So "without the season's shringar" is not only irreverent but wrong. An agent cannot draw him correctly; only a hereditary painter who goes to the temple that day can (PR2).
- **Photographs inside the temple.** At Nathdwara, "Mobile phones, electronic cameras, smart watches, leather belts/wallets, and luggage are strictly prohibited inside the temple premises" (independent portal [14]); Wikipedia's uncited sentence bars phones and shoes [23]. The Board's own notice was not readable (TODO(research)). Darshan photographs carry no copyright claim that any page read states; treat any darshan photograph as the temple's or the photographer's, which means out.
- **Discarded seva items.** Havelis "discard damaged items of seva", which is why old painted pichois reach the art market [8]. A pichoi is a backdrop, not a svarupa. Rule (J; PR10): no deity image or seva article is ever shown torn, burned, binned or on packaging.
- **The home Thakorji's privacy.** No norm found against photographing a home svarupa. The practice on record is consent (owners sending photographs to a gallery [9]) and quiet: seva is kept up "without disturbing his Deity, his family and his neighbor" [1]. The doors of the home mandir "represent the eye lids of Shri Swaminiji (Radhaji), who will grace us to open the eyes" [16] Q81, which is why they are shown closed, once, in the shrine moment (W-61) and are never the locked-room device (W-37). Seva pauses for a birth, a death and other times [16] Q98. Photographing her Thakorji without her knowing is out (PR4; D-02; D-17).
- **Mahaprabhuji.** Static and high-framed (PR19). His picture sits "separately from a Swaroop of Shri Thakorji and not along with Shri Thakorji while performing seva" [2] Seva Q35. A baithak is shown as a place, not with his image. His footwear and footprints are seva objects, not footwear (PR11).
- **Yamunaji.** Static (PR20). "Shree Yamunaji is always seen in clothes and ornaments similar to Shreenathji" [15]. Yamunajal is never poured away on screen.

## Pitfalls

1. **The premiere is a Patotsav.** Do not let the sibling's "minor" stand. The day's meaning is not the film's to borrow (Q1) [3] [47] [R2]. The sampradaya's own accounts say it recalls Shrinathji's coming to Gusainji's house, the Satghara at Mathura, not the installation at Nathdwara [47] [64]. HinduPad's digest gives Krishna Saptami of Faagan in the Rajasthani calendar and of Magh in the Gujarati calendar [63] (D).
2. **The printed fortnight words are wrong from 24 Dec.** The tippani keeps "sud first" in Gujarati order but has switched to North Indian month names, so the words are inverted against the moon. 11 Feb prints "Magha vad 5" for Vasant Panchami, while the festival page says "Mahasud Panchmi" [3] [4]. 6 Feb (new moon, Mauni Amavasya per Drik) prints "Magha sud 15" [3]. Use the dates and festival names; ignore the printed fortnight words; take the tithi from the saurashtra-world table.
3. **The utsav dates disagree in three places.** Dhanteras: Sat 7 Nov (tippani) against Fri 6 Nov (Drik). Annakut: Mon 9 Nov (tippani, "Aso vad 15") against Tue 10 Nov (Drik, Delhi), while the same site's festival page says "Kartak Sud Ekam". Prabodhini: the tippani gives Sat 21 Nov only; Drik gives Fri 20 (Devutthana) and Sat 21 (Gauna) [45]. No sampradaya page states a rule for an Ekadashi that spans two civil days.
