## Why this matters for her film

- **Her world sets the look, the sound, the script and the calendar.** She is from Bhavnagar (Saurashtra, Gujarat), her mother tongue is Gujarati, her family is Pushtimarg (Vallabh sampradaya) Vaishnav, and she keeps all the Hindu festivals. The deities of her life are Lord Bal Krishna, Lord Shiva, Yamuna Maharani, Mahaprabhuji (Shri Vallabhacharya), Harsiddhi Maa, Maa Ambe, Ganesh Dada and Lakshmi Maa. Her phone is Android (D-31, the maker's words, 2026-10-19). Her tradition comes first and pan-Hindu second. A Hindu motif earns its place by being hers (CHARTER, Principle 2).
- **Her district's spine is Shiva, the sea and Krishna's rasa.** Takhteshwar, Nishkalank Mahadev and Gopnath Mahadev are three Shaiva settings in Bhavnagar district. At Gopnath, two pages confirm the Narsinh Mehta story: he fasted by a Shiva lingam for seven days, and Shiva "took him to Vrindavan" to watch Krishna's Rasaleela [27][26]. These are candidates, not chapters, until the maker says which places are hers (O-55).
- **Gujarati is on screen.** Cards, captions and titles carry Gujarati script. The face and the shaping engine decide whether a conjunct fuses or breaks, and whether a date in digits draws at all (Template E). Several faces that look right in a catalogue fail in the test container.
- **The calendar sets the schedule.** The whole Diwali week (Thu 5 to Sat 14 Nov) ends before covert collection starts on Sun 15 Nov (W-10, D-13). Uttarayan (Thu 14 and Fri 15 Jan) sits on the W-10 hard deadline. The premiere Saturday falls inside a Vinchudo period (Sat 27 Feb 05:14 to Mon 1 Mar 16:27) [10]. Sources disagree on several dates, so her house decides (O-56, O-57).
- **It touches the plan's fixed points.** The object clock (W-32), the hands grammar (W-33), the module-break card (W-04), the style bible's hand-made material (W-38), the gathering calendar (W-10, W-49), the premiere week (W-52) and the April releases (W-48). The exact changes to each file and section are in Changelog.

## Core principles

1. **Origin and community decide, not the look.** A motif or craft is in only if its origin is Hindu in maker and subject and the community that made it is named. Shared or ambiguous items are out by default, with one question to the maker (D-28; the rule the researcher applies from review 05, Part 0).
2. **Hers beats generic.** Her deity, her temple, her festival and her region come first. Stock "Gujarat" fails the test: the Rann's white salt flat, the Statue of Unity, garba-night crowds, a thali top-shot, a Gir lion. If a shot would work for another Gujarati family, it is out (W-38; T11 in Template I).
3. **The object clock is domestic and cannot be swapped.** Only home objects she handles, that travel with her or cannot be replaced by use, qualify. A seva article is never the object clock and never a prop: the garbo, the home mandir's cloth, a Thakorji article, a Tulsi plant in worship, the Hindola (W-32; review 0.5).
4. **Somnath, Dwarka and Ayodhya appear only as sea, stone and lamp.** No date of reconstruction, no history card, no map, no crowd, no signage (the three-places rule, Reverence section; `DECISIONS.md` W-71). The rule names Ayodhya (the place and the anniversary line), not the deity: Rama is Hindu and welcome under D-28; he is not on her list of eight (D-31), so nothing of Rama is a default; hers if she says it (O-63).
5. **Test every Gujarati face in the engine the film uses.** Pass the phone size test at 35 cm, the Gujarati digits test and the conjunct test with the language tag on and off. Mukta Vaani is not used for Gujarati text, Hind Vadodara carries Latin digits only, and Kumar One is excluded as shipped (Template E).
6. **Her house's panchang beats any website.** Drik's Bhavnagar pages are the best source found, but they disagree with two Gujarati-language pages and with Drik's own Delhi reading on the Diwali week. A calendar row is marked kept_by_her only when the maker answers (Template C; O-56).
7. **Words follow her register, and the plainest word is welcome.** A card calls her whatever her children call her (O-49, O-61). મા has "mother" as its first sense and the goddess suffix as a further one [21]. The only rule is a layout rule: a word for her never shares a frame with a goddess's name or epithet. Greetings whose literal content is an Islamic-origin blessing are flagged (Template F; the D-28 scope note).
8. **Keep the phone in view.** Build every caption and card for a 6.1-inch phone held landscape: FontSize 28 for the phone file, FontSize 23 for the venue file. Test it at 35 cm against the 0.35-degree target and the 0.30 floor (Template E; D-27; mobile-delivery).

## How to use it, step by step

1. **Read the rules first.** CHARTER Principles 2 and 4; DECISIONS D-28, D-31, W-32, W-33 and W-38. Then read the Reverence section. Nothing from the web enters a shot before it passes the origin gate.
2. **Places (Template A, places table).** For each candidate place, fill a row: the deity and story in two lines, the hour and look, any festival in the window, the photography position (UNVERIFIED unless read), the licence of any open image, the flag and the hers_if test. Only a yes on hers_if moves a place from candidate to chapter (O-55).
3. **Political room (Reverence).** Any shot or card that touches Somnath, Dwarka or Ayodhya carries `political_room: three-places`. A card with any word goes to the maker's one-key-three-guards decision sheet (D-04). Fable flags it and never passes G5.
4. **Crafts (Template D).** Run the origin gate before scoring. Where her own hands made a craft, it is the primary tradition whatever its score (the note's collapse rule), which feeds style-bible decisions 2 and 11 (W-38).
5. **Home objects (Template B).** Score each candidate on daily hand, object-clock candidacy, opening hand and generic risk. A generic risk of "high" fails unless the shot is of her own thing.
6. **Dates (Template C).** Look up every date in the calendar. Before any ask, check the gathering rules: no asks in the Diwali week, no session before Sun 15 Nov, and no session on a Vinchudo evening without the maker's say (D-13, D-17, W-10).
7. **Gujarati on screen (Template E).** Choose the face from the roles table, run the Template E tests, and outline any title set in Shrikhand or Farsan. A live browser page is never set in Mukta Vaani.
8. **Words (Template F).** Pick from the lexicon and the maker's answers to O-49 and O-61. Set the pronoun the maker chooses. Use the word her children use for her, and keep it out of any frame that holds a goddess's name or epithet.
9. **Log the flags in the brief.** Record `tradition_origin` (Hindu-Gujarati, Pushtimarg, shared-flagged, jain-flagged, adivasi-flagged, political-room), `political_room` and `place_id` (P01 to P10, F1 to F3). The note proposes these columns for the film-story beat sheet and the rights log; they are not yet in those templates.
10. **Gaps stay gaps.** Any point the sources leave open goes into the brief as `TODO(research): ...`, never as a guess.
11. **Before the settings card (Sun 1 Nov).** Run the Gujarati fixture on the PC, in Chromium and on the project Android (Exercise 3), with a native Gujarati reader (O-62). Which face her own handset draws is checked only after the reveal (W-75).

### Exercise 6: Test the open chapter card

- **Goal.** Choose one of the three "To be told by her" wordings that reads in one face at phone size.

### Template G: the Gujarati captions and the card rule

- **Captions.** 42 characters a line, two lines, at most 20 characters a second (slower for a room over 70), inside the 90% box, `FontSize=23` for the venue (W-18) and 28 for the phone file (the Gujarati phone measurement in Template E); Gujarati is sized by the height of KA, not by `FontSize` alone. - **Card rule.** A card about any of the three political-room places names the place and nothing else, or says nothing. It carries no year, no verb of conquest or recovery, no number of years, no "ancient", no "rebuilt". If the card needs a sentence, the place is dropped.
- **Language.** Card language Gujarati, with the room's language in the caption track (W-18, W-57). Noto Sans Gujarati for captions, Rasa or Baloo Bhai 2 for cards, Shrikhand and Farsan only as outlined title art.

### Template H: the saathiyo (T12 of the note; the maker's call, O-66, which absorbs O-53)

Both versions are prepared and nothing is decided. The swastika is "commonly used before entrances or on doorways of homes or temples" and has "a particular association with Diwali" [54]. ## Reverence and what we leave out

**The rule.** Origin and community decide, not the look of a thing (D-28; the rule the researcher applies from review 05, Part 0). Shared or ambiguous items are out by default, with one question to the maker. "Hers beats generic": a flagged item on her own sari or in her own photograph is hers, decided line by line. The rule governs motifs and themes, not the religion of the person who made a thing, nor the word on a card, except where a card's literal content is an Islamic-origin blessing (Template F).

**The three-places rule (W-71, written once; it sets the `political_room` field).** *Somnath, Dwarka and anything of Ayodhya appear only as the sea, the temple's own stone and a lamp in her hands: no date of reconstruction, no history card, no map, no crowd, no signage, no flag except the temple's own cloth in motion, and never in a shot that needs a caption to explain it.* "Her devotion, not a position" lives in the shot brief's `motif_check` and the maker's decision sheet. It is never on screen. The Commons category for Somnath mixes devotional photographs with political-event files (a Prime Minister at a Trust meeting, a commemoration chanting): choose by file, never by category [68].

**Why the rule is strict.** Somnath is inside a year-long national commemoration, reported by a secondary source as running "from January 11, 2026 to January 11, 2027" [34]. The broadcaster covers only the opening days and calls them "not a remembrance of destruction" but "a tribute to resilience, faith and civilisational self-respect" [34]. The 1990 Ram Rath Yatra began at Somnath on 25 September 1990 [35]. Historians read the 1026 raid and its memory differently (Thapar, digest only) [36]. Dwarka's Bet Dwarka demolition drive of January and February 2025 is described in opposite ways by the sides; none of the coverage was opened [37].

## Pitfalls

**Calendar**

- Calendars disagree on the Diwali week (Annakut, Bestu Varas, Bhai Beej). Do not pick one; use the house's answer (Template C; O-56).
- The Samvat number is not one number. "Vikram Samvat 2083" begins on 19 March 2026 in the Chaitra reckoning (a digest of Sandesh [69]). The Gujarati year changes at Bestu Varas: Drik's day page reads Gujarati Samvat 2082 Pingala on Mon 9 Nov and 2083 Kalayukta on Tue 10 Nov [4]; the Shubh Panchang page prints "ગુજરાતી સંવત: ૨૦૮૩" on Tue 10 Nov [6]. Do not print a Samvat number on a card without the house's answer.
- No official 2027 Gujarat holiday list exists yet. The General Administration Department's site returned 503 [17], again on 2026-10-19. The third-party lists say the gazette is "expected in late 2026"; the 2026 list's tracker date is 27 Dec 2025, so re-read the Uttarayan row on Mon 28 Dec 2026 [77]. Uttarayan is stated as a convention with its sources, not as a notified date.
- Doodh-pauva on Sharad Poonam (Sun 25 Oct) is attested in one Gujarati-language source (Gujarati Wikipedia: rice flakes with milk and sugar, uncited, not tied to Bhavnagar) [70]; kheer in moonlight is the sourced dish [15]. Hers if she says it.
- Drik's pages state no reuse licence and claim copyright in "All Images and data"; its app terms forbid copying the app [72]. Restate each date as a fact with its source and read date; never copy a page, table or image. Whether restating needs permission is a TD-8 consult question (UNVERIFIED).
- Chhath, Karva Chauth and Ahoi Ashtami appear on pan-Hindu lists. They are "probably not kept: ask".

**Fonts and digits**

- Hind Vadodara has no Gujarati digits. A date in digits set in it, such as ૨૭ ફેબ્રુઆરી ૨૦૨૭, falls to FreeSerif (M).
- The plain name "Anek Gujarati" does not resolve in libass; only "Anek Gujarati Medium" does (M).
- Kumar One collapses in libass because its TrueType hinting program fails in FreeType (M). An unhinted copy renders. Whether the PC's FreeType repeats the fault is UNVERIFIED.
