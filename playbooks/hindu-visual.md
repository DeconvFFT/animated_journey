## Why this matters for our film

The film is about her, so its look has to be hers. The maker's rule (D-28) is that "Indian" always means Hindu: Sanatan Dharma and its sanskriti, with no Mughal, Islamic or Christian motifs. Her world (D-31) now chooses the style, music, script and calendar, her tradition first and pan-Hindu second. The playful, crafted, animated feel the maker wants comes from this Hindu design world (D-28), and D-29 retired the earlier game-world rule (D-23). The film must play beautifully on a phone as well as on a venue screen (D-27).

Three findings change how we plan:

1. **The phone is the harder screen.** A thin line that is clear on the master can lose its meaning after a chat app recompresses it. In our synthetic test a 2 px black line placed at a half-pixel kept 0.48 of its contrast through the smaller chat proxy, and a 4 px line kept 0.83 (M, with proxies that are not WhatsApp's own encoder). The design floor is therefore 4 px for any line that carries meaning [104][105].
2. **The respect problem is live.** The Shree Jagannath Temple Administration filed a police complaint in January 2026 over AI-generated videos of its deity, one of them showing milk poured "using a crane" [83]; the temple's own description of the case as "the first known instance" is relayed by [84] alone. The Hindu American Foundation says AI images "are not inherently sacred" [79]. So the film shows no generated deity figure. The default is P1, symbols and seva objects that are not a svarupa, until she answers.
3. **Rights are per record.** A museum's public-domain or CC0 label clears that record, not the subject. A painting commissioned for a film needs a signed written licence (s.19 of the Copyright Act) that names the film use, the territory and the term [25].

The traditions fit her differently. The floor arts (kolam, rangoli, alpana and the Gujarat sathiyo, a lead to verify as the Gujarati word for the threshold swastika [123]) are native line-drawing and may be her own hands' work [45][46][47]. The Gujarat and Pushtimarg rows of the research tables are live now (D-31). For her the floor-art line (sathiyo and rangoli) is the primary idiom, and the pichwai manner is the frame register, in season and never the niche (Template H1 collapse rule, Decision 1). A Nathdwara pichwai is the most personal painting for a Pushtimarg household. Its origin flag is resolved to an OK-note (a Deccan origin; the page read names no Mughal or Rajput influence [38]; the Nathdwara school is a subset of the Mewar school ([114]; Wikipedia, Nathdwara painting [137]), the one Rajput idiom E10 does not flag [37]; temple art of her own sampradaya). In the temple Shrinathji is never painted into the pichwai (the cloth is cut where he stands), so what P1 drops is the figure, not the cloth: the pichwai-manner border, bands and night sky, with an object of hers or text at the centre, stay usable, never the niche and never behind a face (`research/pushtimarg-world.md` F3, PR5). Two cautions remain: a painted svarupa needs P4 by a hereditary painter who paints the day's shringar, and the manner is dense, so it sits at the phone floor.

## Core principles

1. **Hers first, pan-Hindu second.** A motif earns its place by being on her shelf, on her floor, in her festival or in her hands (D-28, D-31). A famous motif that is not hers is left out.
2. **Symbols before figures.** A lamp, kalash, lotus, conch, Om, linga, padukas, yantra or tulsi, and the seva objects that are not a svarupa (the jhari in its red cloth, the thali, the folded cloth, the butter-pot, the mishri bowl), can carry a home shrine without a deity figure (P1; J, the research's safe route). A figure needs the maker's yes as her proxy (O-42; O-52 is its Pushtimarg form) and a route that is not generative; for a svarupa, P2 and P4 only as a hereditary painter's work in the day's shringar (`research/pushtimarg-world.md` PR2).
3. **Design for the phone's floor.** Meaning-bearing lines at 4 px or more; decoration at 3 px or more and expendable; dots 6 px; puppet perforations 4 px; hatching with a 3 px stroke at 9 px pitch; no 1 px anything (M).
4. **Count, never trust.** Arms, attributes and hand order are counted against the sheet and her own image, by an agent and then by the maker. A deity asset is never mirrored, because a mirror reverses the hand order and can change the form [61].
5. **Respect is a shot rule.** Twenty checkable rules (Template H4) apply to every deity shot. Default OUT on any doubt (D-04).
6. **Shared origin is out by default.** A motif whose origin is shared or ambiguous is flagged and left out. The maker overrules one line at a time, in a log (D-28).
7. **Every image has a rights row.** "Public domain", "CC0" and "non-commercial" are checked per record. Non-commercial and share-alike terms are "ask", not "yes", for a public screening [14][15][10][17].
8. **Real hands, no generated gods.** A deity image comes only from a hand-drawn symbol checked against the sheet, a logged hand-traced public-domain plate, a commissioned painting under written licence, or, in the April version only and only if O-52 says yes and she knows, her own picture. From her shelf the maker photographs only seva objects that are not a svarupa (the lamp, the jhari in its red cloth, the thali, the folded cloth), never the Thakorji, Lalan, chitraji or any picture of hers without her knowing (`research/pushtimarg-world.md` PR4). Never a generated or AI-altered image [79][83][84].
9. **The shrine moment is closed doors.** When a chapter must say that she prays, the first image to try is her home mandir's closed doors at the hours the house closes them, with the jhari in its red cloth and a lamp outside: it shows devotion kept, draws nothing in seva, opens onto no person (`research/pushtimarg-world.md` PR6) and has the same grammar as the locked rooms (W-37); she is the one person in the room who knows what is behind them (review 04 addendum, 1.2). Second, the shelf's small lamp with the backcloth changing by season, only if the maker's Q4/O-48 confirms a household occasion cloth (otherwise cream and the lamp). Third, her threshold drawing as the title card, the Vasant kalash on a lotus on a sathiya (`research/pushtimarg-world.md` T10) if SW1 or SW2 is chosen (Decision 4); under SW0 the kalash on a plain lotus. No door, curtain, niche or seat ever opens onto a person (PR5 to PR7).

## How to do it, step by step

**Step 1. Fill the household block, offline.** Answer the household keys in an offline notebook that is not synced (Template H5 lists them). D-31 already answers the home region, mother tongue, sampradaya and deity list. Review 04's proposed O-rows are not yet in the register, so their dates are proposals: O-36 to O-43 by Sun 25 Oct and O-44 by Sun 1 Nov, both as proposed in `reviews/04-hindu-mobile-briefs-review.md`. Still open after D-31: the prayer language and script (O-36), the shelf, the kuldevi or kuldevta and the temple she visits (O-38), the festivals she keeps beyond "all the Hindu festivals" (O-39), what her hands make (O-40), her songs (O-41), her phone (O-43, D-31 gives only "Android"), and the room (O-44). Describe the shelf in words first. Nothing is asked of her or of any relative (D-02, D-03), and nothing is photographed until the unsynced-phone rule (W-32) allows it, and then never the Thakorji, Lalan, chitraji or any picture of hers (Step 6).

**Step 2. Gate each tradition (D-28).** A tradition is eligible only if all three hold. (a) Its flag is OK or OK-note, not FLAG or OUT. (b) It is hers: her region, her sampradaya or her hands call for it, or it is a floor art of her region. (c) It can be drawn at the phone floor without losing its identity. Scoring then ranks only the eligible ones (Template H1). Until her answers are in, each row reads "hers if", and nothing is chosen.

**Step 3. Apply the collapse rule.** Once the answers are in, one to three rows usually survive. Where her own hands made a craft (O-40), that craft is the primary tradition whatever its score. Decision 2 (her hand-made material), decision 6 (the reconstruction marker) and the new decision 11 then become one thing, because the reconstruction is drawn in that craft's own line and the idiom adds only subject and architecture. If her hands made nothing relevant, decision 2 stays her actual material and the idiom dresses only the cards, titles and set.

**Step 4. Choose architecture from the element table (Template H2).** The order follows her region (research K1); for her (D-31) the Pushtimarg haveli row and the Gujarat set leads come first. Elements flagged as shared stay out, as listed under "What we leave out". Sets are drawn, never photographed. The sanctum (garbhagriha) is a dark, windowless room (digest only [60]) and is drawn, not photographed (J). Temple photography rules are per temple; Tamil Nadu bans it [86]; her temples' rules: the Somnath Trust bans photography and phones (its FAQ [118]) and the Ambaji trust bans phones, cameras and electronic gadgets in its premises (its home page [145]); no page states a rule for Takhteshwar, Nishkalank, Gopnath or Harsiddhi, and Dwarka's sanctum is restricted by digests only (UNVERIFIED; `research/saurashtra-world.md` T1 P01 to P03, P06, P08).

**Step 5. Build one sheet row per deity on her shelf (Template H3).** One named form per shot. Her own shelf image beats the agamic default; the maker looks, counts and describes it, and takes no photograph of a svarupa or of any picture of hers without her knowing (Step 6). Where sources disagree, the disagreement goes to the maker as a one-line question. A kuldevi or regional mother goddess without a standard form is shown only as a symbol, or not at all.

**Step 6. Choose the deity source route (R17) and fill `deity_source`.** Four routes, in the order the research prefers (J on S).
- (1) A symbol drawn by an agent as vector, checked against the sheet.
- (2) A hand-traced public-domain plate, with the plate ID and licence text logged, screened for satire and irreverence (R18) and approved by the maker as her proxy.
- (3) Her own photograph of seva objects that are not a svarupa (the lamp, the jhari in its red cloth, the thali, the folded cloth), taken on an unsynced phone; never the Thakorji, Lalan, chitraji or any picture of hers without her knowing (`research/pushtimarg-world.md` PR4, F6: a picture may be *pusht* and in seva, and the film cannot tell by looking); a svarupa only if O-52 says yes and she knows, which under D-02 means the April version at the earliest. A murti of another deity on her shelf (Ganesha, Lakshmi, a linga, a yantra): no source or sibling ruling was found, so default out until the maker rules (J, D-04). Never a framed print (its copyright is someone else's) and never a temple murti [86].
- (4) A commissioned painting under a written licence (see Decision 6).

AI clean-up of a deity plate is allowed only as non-generative scaling or tonal work, logged L1 or L2. Diffusion repair of a deity plate is banned, because it can silently change an attribute. That is stricter than the face rule for people (W-40).

**Step 7. Draft the `hindu_idiom` block as style-bible decision 11 (Template H5).** It inherits decisions 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9 and 10 by name and never edits them. Adding an eleventh decision changes the ten-decision style bible that G1 locks (W-38), so the maker approves it at G1 (O-24).

**Step 8. Brief each shot with the checks.** Fields: `motif_check` (hindu, flagged or excluded), `deity_form`, `deity_source`, `respect_check` (R1 to R20) and `phone_stroke_ok`. Flagged briefs go to Fable (the G2 pack, W-42).

**Step 9. Draw as seekable SVG where it fits (Route A).** A floor-art line-draw uses a pulli grid and a closed stroke around each dot, with `stroke-dashoffset` against `pathLength` (method read from [45]; the existing R toolkit under AGPL is for method only and is not linked or copied [50]). Shadow-puppet cut-outs use a `data-pivot` per joint, since puppets have four to twelve joints [44], and `data-z` for stacking. Torana, kalasha, lotus, diya, Om and conch are vector symbols. Gold is a flat colour with a baked highlight: VectorCraft drops filters, so a filter-made glint is lost (the craft-visual playbook). No transition, dissolve, wipe or morph touches a deity image. Entry and exit are by cut or a lamp-lit hold (R5).

**Step 10. Test on the phone, then on her phone.** Run the synthetic chart through the two chat proxies first (Exercise 4), then test real app output on her phone (O-43). The mobile-delivery track measures real output with ffprobe; this playbook does not replace that. The default view is landscape full screen, where one video pixel maps to one device pixel (J). An upright chat view scales the picture to about 0.56 of a pixel, so even clean 2 px lines are lost there (J on M).

**Step 11. Count and approve.** The agent counts arms and attributes against the row and her image. The maker counts again (R10). The maker approves every deity drawing as her proxy. Fable reads every flagged brief, every deity shot and every shot carrying a motif from the exclusion list.

**Step 12. Log the rights and the honesty label.** Each asset gets a rights row (Template H6) and an honesty label in the file name and `steps.csv` (L0 to L5). Deity images carry L0 or L1 for her own photograph (seva objects now; a svarupa picture only in the April version, with O-52 yes and her knowing), L2 only for non-generative scaling, L3 for a traced plate, and never L4 or L5. The research does not set a label for an agent-drawn symbol: TODO(research), to be set at G1; the nearest rung is L3, stylised.

## Learn-by-doing exercises

Each exercise uses public, synthetic or no material. None touches her material, and none asks anyone for anything.

### Exercise 1. Fill the household block offline

- **Goal.** Turn what you know into the household keys, with "don't know" allowed.
- **Steps.** (1) Open an offline notebook that is not synced to any cloud. (2) Answer the keys in Template H5 from memory, one line each; for the shelf write words only. If a photograph is ever planned, it is of seva objects that are not a svarupa (the lamp, the jhari in its red cloth, the thali, the folded cloth), on an unsynced phone, and never of the Thakorji, Lalan, chitraji or any picture of hers without her knowing (`research/pushtimarg-world.md` PR4, O-52). (3) Mark each answer "known", "not sure" or "ask later". (4) Keep the file offline with the ID key. Copy nothing into the repo; the repo copy keeps placeholders.
- **Time.** 45 minutes, at home, with nobody asked.
- **What good looks like.** Every key has an answer or "not sure". The D-31 entries are copied from the register, not retyped from memory. Nobody was asked, and nothing was photographed. The shelf entry is words, and no photograph of a svarupa or of any picture of hers exists or is planned without her knowing.

### Exercise 2. Count a plate, and let an agent count it too

- **Goal.** Learn the count discipline on a form from her list.
- **Steps.** (1) Pick one public-domain or CC0 plate of a form on her sheet, for example a Krishna child-form plate from a museum record with its licence text saved (Wellcome plates with the Public Domain Mark [14][15]; Cleveland records under CC0 [1]). This is learning only; the plate is not used in the film. (2) Count arms and attributes and read the hand order, then compare with the Template H3 row. (3) Ask an agent for the same count without showing it yours. (4) Write down each disagreement.
- **Time.** 30 minutes.
- **What good looks like.** Your count and the agent's agree, or the difference is named. Any attribute the row does not settle gets one "ask" line. The record and its licence text are saved with a date.

### Exercise 3. Label a temple from one CC0 photograph

- **Goal.** Learn the shikhara vocabulary and the element table.
- **Steps.** (1) Open the Commons file for the Kandariya Mahadeva temple, CC0 (Ms Sarah Welch) [21]. (2) Label the visible parts of the tower with the terms in Template H2 (latina, sekhari and bhumija as defined in [53]). (3) Mark anything you cannot confirm "not sure". (4) Check the silhouette rule: a curvilinear beehive rising to a disc.
- **Time.** 40 minutes.
- **What good looks like.** Each label has its definition from [53] or is marked "not sure". You can say in one sentence why this is a northern (Nagara) curvilinear tower rather than a southern stepped vimana [52]. You keep to the silhouette, not the carvings: the research notes that Khajuraho invites the "erotic temple" stereotype in a public room (J).

### Exercise 4. Measure the phone floor with a real file

- **Goal.** Replace the proxy rows with a measurement from a real app.
- **Steps.** (1) Use only the synthetic chart `research/hindu-visual-fixtures/phone_chart.svg`. No family material. (2) Export it as a 1080p, 24 fps clip with the command the mobile-delivery track gives (not this playbook's). (3) Send the clip only to your own phone, through the app she uses (O-43). (4) Save what the phone plays, copy it to the PC and run ffprobe on the saved file; note codec, size, bitrate and frame rate. (5) Read the line rows of Template H9 against what you see.
- **Time.** 60 minutes.
- **What good looks like.** You have one row for the real app and phone. If the 4 px floor holds, say so. If it does not, the floor moves, and the change goes to the register as a decision, not as a quiet edit to this playbook.

### Exercise 5. Audit one storyboard frame against R1 to R20

- **Goal.** Practise the respect rules as shot rules.
- **Steps.** Take one frame of your storyboard that has a lamp or a symbol, or draw one in a notebook. Walk R1 to R20 in order and mark each "pass", "fail", "n/a" or "maker". Then fix one fail in the frame itself, not with a note.
- **Time.** 30 minutes.
- **What good looks like.** Every rule carries a mark. The fix changes the frame. No deity sits on a surface that is walked on (R1), and no lamp is blown out inside the shot (R15).

### Exercise 6. Write the rights rows before the art

- **Goal.** Make the rights row the first thing written, not the last.
- **Steps.** Write four rows in the Template H6 format. (a) AR-002: the Kandariya Mahadeva CC0 photograph, reference only, status GRANTED [21]. (b) AR-001: a Wellcome plate with the Public Domain Mark, the Ganesha-with-rat sample the research records, plate screening NOT DONE, status VERIFY [15]. (c) A row for the Lakshmi plate under CC BY-NC, status ASK [14]. (d) CA-001: a commissioned painting, status NS. Save each licence text with the date.
- **Time.** 30 minutes.
- **What good looks like.** Each row has type, source, saved licence text, tradition origin and status. The CC BY-NC row says ASK, not yes.

### Exercise 7. Draft the commission brief, and do not send it

- **Goal.** Learn what a written licence must say before anyone is asked.
- **Steps.** Fill Template H7 for one form on your sheet, in your own words. Mark the open questions: territory, term, media, credit and delivery. Keep the draft in the offline folder. Do not send it. Whether an artist may be told only "a film project" is the maker's call (V5), and any commission waits for the rights consult (W-55).
- **Time.** 30 minutes.
- **What good looks like.** The draft names the film use, the territory (worldwide, because the premiere country is still open, TD-7), the term, all media including a phone file and the archive, and the credit. It is not sent.

## Templates and checklists

### Template H1. Style-options matrix (gate first, then rank)

**Gate.** Step 2 above. **Scores** are 1 to 5, five is best (J on the sourced conventions; the scores and weights are the researcher's judgment). Meaning = how much of a specific, domestic life it can carry if it is hers. Motion = how naturally it breaks into moving parts. Phone = from the chart (M) and the identity features, then J. Big screen = richness at 10 m (J). Effort = how little hand work an agent-plus-maker team needs (5 is least; hours per 10-second shot were not measured). Rights = how clean the route to drawing in the manner is.

**Formula.** Total = Meaning × 2 + Phone × 2 + Motion × 1.5 + Effort + Big screen + Rights. **Correction:** the research header says "/40", but the formula's maximum is 42.5 (2×5 + 2×5 + 1.5×5 + 5 + 5 + 5). The totals below use the formula, so no score changed; only the label is corrected here.

## Pitfalls

- **AI deity images, even as storyboard placeholders.** Anything generated can leak into the cut. Route B edits, Route C adapters and Route D LoRAs are never used on a deity (research, ai-imagery Step 9). The 2025 "Ghibli-style" trend produced no Hindu-organisation objection that the research found (digest only, UNVERIFIED) [89].
- **Mirroring a deity asset** reverses the hand order and can change the form. The research's example: Keshava, Narayana, Govinda are permutations of the same four attributes [61].
- **Mughal-look creep.** An arch or a dome "for an India feel" is the most likely slip. Check the refuses list before every set.
- **Mirror work looks Hindu and is flagged.** Abhla or shisha mirror work is central to Kathiawar embroidery, but one page read says the method "originated in Persia during 13th century" (tagged "citation needed") [129]: out by default (E27), and hers only if it is on her own piece.
- **A pichwai with a niche, an opening or a face in front of it.** In the temple Shrinathji is never painted into the pichwai and the cloth is cut where he stands (`research/pushtimarg-world.md` F3, its [35][32]), so P1 keeps the pichwai-manner border, bands and night sky with an object of hers or text at the centre and drops the figure, never the niche and never behind a face (PR5). Its origin flag is resolved to an OK-note ([38]: a Deccan origin; the page read names no Mughal or Rajput influence [38]; the Nathdwara school is a subset of the Mewar school ([114]; [137]), the one Rajput idiom E10 does not flag [37]; temple art of her own sampradaya); the cautions that remain are that a painted svarupa needs P4 by a hereditary painter, that painted pichwai belongs only to Sharad and Gopashtami chapters (PR23; the sanctum's rule from Talwar, `research/pushtimarg-world.md` [32]; a household's occasion cloth, painted or printed, is the maker's Q4/O-48, and `pushtimarg-world.md` records the open question), and that the manner is dense (the phone floor).
- **Thin lines on a phone.** A 1 px line is gone in the smaller chat proxy (M). The existing access-copy test cannot catch chat loss, because the master is fine [104].
- **Gold.** VectorCraft drops filters, so filter-made glints and glows are lost. Bake the highlight (craft-visual playbook).
- **Public domain is not respectful.** Some public-domain Kalighat plates are satirical (a Ganesh playing the dhola; a monkey "possibly Hanuman") [15][29]. Screen every plate (R18).
- **"Public domain" and "CC0" clear a record, not the subject.** CC BY-NC and "non-commercial" are ASK for a public screening [14][16].
- **A photograph of a framed print** reproduces someone else's artwork, and a framed picture on a Pushtimarg shelf may be a pusht svarupa in seva (`research/pushtimarg-world.md` PR4, F6). Photograph neither; seva objects that are not a svarupa only (Step 6).
- **The research's photography sentence overreaches.** Its element table said photography is "banned inside most sanctums [86]", but [86] covers Tamil Nadu temple premises. This playbook says photography rules are per temple: Tamil Nadu bans it [86] and the Somnath Trust bans photography and phones [118]; no page states a rule for her other temples (Takhteshwar, Nishkalank, Gopnath, Harsiddhi; `research/saurashtra-world.md` T1; UNVERIFIED).
- **Stepwell and torana builders.** A named stepwell is not cleared by its photograph; the builder must be checked (E8).
- **Sita's side.** Rao and Wikipedia disagree on whether Sita stands to Rama's left or right [61][78]. Copy her group, not a guess.
- **Rao's 1914 historical claims** (a "Buddhist relic" in the Jagannath image; Tirumala as "Harihara") are contested and are not repeated [61].
- **Three briefed assumptions the sources did not support.** (1) There is no 2023 UK change for photographs of public-domain art; the 2021 IPO guidance says a faithful copy "seems unlikely" to be original, with "a degree of uncertainty" [24]. (2) "Buying the painting does not transfer copyright in India" is true only for a painting that already exists; a commissioned work is governed by s.17(b) and its proviso [25]. (3) The arm-count and attribute failures are not measured. The documented AI failures are cultural: a "very white" Rama, sexualised goddesses, an always-angry Hanuman, tantra rendered as satanic, a saint mistaken for Rama, a "too feminine" Ram [81][82]. Arm-count errors stay UNVERIFIED and are treated as likely.
- **Dates and prices.** The HAF guidance was published 6 Oct 2025 and updated 24 Oct 2025 [79]. The Jagannath complaint is January 2026 [83][84]. The Tamil Nadu rule is dated 11 Oct 2026 [86]. Prices are digests from blocked pages. Re-check each before you cite it.
- **Two numbering traps.** The research's swastika options S0 to S2 collide with the stranger screenings S1 and S2 (renamed SW here). The research's templates T1 to T11 collide with the theatrical-delivery playbook's T8 and T10, so this playbook numbers its templates H1 to H10.
- **The deity label.** Deity images are never L4 or L5 (Step 12). A deity must never pass a diffusion repair, not even under W-40's CodeFormer rule.
- **Privacy.** The filled household block lives offline. The repo copy keeps placeholders. Photographs of her shelf are taken only by you, unseen, on an unsynced phone (W-32), and only of seva objects that are not a svarupa (the lamp, the jhari in its red cloth, the thali, the folded cloth); never the Thakorji, Lalan, chitraji or any picture of hers without her knowing (`research/pushtimarg-world.md` PR4, F6: a picture may be *pusht* and in seva, and the film cannot tell by looking); a svarupa only if O-52 says yes and she knows, which under D-02 means the April version at the earliest. Never ask a relative to photograph her shelf or her temple.
