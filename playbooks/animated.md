## Why this matters for our life journey

Your mother's life has years with no camera in them. Some years have a shoebox of prints. Others have nothing at all. Filmmakers have faced this problem for decades, and several have solved it in ways that are moving and honest.

Our charter sets the rule: real memories stay real. We may restore, restyle, animate and connect real photos and stories. We may not invent events or put words in anyone's mouth. Any scene we draw or build must be visibly a reconstruction. The films in this playbook follow that rule on purpose. Most of their methods are cheap: interviews, one drawing material, a clear card at the start, and restraint.

Some methods are expensive. Flee had about fifty people working on its animation at once [2]. Rocks in My Pockets took years [21]. We are a learner with an AI team and a deadline of 27 February 2027. So we copy the cheap methods and treat the expensive ones as references only.

## Core principles

1. **Sound comes first.** In the exemplar films the recorded voice came before the pictures. Flee's team built a sound-and-storyboard master before animating [3]. Ryan was built on about 20 hours of recorded interviews [11]. Rocks in My Pockets recorded its voice-over first and used the script as its blueprint [21]. We cannot copy that for the premiere cut. Under only-me (D-03) the premiere cut carries no recorded voice of any living person, except her own HER-PUBLIC clip if one passes one-key-three-guards (D-05, D-04). So the sound that comes first for us is her-world sound (objects and rooms in her empty house, no seva article touched, W-70) and the maker's hum, recorded in two takes (W-44, W-65). Relatives' voices arrive in the April version, recorded in response to the film (D-06).

2. **One hand-made material for everything that was never photographed.** The Missing Picture's clay figures are a material that is hard to mistake for a photograph [6][7]. Pick one material for our piece and use it for every invented scene. For the film it is whatever her own hands actually made (W-38 decision 2); cut paper, clay, thread and a floor-art line are the exercise stand-ins.

3. **Say so once, at the start.** Flee opens with a card saying that names and places were changed to protect its subjects [1]. Ours should say which scenes are reconstructed and what material marks them.

4. **Replace the stand-in with the real thing.** Tower lets the real survivor replace the actress who played her [13]. Waltz with Bashir ends by dissolving into real news footage [16]. Every reconstructed person or place should eventually meet a real photo, voice or object, or stay clearly labelled.

5. **Restraint is the craft.** Ken Burns called "mindless, anxious movement for the sake of movement" the enemy [37]. Pick two or three moments and make them perfect [28][29]. Where the picture can carry the feeling, drop the dialogue and sound effects. The Up team cut both from its montage and said the sequence felt more emotional for it [56][58].

6. **Objects and interfaces keep time.** Up's coin jar is broken open early by a flat tire, a broken leg and a storm [57]. Google's Parisian Love moves through eleven searches in about 52 seconds [54]. If she left letters, messages or a notebook of recipes, those can be the clock.

7. **Specific beats generic.** The Wonder Years' fake home movies work because the action is generic enough for any family to see itself in [62]. For a real person, generic is the failure mode. Use her kitchen, her car, her sayings and her songs (after a licence check).

8. **Change the medium, not just the filter. Build it to last.** Era shows most in grain, frame shape, tint and crop, more than in palette alone [88][91]. Snow Fall's own files still resolved fourteen years after launch, while its third-party video script had been redirected [39]. Plan for a self-contained build and a plain video file.

9. **Routines are chapters too.** A repeated ordinary shot (the same kitchen table, the same route, the same Sunday) is a time-passing device in its own right. Fixed-frame repetition works on faces, as Kalina and Hofmeester show [70][74]. Our judgment is that it works on places and objects too; the research found no sourced fixed-frame example on a place. Up's montage also keeps objects that come back across the years, such as the ties and the house repainted [56][57].

## How to do it, step by step

Steps 1 to 4 can start now. Steps 5 to 11 are the production pattern. The exercises in the next section teach each step on practice material.

**Tools.** Your PC runs Windows and has limited memory, so heavy work (research, generation, rendering) happens in the cloud wherever the photo set's `cloud` level allows (D-10, W-23). The ArtCraft Crafting Apps are installed; the charter names PhotoCraft, VectorCraft, FilmCraft, EffectCraft, LightCraft and SoundCraft. Use them as refinement layers for quick hand touch-ups. Agents do the generating. The Craft apps are free, open-source and early alpha, so every step has a fallback, and no alpha app is on the critical path. 1. **Check the rules before you touch any material.** The LEARN phase has ended (D-30, build approved 2026-10-19), but the material rule has not changed: use practice or synthetic images only, public-domain pictures or your own photos that have nothing to do with her. Family photographs wait until the pipeline and process are locked, as the charter requires: the P-LOCK process lock is Sat 14 Nov 2026 and covert collection starts Sun 15 Nov (D-13, W-06, W-10). Keep practice and family files off shared computers, phones and auto-syncing photo libraries, because family members may see them.

2. **Inventory each chapter.** For each chapter, list what exists: photographs (with date and place), letters, messages, recordings, objects, and who can confirm each fact. Mark the gaps. This list is the input to every choice below. The story gathering itself belongs to the story and surprise-ops tracks, and it stays covert. Add a texture column: routines, places and objects that could carry a fixed frame (principle 9). TODO(story): ask the story track to add that column.

3. **Gather the sound, later and carefully.** Covert collection starts Sun 15 Nov, after P-LOCK (D-13, W-10). Before the premiere there are no recorded relative interviews and no consent card is read to anyone (D-03, D-05). The gathering is album-and-hands sessions under a true sentence ("I'm filming the album while you tell me about it"), with the hands and the pages in frame, never a face, and a spoken "fine" on the file (W-49). Add her-world sound (what the maker captures alone in her empty house: kitchen objects and 60 s of room tone per room, never a seva article touched, W-10, W-70) and the maker's own hum, in two takes, closed-mouth and open vowel (W-65). Room tone alone is never a first sound (W-36, W-76). The closed-eyes method [2] is for the April version: the speaker lies back, closes their eyes and describes a place in the present tense, covering materials, colours, light, sounds and feelings. Relatives record that after the premiere, once they sign release 4d (D-06, W-48). Any earlier recorded Door A session is a bringing-in decision and waits for TD-L2c, Sun 3 Jan (W-49). Keep the original audio untouched.

4. **Cut the audio, then storyboard to it.** Cut the best 60 to 90 seconds of the sound you have and leave the natural pauses in. For the premiere cut that is her-world sound, the hum and, if one clip passes one-key-three-guards, one HER-PUBLIC line (W-35, W-45). The April version adds relatives' voices (D-06). Sketch six to ten frames, one per phrase or sound beat, and let the sound set the timing [3][21]. A sketch that needs music to work is not ready yet.

5. **Choose the time-passing device for each chapter.** Use the material you have.
   - An object that appears across years, such as a jar, a tie, a car or a kitchen table: this is Up's device [56][57].
   - Letters, messages or other written words: show them, one line per year [49][54].
   - Eight or more portraits across years: a portrait ladder, with the eyes aligned in the same frame [70][72].
   - Places but no people: a scale zoom, or a model of the place [6][43].
   - A routine with a known place, such as the kitchen table or the route to school: fixed frame on the place, one frame per era, with the people and objects changing [70][74][56]. Our judgment; the research found no sourced example of this device on a place.
   - One strong photograph: one long, slow hold, used once per chapter at most [37][38].
   - Nothing at all: show the gap with a black frame or a single object [62].
   Every device must say something true about her life. If it does not, cut it.

6. **Set the reconstruction look.** Choose one material for every invented image. For the film itself it is whatever her own hands actually made (dough, thread, a garden, a ledger), answered by the maker in week 1 and filled at G1, Sun 15 Nov, with no placeholder (W-38). Cut paper drawn in a vector editor, and clay or paper sets photographed [6][21], are the exercise stand-ins. The game-style block look of the earlier plan is retired (D-29). Where her hands made floor art, embroidery or garlands, the marker is that craft's line and decisions 2, 6 and 11 are one thing; where that is seva craft, the marker is the *making* (the stitch, the thread, the flower string, the sathiyo line, the bhog dough), never the finished seva article and never a reconstruction painted onto Thakorji's clothes; otherwise the Hindu idiom dresses cards, titles and set only (W-38, W-72). No deity is drawn, generated or photographed in a reconstruction (W-61). Write a one-page art bible with three "good" and three "not good" examples, and a rule for how hands and faces are drawn [1][2]. Flee's team kept a document like this, down to how to draw a hand or an eye [1][2].

7. **Animate with restraint.** The default is a still photo with one slow move (a push, a pull or a pan) across about 3 to 5 percent of the frame width, held for no more than 8 to 10 seconds. These limits are the researcher's judgment from the notes, not a tested rule. Alternate still and moving shots so the motion keeps its meaning. For each operation, choose the route in the table below: the agent team generates, you refine in a Craft app, and a named fallback catches any failure. The manual layer method (pen-tool outline, content-aware fill, a 28mm-style camera, a slow push) is the fallback for cutouts [28][29]. The Fusion 3D and Blender routes are in the appendix [33][104]. Use parallax only on your best photographs (see Exercise 5).

8. **Prepare the sources.** Old prints are soft and grainy. The 110 film of 1972 was small and is often "rather grainy and unsharp" [93]. Scan small prints at 600 ppi. The handout minimum is 400 ppi for prints, and slides and film need 2000 to 3000 dpi [96][127]. By the notes' arithmetic (round 2, research Q1), a 4 by 6 inch print at 600 ppi gives about 3,600 pixels on the long side, enough for a 1080p push-in with margin; a 3.5 by 5 inch print gives about 3,000, roughly 9 percent short of that need. The notes expect the shortfall to be invisible, because a print holds little detail beyond about 300 ppi; that is one source, so test it on three of her prints. Dye fades unevenly, and shadows and cyan lose the most [89]. Correct each colour channel separately rather than with one filter, and keep the print's own character. Batch clean-up is an agent job, not a hand job (see the table below).

**Steps 7 and 8, by operation.** Heavy generation runs in the cloud session on practice images only. Family photographs stay on the PC (`cloud: none` by default; only the maker raises it, W-24), because the charter forbids uploading personal material to a third party without the maker's explicit say. The Craft-app cells come from the research team's reading of the apps' source and docs, not from running them on Windows, so each stays TODO until the maker has tested it. The vendors say so themselves: EffectCraft's README states "macOS is the most tested platform. Linux and Windows users have hit basic interaction bugs", and its first commit was on 1 October 2026 [126]; FilmCraft shipped six releases between 8 and 18 October 2026 [123][126] (advisor check, 2026-10-18). So: pin the installed version in every Craft cell (W-22; no Launcher, D-12), do not update mid-exercise, and run the five-minute acceptance test from research F5 before any cell loses its TODO.

9. **Put the era on the new layer only.** Apply grain, a 4:3 crop or a tint to title cards and reconstructions, not to the family photographs themselves. Set grain and the 4:3 crop in the Craft apps: EffectCraft's grain effect (named "Add Grain" in its source; the README lists only a "noise and grain" category, so confirm the on-screen label on the PC), and a 1440 x 1080 FilmCraft sequence (TODO(craft-motion)) [123][126]. Blue Spill filmed a hand-cut title card on real Super 8 stock so it would sit with the family's cine film [64]. A typical Super 8 look uses 12 to 18 frames a second, a 4:3 frame, reduced sharpness, grain and a slight colour tint, with stabilisation turned off [88]. Do not add fake date stamps. Do not age material that is already aged [89].

10. **Build the honesty layer.** Write the disclosure card: one short paragraph at the start that names which scenes are reconstructed and what material marks them [1]. For each reconstructed person or place, write the reveal: which real photo, voice or object replaces it, and at which point in the piece. If no real item exists, the stand-in stays labelled.

Ask a viewer who has not been briefed to name the reconstructed scenes. Master to agent-pipeline's project settings card (1920x1080, 24 fps, Rec.709, ProRes 422 HQ), signed Sun 1 Nov (W-14 to W-16). The phone files, PH1 and PH2, are emitted from the same locked timeline with Trim P and their own caption burn-in, never a second edit (W-63), and every reconstruction is briefed to the phone-safe frame from the first C01 brief: must-read type at an x-height of 3.2% (hard floor) to 3.8% (target), meaning lines 4 px, hands and the object inside the central 80% of height. The venue's written answer, due Sun 15 Nov, decides file or DCP (W-17). Keep the H.264 1080p MP4 as the screening and fallback copy, not the master [78], exported from FilmCraft with an ffmpeg fallback in the cloud (TODO(craft-motion); TODO(toolchain)). Settled (W-15): a 1080p master, stills prepared at 3840 px on the long edge, a 2K Flat DCP derived from the master and never re-authored, and 4K not needed (UHD only for single hero shots if a 4K cinema is booked). The arithmetic in research Q1 (round 2) agrees: 600 ppi holds at 1080p and 2K. Keep a theatrical master and a plain MP4 always, even if an interactive version also exists. Interactives that died in the research died through a runtime or a third party, not through their files [46][47]. For any web version, build a self-contained folder: no content-delivery-network scripts, no third-party video host and no analytics [39][46]. Offer a static fallback [100]. ## Learn-by-doing exercises

Every exercise uses practice material. The time estimates are ours, not sourced. Each exercise ends with a "good" line you can check yourself. If your PC struggles with memory, run practice renders in the cloud session, but only with practice images. One editor per type, for every exercise: PhotoCraft for photos, VectorCraft for vector, FilmCraft for video, all as installed. The fallbacks are GIMP, Inkscape and a free video editor, pending toolchain (TODO(toolchain)). Do not add a second editor.

### Exercise 1: Watch one beat sheet with a stopwatch

- **Goal:** learn how three pieces time their beats. Use Up's opening montage (about 4.5 minutes [56]), Parisian Love (about 52 seconds in one analysis, and 60 seconds in a comScore snippet [54][55]) and Dear Sophie (about 90 seconds [49]).
- **Steps:** (1) Watch once with no notes. (2) Watch again with a stopwatch. Log every beat with a timestamp, what changes (an object, the camera or the sound) and the type of cut. (3) Compare your log with the written breakdown in the notes and list every disagreement. (4) Find out where each piece can be watched legally. For the four films in Best references, US options from JustWatch listings checked 2026-10-17/18 [129]: Flee free with ads on Tubi; Tower free on Kanopy or Hoopla (library card); The Missing Picture free on Kanopy (SD); Up rent 3.99 USD. About 4 USD for all four films if your library card gives Kanopy or Hoopla access. TODO(research): Parisian Love and Dear Sophie are not on that page, so verify their streaming availability. TODO(maker): your country and whether you have a library card, since prices and availability differ outside the US. The Dear Sophie link in the notes is a mirror; no official upload was confirmed [50].
- **Time estimate:** 2 to 3 hours.
- **Good:** one beat sheet per piece, with at least ten beats for Up. You can say which beat carries the feeling and why, and whether the sound was stripped. Your disagreement list goes to the team watch session the research asked for. TODO(research): schedule that session with a stopwatch.

### Exercise 2: Closed-eyes interview, practice round

- **Goal:** learn to draw out sensory detail in the present tense [2].
- **Steps:** Ask a friend who knows this is practice, and who is not one of her relatives (tell them only that you are practising a technique, and say nothing about a film, D-03), to sit back and close their eyes. Ask them to describe a room from their own childhood in the present tense, for example "I'm at the table. What is on it?" Ask about materials, colours, light, sounds, smells and feelings. Do not interrupt. Record it, then transcribe the first five minutes.
- **Time estimate:** 1 hour for the session and 1 hour for the transcript.
- **Good:** at least 15 concrete details in the transcript. You can name three that you could draw. The speaker says afterwards that they felt listened to.

### Exercise 3: Audio-first cut

- **Goal:** let the voice set the picture.
- **Steps:** Using the recording from Exercise 2, or a public-domain speech clip, cut a 60-second piece that keeps its pauses, in FilmCraft with the audio track only (TODO(craft-motion): the notes do not cover audio cutting in FilmCraft or SoundCraft). Write six storyboard frames, one for each phrase, on paper or in VectorCraft. Add one object that appears twice.
- **Time estimate:** 3 hours.
- **Good:** the 60 seconds stand up without music. The frames follow the phrasing. The repeated object appears twice and changes between the two appearances.

### Exercise 4: One material, one disclosure card

- **Goal:** build an invented scene in one visible material and disclose it.
- **Steps:** Invent a small room or street and label it clearly as invented. Make it in cut paper drawn in VectorCraft (fallback: Inkscape), or as a short floor-art line drawing (W-72). Animate a slow move of about 8 seconds across it in FilmCraft (TODO(craft-motion)). Write a three-sentence disclosure card that names what is invented.
- **Time estimate:** 4 hours.
- **Good:** a viewer can tell the scene is drawn or built without being told. The card names what is invented. Every invented scene in the piece uses this same material.

### Exercise 5: Parallax spike on practice snapshots

- **Goal:** find out what photo animation can do with small, soft prints.
- **Prerequisites:** a flatbed scanner, or a phone on a tripod or copy stand (which one you have is unknown: TODO(toolchain)). By the research team's arithmetic, a phone shot of a 4 x 6 print at about 4,000 px on the long edge clears the roughly 3,300 px push-in need, so a flatbed is not required for this exercise [127]. A GPU for DepthFlow on the PC, or run it in the cloud session with practice images only; the cloud route was checked only on a GPU-less Linux container, and Windows is untested [113]. A scripted flat-push fallback, named once toolchain confirms the tool (TODO(toolchain)).
- **Steps:** Pick three small, soft practice snapshots that are public-domain or otherwise non-personal. Scan each at 600 ppi [96], or photograph it on the phone rig. Method A (manual): cut out three layers in PhotoCraft (fallback: GIMP), fill the gaps, place the layers in depth, and keyframe a push of 3 to 5 percent over about eight seconds in EffectCraft's 3D camera or FilmCraft (TODO(craft-visual); TODO(craft-motion)) [28][29][123][126]. Method B (automatic): run DepthFlow, if your PC has Python 3.10 or newer and a suitable graphics card, or run it in the cloud session [30][31]. A plain CLI render holds the camera still, so motion needs a short script, which the agent team writes in the build phase, not in LEARN [113]. Compare both methods at 1080p on a full screen.
- **Time estimate:** 6 to 8 hours for the first pass.
- **Good:** no gaps show at 1080p, and faces are clean at the push-in. You can name the snapshot that fails and say why. If only a slow flat push works for a snapshot, that is a valid result, and the notes suggest it (researcher's judgment). The spike on her own photographs is a production step, to be done after P-LOCK on Sat 14 Nov (D-13).

### Exercise 6: Portrait ladder with practice portraits

- **Goal:** learn alignment and rhythm across years.
- **Steps:** Gather eight portraits of one practice person taken across several years. Mark both eyes on each one. Align every portrait to the same eye positions and the same crop, using a grid in PhotoCraft (fallback: GIMP), the same idea as the overlay grid in the Everyday app [71]. Hold each portrait for one to two seconds, with a short dissolve of about half a second (our judgment). Keep the total under 30 seconds. Automatic five-point landmark alignment, as used on Kalina's "7777 Days" [72][77], is free with dlib, but dlib's Windows installation is untested; if it fails, align eight portraits by hand and log the time. dlib ships on PyPI as source only, and building it needs CMake and a C++ compiler [117][119]. OpenCV's YuNet detector returns the same five points from one wheel and is the other option to test; its accuracy on faded prints is untested [122].
- **Time estimate:** 3 hours.
- **Good:** the eyes do not jump between frames. The rhythm changes once. You can explain why you cut rather than morphed.

### Exercise 7: A floor-art title card

- **Goal:** make a title card as a drawn floor-art line, timed, and then make an era cue physically and film it.
- **Steps:** (1) Draw one rangoli or sathiyo line on a dark ground as seekable SVG (Route A): a pulli grid, a closed stroke around each dot, `stroke-dashoffset` against `pathLength` for the line-draw, cream lines, meaning lines 4 px, dots 6 px, holes 4 px, no hairlines (W-72; `playbooks/hindu-visual.md` Step 9 and Template H9). Run the line-draw with `recipes/r3_linedraw.py` (`playbooks/toolchain.md`, Exercise 6) and write down how long the card took you. This is a practice drawing for your own screen: whether any sathiyo is shown in the film is your call at O-66 (SW0 by default, `playbooks/hindu-visual.md` Template H8), and no deity is drawn on it (W-61). (2) Print the finished card on paper, or hand-letter it. Film it with a phone, held by hand, with stabilisation off, in ordinary room light [88]. Grade it to a 4:3 frame with grain and a slight tint in FilmCraft (a 1440 x 1080 sequence). Take the grain from EffectCraft's grain effect (named "Add Grain" in its source; confirm the on-screen label on the PC); FilmCraft's Noise effect is not a film-grain emulation, as far as the notes found, so test it before relying on it (TODO(craft-motion)) [123][126]. Put it side by side with the clean digital card and compare. (3) Look at both on a phone at arm's length.
- **Time estimate:** 3 hours (our estimate; replace it with your timed figure for the drawing).
- **Good:** the line draws at an even pace and every stroke is still readable on a phone. The card looks filmed, not added. The grade touches only this card.

## Decisions you'll need to make

For each decision, the options come first, then our current lean. A lean is a starting point, not a decision. Each one stays open until you decide, except where the register has already decided or defaulted it: those entries say so and link [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md).

1. **How invented scenes look.**
   - Options: (a) one hand-made material, such as cut paper or clay, or whatever her own hands made; (b) full character animation in the style of Flee; (c) actors re-performing on camera and traced frame by frame, as in Tower.
   - Our lean: (a), with slow moves, a disclosure card and reveals [1][6][13]. Defaulted in the register (W-38, [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md)): the material is whatever her own hands actually made, answered by the maker in week 1 (O-06) and set at G1 (Sun 15 Nov). Candidate hand-made idioms, if hers and each held to the phone floor: the floor-art line (sathiyo, rangoli), the stitch line, the flower-string line and the dough; shadow-puppet cut-outs and scroll panels are structure to borrow, not her craft (`playbooks/hindu-visual.md` Template H1 and Decision 7). A hanging or cloth changed by hands is shot content, never a transition (W-72). W-38 stays in charge. Option (b) is far too large for this schedule [2]. Option (c) needs actors and a rotoscope workflow, so we do not recommend it.

2. **How much motion.**
   - Options: a still with one slow move; parallax on the best photographs; full animation.
   - Our lean: a still with one slow move as the default, and parallax only for the photographs that pass Exercise 5. The notes suggest picking a few sections and making them perfect [28][29].

3. **Which device carries each chapter.**
   - Options: an object clock, an interface timeline, a portrait ladder, a scale zoom, or a long hold (see step 5 above).
   - Our lean: a repeating object as the main clock across the whole piece; a portrait ladder for the years that have photographs; an interface timeline only if she left written messages. This is judgment and has not been tested. Defaulted in the register (W-32, W-33, [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md)): the object clock is the film's only transition device, seen first and last, and every chapter opens on hands.

4. **Hard cut or morph between portraits.**
   - Options: a hard cut on aligned eyes, or a morph made with free frame-interpolation tools such as RIFE or FILM, marked on screen as a morph [77].
   - Our lean: a hard cut. It is cheaper and more honest, and interpolation shows artefacts between very different faces [77]. TODO(research): establish the tedium threshold for morphs, which the notes could not find. Our judgment is to keep any single morph or ladder between 20 and 90 seconds, with one idea and a change of rhythm halfway.

5. **Where personal files get processed.**
   - Options: your own PC; a cloud container, which the charter prefers for heavy work; or a hosted web tool.
   - Decided (D-15, W-23, W-24; see [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md)): practice material can go anywhere. Every family photo set carries `cloud: none` by default and only the maker raises it. ## Pitfalls

- **Reel grammar.** Whip pans, beat-synced zooms and trending transitions are the anti-pattern the charter names. The notes list them but did not research them further. TODO(research): find a source on reel-grammar anti-patterns before anyone copies or rejects them.
- **The same move on every photo.** Uniform motion turns a memory into a slideshow. Alternate still and moving segments [79].
- **A swelling score doing the work.** One French review said Dear Sophie was "a bit too much like American soap operas", in the notes' translation [52]. Let the picture carry the feeling, and cut the music early.
- **Unlabelled fakes.** Stories We Tell mixed unlabelled Super 8 re-creations with real home movies, and critics split between praise and discomfort [66][67]. Its fake Super 8 is a good lesson in how convincing a fake can look. For factual chapters, do not copy the unlabelled approach, because charter rule 4 requires every reconstruction to be visibly one.
- **Selection by popularity.** Facebook's 2014 Year in Review put a photo of Eric Meyer's late six-year-old daughter under party graphics, because it had many likes and comments [81][82]. Select her photographs by hand; you are the proxy, and the advisor flags.
- **Stacked era filters.** Avocado appliances, Cooper Black and heavy grain on every frame look like a costume [87][90]. Change the medium on the new layer only, and do not age scans that are already aged [89].
- **Faces at small sizes.** Depth maps and interpolation fail first on thin objects and on faces [27][77]. Keep push-ins gentle near faces, or cut instead.
- **A deity drawn, generated, photographed or moved.** Under the default deity policy (P1, W-61) no deity is drawn, generated or photographed in any scene, reconstruction or title; symbols (lamp, kalash, lotus, conch, Om, linga, padukas, yantra, tulsi) and the closed mandir doors carry devotion. No AI route ever touches a deity, no seva article is touched or handled to be filmed (W-70), and no reconstruction is painted onto Thakorji's clothes (W-38 decision 2). The 1992 animated *Ramayana* drew objections to gods "drawn as cartoon figures" (`playbooks/hindu-visual.md`, Decision 2). `playbooks/hindu-visual.md` and `playbooks/pushtimarg-world.md` own the rules.
- **AI face animation and restoration.** Charter principle 4 forbids invented events, fake people and invented speech. Our team rule, from the charter review, adds: no AI animation of any real person's face and no synthetic voice of any real person, living or dead, even with consent (D-16, decided). Restoration must not invent facial detail. Not researched: AI face-animation reactions, such as Deep Nostalgia (TODO(research)).
- **Runtime dependencies.** Flash-based interactives from several news sites broke when Flash support ended on 31 December 2020 [47]. Shared resources and third-party scripts also rot [46]. Self-contain the build.
- **Music clearance.** The Wonder Years' theme song disappeared from Netflix because relicensing was costly (search snippet) [105]. Check every song before you use it.
- **Surprise leaks.** Relatives' group chats, shared computers and cloud photo libraries can show her the work too early. The charter names all three. Keep drafts off them.
- **Phone "memory" features.** Phone and photo services add music, titles and transitions by theme, so every memory looks alike [83]. Do not run her photographs through them.
- **Tools that may not last.** DepthFlow is new: version 1.0.1 was published on 25 August 2026 [30]. Its documentation was blocked for the researcher. TODO(research): confirm the Resolve free-edition feature split. Keep a manual route working for every shot, so no single tool is a single point of failure.
