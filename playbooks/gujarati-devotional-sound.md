## Why this matters for her film

The premiere's sound has to come from her house, the sea and the maker's own hum. Any sung recording of this music is a living voice, and most carry a label's master [M2.4]. Under only-me (D-03), a living voice cannot be in the premiere cut unless it is her own HER-PUBLIC clip (D-05), and no musician can be commissioned before the premiere (D-07). So the Gujarati and Pushtimarg music in the premiere is a set of struck objects, rooms, water and the hum. The sung devotion waits for the April version (D-06, W-48).

Two other things make this harder than choosing tracks. First, the phone. The research's phone model says a closed-mouth hum and the pakhawaj's bass strokes fail on a 50% speaker, and the maker's own Android tone ladder has to replace the model (D-27). Second, origin and rights. The tradition's own instrument lists name instruments whose origins are flagged as court, colonial, shared or unverified, and a label's "Traditional" credit is not a clearance. One 2022 label compilation has three tracks marked "Traditional", and one of them is a film song whose music director is named [M2.8]. D-28 says a shared or ambiguous origin is left out until the maker rules, so the default is silence.

Her sound also carries reverence. Kirtan is offered as seva. A pada is not a mood bed, a darshan is not a life stage, and no raga name goes on screen.

## Core principles

1. **Hers first, and Hindu only (D-28, D-31).** "Indian themes" means Hindu. A sound enters because it is hers: her tradition, her region, her household. A shared or ambiguous origin is `ASK`, and an `ASK` still open on Mon 14 Dec becomes silence or room tone.
2. **The premiere sounds like objects, rooms, the sea and the hum (D-05, D-07, W-44).** Sung devotion is an April column. A beat that seems to want a bhajan is written as silence plus one struck bell.
3. **Two columns, always: composition and recording.** A public-domain text does not make a tune free, and a free tune does not make a recording free. A label's "Traditional" is entered `unknown` until a registry or the original record says otherwise.
4. **The phone decides, not the model (D-27).** Hum on an open vowel. Open the film with a first sound above about 400 Hz. The maker's Android tone ladder at 50% volume settles every pass or fail in this playbook.
5. **Kirtan is seva (reverence).** No pada as a bed or a mood. Never cut a pada mid-line, because the poet's name sits in the last line [4]. No darshan name as a life stage. No raga claim unless a source names it.
6. **The maker's hands, an empty house, nothing of her (D-02, D-17, W-10).** The sounds of objects and rooms are captured by the maker, with no recording of her and no relative asked to record, on a device that does not sync to a shared cloud.
7. **Nothing narrows the field (D-02, D-03, D-15).** Outbound briefs are name-free. A hum is not sent to a vendor to be identified without the maker's OK. A test on her own phone is a decision, not a default.

## Learn-by-doing exercises

Each exercise needs no code. Times are estimates. Each one ends with a row you can check.

**Exercise 1. Hum two takes and log them (week 1, about 40 minutes).**
- *Time.* About 40 minutes.
- *Goal.* Two uncleaned takes of the tune she sang to you: one closed-mouth, one on an open vowel, on a device that does not sync.
- *Steps.* Turn sync off on the device. Record the tune twice: closed mouth ("mm"), then open vowel ("aa" or "la"). Name each file with an ID only, never a name or a place. Save both in `originals/`. Fill in the hum log (template E) except the decision. Do not play the takes to anyone, and do not send them to an identification service.
- *What good looks like.* Two files, both uncleaned and both logged. If you know the first words, a text-only note of them in Gujarati script, with the source where you found them.

**Exercise 2. The Android tone ladder at 50% (before Sat 14 Nov, about 30 minutes).**
- *Time.* About 30 minutes, before Sat 14 Nov.
- *Goal.* Find the lowest audible tone on the project's Android phone, and re-read the pass or fail verdicts against it.
- *Steps.* Use the project phone that the mobile protocol names (the mobile-delivery playbook, protocol row 10). Set the volume to 50% in a quiet room. Play the ladder from 100 Hz to 3.2 kHz, then pink noise, then earbuds. Write down the lowest audible tone. Play the two hum takes, and the bell, jhanj, manjira or conch if you have them. Write PASS or FAIL for each on that phone. Use the project phone, not her phone, unless you are sure a test there tips nothing (D-02).
- *What good looks like.* One row per take with the lowest audible tone written down. If the closed-mouth take is audible, say so. The verdict is the phone's, not the table's.

**Exercise 3. Listen for the first sound (about 30 minutes, after Exercise 2).**
- *Time.* About 30 minutes, after Exercise 2.
- *Goal.* Test the rule that the first sound must carry, with energy above about 400 Hz.
- *Steps.* Play a room-tone-only opening on the Android phone. Then play, as the first sound, a struck bell, a spoon on a steel *vati*, the pressure-cooker whistle and the jhanj, one at a time. Note which ones the phone carries.
- *What good looks like.* A short ranked list of first sounds that carry on the phone. Room tone and a distant swell are written down as failing.

**Exercise 4. Place one tune (by Mon 14 Dec, about 60 minutes per candidate).**
- *Time.* About 60 minutes per candidate, by Mon 14 Dec.
- *Goal.* Decide which hum case (HC-1 to HC-7) the tune falls in, with the source named.
- *Steps.* Type only the first line, in Gujarati script if you know it, or in Latin spelling if not. Search for the lyric. For any composer, find the composer and the death year on two independent pages. Use the hum-case table in the templates. For a Narsinh Mehta or "Vaishnav Jan To" tune, look for Narayan Khare's death year in a printed biography. The research could not find it [21][55]. Record `composition_status` and `recording_status` in separate columns.
- *What good looks like.* A finished hum-log row with the case, the source and the decision: hum in the premiere, silence and kitchen sound instead, an April question, or a publisher question (which tells someone).

**Exercise 5. Read a label's credits and split the two columns (about 45 minutes).**
- *Time.* About 45 minutes.
- *Goal.* Practise separating composition from recording, using the 2022 Saregama Navratri compilation as the research read it [34].
- *Steps.* Open the credits CSV in the fixtures folder (`research/gujarati-devotional-sound-fixtures/saregama_navratri_garba_songs_2022_credits_as_read_2026-10-19.csv`), or the table in the note's M2.8. For each of the 12 tracks, write the composer as printed, the lyricist if printed, and the recording owner. Mark every "Traditional" as `unknown`. For Avinash Vyas (died 20 Aug 1984, term to 31 Dec 2044) and Purushottam Upadhyay (died 11 Dec 2024, term to 31 Dec 2084), use the sourced dates. For the other named composers, the death years were not read, so mark them "to confirm".
- *What good looks like.* Twelve rows with two status columns. The three "Traditional" rows read `unknown`. No row says "cleared" because it appears on a label.

**Exercise 6. Set the darshan sets, with sources, and keep raga names off the screen (about 30 minutes).**
- *Time.* About 30 minutes.
- *Goal.* Build the darshan column of the cue sheet without naming a raga on any card.
- *Steps.* Use the darshan table in the templates. For each darshan, put the two sources' sets side by side, and mark agreement as agree, partial or disagree. Write the cue value as `tradition_set:<darshan>:<source id>`. Confirm that no darshan name is printed on a card, and that kitchen-hour values never rise in darshan order across the modules.
- *What good looks like.* Eight rows. Three rows agree in full and five are partial or disagree (Pitfall 7). Every raga in the sheet has a source ID beside it.

## Reverence and what we leave out

**The reverence rules.**
- Kirtan is seva, not atmosphere. Do not use a pada as a bed or a mood. Never cut it mid-line.
- Never put kirtan under a scene of her being cared for. The Mangla and Sandhya darshans are the parental moods, and pushtimarg-world's VR-1 forbids seating her in the svarupa's or Yashoda's place [R2]. The darshan value in the sheet never maps to a life stage. Shayan is never "the present" [R2 T3 header].
- A garba circle round a goddess's lamp is worship, not decoration. A garba never scores a kitchen scene.
- The goddesses keep their own registers. Jai Adhya Shakti, Harsiddhi and Ambe are Shakta registers, and each gets its own frames or chapter [R2 PR18].
- Shiva's shore: the Koliyak shrine is Shiva's. A recording of the sea there is fine. A recording of the temple is a permission question (Pitfall 20).
- Narsinh Mehta, Dayaram and Mahaprabhuji are tradition. Do not paraphrase them on a card beyond the family's own words.
- Do not tell the Akbar and Tansen legend about Mirabai (D-28).
- Reverence norms for recording a kirtan outside a haveli rest on one 2021 study's interviews. No practitioner, label or musician was contacted (D-03). Treat them as the study's words, not as rules.

## Pitfalls

1. **The closed-mouth hum fails on the phone.** The model shows a loss of 6 to 21 dB at the 500 Hz corner. "The hum survives" is half true: it survives on an open vowel. The END cue and the motif need the open-vowel take (W-36, W-44).
2. **A room-tone opening fails as a first sound.** W-36 says "black and one true room sound from her kitchen." On the phone, room tone's rumble is inaudible. The first sound needs energy above about 400 Hz: a struck bell, a spoon on a steel *vati*, the cooker whistle or the jhanj.
3. **A label's "Traditional" is a claim, not a clearance.** Three of twelve tracks on one 2022 compilation say "Traditional". One of them is a film song with a named music director [34][33].
4. **A garba that is a film song is an owned composition.** Humming it is a performance of the work (HC-4). Chogada Tara, "based on 'Hey Ranglo' written & composed by Avinash Vyas" [35], is one example; the sources disagree on other attributions.
5. **Attribution conflicts.** "Mehndi Te Vavi Malve" is credited "Traditional (Folk)" for lyrics in the film's track list, "Traditional" as composer on the compilation, and Avinash Vyas as lyricist on his Wikipedia table. "He Ranglo" is Vyas's on one page and Upadhyay's on another. Only a registry extract or the original label copy is authority. A web page is a lead.
6. **Madhurashtakam is not one of the sixteen.** The brief and the review pair it with Yamunashtakam as "the pair". The first is the opening Shodasha Grantha. The second is attributed to Vallabhacharya by tradition [12][13]. Print "attributed to". The only text file the research read carries a personal-study notice, so type the card from the family's copy [14].
7. **The darshan sources agree less than the summary says.** The note's summary says the sources disagree on five of eight rows. Its table marks two rows "disagree" and three "partial". Read it as: full agreement on three rows, partial or disagreement on five. Use sets, never single ragas.
8. **Raga names stay off the screen, and a drone is not a raga.** No credit prints a raga unless the recording's metadata does.
9. **"Free" libraries are not licences.** The archive.org haveli library has no licence [44]. The CC BY-NC labels on the haveli recital and the pakhawaj recital were set by the uploader, not the rights holder [45][46]. The Pixabay licence has no warranty and was read at its 2024-11-18 terms [48]. The Gujarat High Court's direction on Chinese manja (Sept 2026) is SR and UNVERIFIED in its detail [53].
10. **No recording inside a haveli or at darshan.** The sanctum's sound is made by living voices (D-05). Strike the household's own bell, jhanj or manjira in an empty room.
11. **Kites are a safety question first.** Plain cotton thread only. No crowd, no terrace shouting. The SR summary says glass-coated thread is restricted and that fatal injuries have happened in Gujarat in earlier years [53].
12. **Testing on her own phone may tip her off (D-02).** The protocol's test device is the project's Android phone. Use hers only if you are sure it tells her nothing.
13. **Identification by hum sends a recording to a vendor (D-15).** Not without the maker's OK (question 14).
14. **The commission window is tight.** The brief says about five weeks for commission, performance, delivery and one revision. That is 6 Mar to about 10 Apr, when the April response recordings close (W-48). The April mix runs 11 to 23 Apr. Start the brief at once after 6 Mar, not later.
15. **"A private film" needs a wording check.** The brief template's cover line is true of a film that is not yet released. It will not be true of a public premiere or a later cinema, and the letter says so. D-18 forbids untrue statements and allows omission. Fable reads the wording before any brief goes out.
16. **Register ID clashes.** The note cites O-38, O-41, O-46, O-47, O-50, O-51 and O-54. None is a row of `DECISIONS.md` (Table 3 ends at O-35). O-51 appears to be the song question, O-07 in the register. The note's "H-1" to "H-7" clash with H (the maker's hours), so this playbook writes them HC-1 to HC-7. Its "S1" and "S2" source classes clash with the register's stranger screenings, so this playbook writes them E1-S1 and E1-S2. The note's "T8" header clashes with the rights log (T8), so this playbook names the consult list T10 only.
17. **Dates the note records but this pass did not verify.** The note says 27 Feb 2027 is Shrinathji's Patotsav, that Dhanurmas runs 16 Dec to 14 Jan, and that the Vasant season begins Thu 11 Feb. These come from repo files R2 and R7. TODO(research): verify each against a dated calendar before any sound, card or schedule depends on it.
18. **No garba or dhol capture is possible before Sept 2027.** The next Sharad Navratri runs Thu 30 Sep to Sat 9 Oct 2027 [52], after the April version. Do not use a stand-in without a label.
19. **The "Shivanand Swami" signature is a signature, not a documented person.** It appears in two aartis. The Surat identification rests on a 2017 social-media post and popular sites (SR) [28]. Do not print "written by" on a card.
20. **The Koliyak temple is Shiva's, and its Pandava story needs a citation.** The shrine is reachable only at low tide, and tides are strongest at new and full moon [51]. Plan each day from that day's tide table. Record the water's edge, mic low, not the far swell.
