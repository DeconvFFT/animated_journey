## Why this matters for our film

The charter makes the film's world Hindu: Sanatan Dharma and its sanskriti, rendered as animated design she will love (D-28). The game-world look is dropped (D-29). "Indian themes" always means Hindu themes: no Mughal or other Islamic motifs, no Christian motifs, ever. A Hindu motif earns its place by being hers (Principle 2), so her answers are the first input:

- **Rites by life stage** give chapter markers: naming, first rice, first letters, her sampradaya's initiation and its tulasi *kanthi*, marriage, the rites for her children. Most rites leave no photograph, so a marker needs an object or photo (Template A).
- **Rasa** gives each 3:00 module one dominant feeling, with an accent sampled from her own material (Template B). **Vatsalya is her household's own bhava (D-31), not a borrowing.**
- **Festivals and vrats** give the calendar and collide with the schedule. On her sky (D-31; Drik for her state's city, read 2026-10-19, and T; Template D): Dhanteras Fri 6 Nov (Drik; T prints Sat 7); Diwali and Chopda Puja Sun 8; **Annakut Mon 9 Nov**, the flagship festival of her sampradaya (Delhi says Tue 10); Gujarati New Year (Bestu Varas) Tue 10; Bhai Bij Wed 11; **Labh Panchami Sat 14 Nov is P-LOCK**; Jalaram Bapa Jayanti Mon 16 Nov; Prabodhini Fri 20 and Sat 21 Nov with Tulsi Vivah Sat 21; Uttarayana Thu 14 and Fri 15 Jan, the hard deadline for asks if she keeps it (last send Tue 12 Jan, answers to Tue 19 Jan: W-10; the 14 Jan kite custom is in Wikipedia; her keeping it is UNVERIFIED); picture lock Sat 6 Feb is Maha vad Amas (Drik Delhi prints Mauni Amavasya); Vasant Panchami Thu 11 Feb; **the premiere, Sat 27 Feb, is Shri Nathji ko Patotsav**; the her-turn conversation moves off Maha Shivaratri (Chaturdashi 12:03 Sat 6 Mar to 13:46 Sun 7 Mar; Shiva is hers) to Sun 7 Mar after about 14:00, else Mon 8 Mar (W-48). A festival of another region is not hers (D-31).
- **Invocation lines** are few and checked: hers first (Template H Q33), then the D-31 block, then the pan-Hindu fallback (Template C; W-35).
- **Objects and hands** give the object clock and chapter openings. The object clock is a hand-held kitchen object that can stand under the screen (the velan and patlo, the masala dabbo, the thepla tin; W-32) and never a seva article; chapters open on hands making or tending (the stitch, the flower string, the garbo's lamp, the kanthi beads; W-33; Template E). The hichko is the wrapper's seat and never the clock (W-67).
- **Hard chapters** are shown as what she keeps, never the event (Template G; D-04).

A mixed public room includes strangers, men, children and people who will read a Sanskrit verse as a lesson or a memorial. A card that tells the room how to feel, or reads as a memorial for a living woman, fails the film even when it is beautiful. Every card must also read on a phone (D-27); script shaping and phone-size checks belong to the sibling devotional-sound-script track (TODO(research) below).

## Core principles

1. **Hers first, pan-Hindu second.** Her region, language, sampradaya and deities (D-31) choose the calendar rows, the lines and the rite names. A pan-Hindu item she does not keep stays out, however photogenic (D-28; Template F4).
2. **A marker exists only where a photograph, object or witness exists.** `evidence_id` stays empty until inventory v1 (Sun 13 Dec 2026). "She must have had a naming ceremony" is a reconstructed event, which D-16 forbids.
3. **Show what is kept, never the event.** A death can be a garlanded photograph, a lamp, the annual shraddha meal; the pyre, ashes and immersion stay private (Template G).
4. **One dominant rasa per module,** chosen by the module's turn, its colour an accent sampled from her own print or object, never a palette swap. **Vatsalya, in a sampradaya whose seva is Yashoda's stance toward the child Krishna (D-31), is the household's own bhava, not a borrowing; the borrowing caveat applies only if D-31 changes.** No card assigns a bhava to her (the sibling note: the sampradaya treats the parent-bhava as personal), and no frame puts her where Thakorji sits (VR-1); the film shows her vatsalya as the hands and the seva objects, never the deity (W-77). **Vira: the term is *gaura*; Ghosh renders it light orange, others saffron, bright white or golden (Sanskrit 6.42-43 checked on Wikisource 2026-10-19); the brief's gold is one reading, not an error.**
5. **Name the edition or do not quote.** Sanskrit is proofed against a printed edition and printed with the maker's own gloss; attribution only when VERIFIED; published translations are presumed in copyright (UNVERIFIED, not legal advice); no AI transliteration, translation or recitation of sacred text, no AI-generated Indic script on a card (D-16).
6. **Her standing as a mother** comes from texts that honour a mother directly (*mātṛdevo bhava*), not law-code verses. No card uses *pativrata*, *stridharma*, *sumangali* or *ardhangini* as her definition.
7. **Cap the sermon.** Two invocation cards by default (title epigraph after the first true sound; one end card), five at most; never a moral (W-35, W-36).
8. **Ambiguous origin is flagged and defaults out,** with one question to the maker. Shared origin is a category, not a loophole.
