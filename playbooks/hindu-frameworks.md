## Why this matters for our film

The charter makes the film's world Hindu: Sanatan Dharma and its sanskriti, rendered as animated design she will love (D-28). Minecraft is dropped (D-29). "Indian themes" always means Hindu themes: no Mughal or other Islamic motifs, no Christian motifs, ever. A Hindu motif earns its place by being hers (Principle 2), so her answers are the first input:

## Core principles

1. **Hers first, pan-Hindu second.** Her region, language, sampradaya and deities (D-31) choose the calendar rows, the lines and the rite names. A pan-Hindu item she does not keep stays out, however photogenic (D-28; Template F4).
2. **A marker exists only where a photograph, object or witness exists.** `evidence_id` stays empty until inventory v1 (Sun 13 Dec 2026). "She must have had a naming ceremony" is a reconstructed event, which D-16 forbids.
3. **Show what is kept, never the event.** A death can be a garlanded photograph, a lamp, the annual shraddha meal; the pyre, ashes and immersion stay private (Template G).
4. **One dominant rasa per module,** chosen by the module's turn, its colour an accent sampled from her own print or object, never a palette swap. **Vatsalya, in a sampradaya whose seva is Yashoda's stance toward the child Krishna (D-31), is the household's own bhava, not a borrowing; the borrowing caveat applies only if D-31 changes.** No card assigns a bhava to her (the sibling note: the sampradaya treats the parent-bhava as personal). **Vira: the term is *gaura*; Ghosh renders it light orange, others saffron, bright white or golden (Sanskrit 6.42-43 checked on Wikisource 2026-10-19); the brief's gold is one reading, not an error.**
5. **Name the edition or do not quote.** Sanskrit is proofed against a printed edition and printed with the maker's own gloss; attribution only when VERIFIED; published translations are presumed in copyright (UNVERIFIED, not legal advice); no AI transliteration, translation or recitation of sacred text, no AI-generated Indic script on a card (D-16).
6. **Her standing as a mother** comes from texts that honour a mother directly (*mātṛdevo bhava*), not law-code verses. No card uses *pativrata*, *stridharma*, *sumangali* or *ardhangini* as her definition.
7. **Cap the sermon.** Two invocation cards by default (title epigraph after the first true sound; one end card), five at most; never a moral (W-35, W-36).
8. **Ambiguous origin is flagged and defaults out,** with one question to the maker. Shared origin is a category, not a loophole.
