### 1.4 Pipeline changes adopted from the four tracks' advisor insights

Each of these was offered in a final review as a non-obvious idea. I adopt them here as pipeline changes because each one passes the charter's "must mean something" test, uses data the pipeline already carries, and costs a column, a rule or a layer name rather than a new tool. The orchestrator should write them into the named files.

1. **`L00_asfound` is the first layer of every restored still.** `make_ora.py` (`research/craft-visual-fixtures/`) writes the print exactly as scanned, scanner border, foxing and thumbprint included, as layer `L00_asfound`; the touch-up sheet gains one rule, "never paint on `L00_asfound`"; LightCraft presets are authored **per decade or film stock** (`.lcpreset` files named by batch), never one "fixed" look. Some chapters open on `L00_asfound` and resolve to the restored layer over one or two seconds. Restoration is shown as a gesture of care, and the reconstruction is visibly a reconstruction (principle 4) by the same gesture. Cost: one layer name, one rule, several preset files.
2. **The paper border's measured drift becomes the palette.** Template C in `playbooks/craft-visual.md` already records the mean RGB of each print's unprinted border; keep it per batch in the manifest as `border_rgb`. The LightCraft preset for a batch neutralises only part of the drift (a 1978 print still looks like 1978), and the measured cast is written into the chapter's SVG rig as `data-tint` and into the title colour. The palette is derived from how her photographs aged rather than picked from a swatch. Cost: one manifest column, one `data-*` attribute, one rule in the G1 checklist ("palette sampled from `border_rgb`, six colours or fewer").
3. **The layer stack moves on screen.** `doc.exportLayers` already gives every ORA layer as its own PNG. For a chapter still, begin on the drawn or restored layers and, at the moment the memory is told, fade them to show the actual print (`L00_asfound`) before the drawing settles back. The audience sees what was kept and what was imagined without a caption. Cost: a Route A scene template `layer-reveal.html` that takes the per-layer PNGs by their stable names (`L00_asfound`, `L10_sky`, `L20_figure`).
4. **One "still still" per chapter, and it is a photograph she took.** Gate G4 already requires one shot per chapter where the photo does not move. Make it the weight-3 shot (craft-motion skill step 4: motion `none`, full voice line, hard cuts both sides) and, where the manifest allows, a photograph with `source.holder: P-001` and `P-001` not in `people`: in most families her photographs stop where she picked up the camera. Everything else moves because someone is remembering her; this frame is still because it is what she was looking at. A caption in her handwriting is allowed only if she wrote one on the back. Cost: one query on `media.jsonl`, one rule in `lint_template.py`, one line in the chapter spec (`weight: 3`). Carries straight into the playable companion if it is made: the world keeps moving until the player stands in front of that photo.
5. **Scan the backs.** During covert digitisation, scan the back of every print at the same sitting (manifest column `back`). Date stamps, lab names and handwriting from the time ("Summer 1978, the lake") are title cards nobody designed. For each chapter's weight-3 photograph, open on the back held still at native pixels, then dissolve through to the front (a second still in the bundle, a 1.5 s dissolve). Cost: scanning time roughly doubles for prints with writing on the back, so surprise-ops Exercise 2 must time backs as well as spreads.
6. **Markers are the maker's voice.** Every interchange format in the craft-motion matrix preserves markers with colour and comment, and FilmCraft exports them as CSV (`markers.exportCsv`). Before any keyframe editing, the maker watches each chapter once and only drops markers: **red** cut here not a dissolve; **yellow** hold longer; **green** she would laugh here, leave a breath; **blue** wrong photo for this sentence, comment names the right one. The agent diffs `markers.csv` against the spec and re-emits; the maker's Ken Burns pass happens on the second bundle. Cost: a colour legend in the craft-motion playbook Step 9 and a `markers_to_spec.py` in spike S5. Keep every CSV: after the gift, the list of moments her child knew she would feel something belongs in the keepsake.
7. **One timeline, two emitters.** A frame is a pure function of `t`, so the same chapter source emits the linear ProRes master for the room and an offline, seekable page for the USB stick she takes home, with one control: a scrubber shaped like that chapter's photo strip, the relatives' voices playing at whatever pace she drags. This settles the charter's format question without choosing: the film is what the family gives her; the scrubber is what lets her give herself time, and the empty last chapter is a place she can stop. Cost: one extra emitter in the harness and one rule, "Route A scenes stay light enough to seek in real time on the PC" (the S2 fps numbers already measure it). It replaces the live-interlude prototype of format Step 9 as the default companion (J).
8. **Card A produces the title cards.** The photo-labelling pretext (surprise-ops Templates, Card A) is the one place she knowingly contributes with her own hand. Have her write names, places and years on paper slips in pen and photograph each slip at 600 ppi beside its photo. The slips become the only on-screen text in her handwriting, honestly acquired and disclosed at the reveal ("you labelled your own film"). VectorCraft's trace presets (craft-visual, Step 7) turn the slips into SVG titles. The final chapter's card is a blank slip and a pen in the keepsake. Cost: Card A moves early (W5–W8) and the slips are scanned, never typed; charter principle 4 and pipeline Pitfall 16 (her handwriting only for words she wrote) are satisfied by construction.
9. **The coverage map.** Every "only you" answer pins a span of years in which someone other than her was in the room. Add `years_covered` to each interview sidecar (ten seconds to fill) and draw her fifty-plus years as a strip before any editing. The dark stretches are the chapters that can only be hers: they set Card A priorities (photos from a dark stretch are worth most), name where the film should admit "we were not there" (a closed door, a photograph with no caption, a title card in her hand with nothing under it), and give the Sat 2027-03-06 "her turn" conversation its opening line ("nobody could tell us about 1991 to 1994; will you?"). Cost: one column in the consent ledger and one script that draws the strip.
10. **As-found frames are the visual grammar.** Chain of custody already forces the maker to photograph everything as found (shelf, drawer, spine, box) before it moves and again when it is back. Each told-by chapter opens on that frame ("this is where she keeps 1988"), holds, then the album opens; the last minute shows each thing back in its place, untouched. The game-world idea of walking up to a chest and opening it becomes literal, with no AI background. Cost: nothing new; the manifest already records where each item lives. Rule for the brief: the as-found frames are `L0` scans and never restyled.

Items 1, 2, 4, 6 and 10 should be in the Chapter 1 slice so G9 judges them; items 3, 5, 7, 8 and 9 can land in W12–W16 without changing the slice (J).

### 3.3 Who is in on it

The charter says "only the maker knows... nobody else is in on it". The surprise-ops plan, the agent-pipeline gate G5 and charter principle 5 all require at least one proxy, and the plan's Ring 1 is one co-keeper plus two proxy keys. These cannot both hold. **Recommendation (J): bring in Ring 1 and no more, by Sun 1 Nov 2026** (the story track's deadline to confirm the mode): one co-keeper who helps by looking, not asking; two proxy keys scored on the playbook's six criteria (knows her well, discreet, no stake, will say no to the maker, trusted by her, available weeks 12–18); a partner may be one key but never the only one. Contributors (Ring 2) are each told only their part and asked personally, never in a chat she is in; the room (Ring 3) is told Wed 24 Feb. Without a proxy, every hard chapter is OUT of the public cut by rule, and the film loses its middle.

### 3.4 Secrecy rules (the ones that reach the pipeline)

- Repo names, commit messages, folder names, bucket names, cloud-session names and phone notifications must look harmless; the repo holds person IDs, never names; the ID-to-name key stays offline.
- No media in git. Full-size scans, interview audio and masters live only in the VeraCrypt container on the external drive; `cloud: none` by default; the account's model-improvement setting is off before the first `preview` proxy goes to a session.
- A separate Windows account for the project; OneDrive backup off for Desktop, Documents and Pictures; the Craft apps as portable zips with their data folders beside the program; recent-file lists, Recovery folders (`%APPDATA%` and `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Photocraft\Recovery`), LightCraft caches and XMP sidecars cleared after every session; control ports on only during a session.
- Never a family name, file name, screenshot or clip in any bug report: the Craft projects' ticket bot relays reports to public GitHub issues. Reproduce with test cards. Working copies are named by shot id (`ch01_s07.png`), never by the original file name.
- Omit, never lie. If she asks "are you making something about me?", the answer is yes with the details kept until the date. No recording of her without her knowledge and a spoken yes (the announced family-stories evening and a wide shot of the room at the reveal, if she agrees in the quiet word, are the only planned cases).
- Slip protocol: stop, assess within ten minutes, tell the co-keeper within an hour, choose carry on / partial reveal / convert to collaborative / stop and ask the proxy, fix the cause, log it.

### 3.5 The proxy reviewer

Two keys, substituted judgment ("what would she choose, and what have you seen or heard her say or do that tells you?"), one decision sheet per candidate hard chapter, decisions OUT / PRIVATE / SHARED-LIGHT / HOLD with a reason. Any OUT on the public cut is out; disagreement on PRIVATE is HOLD; a key who is part of the story steps back; the maker never overrules a key towards inclusion. **The public gathering tightens the test** (review 02): the question for the public cut is "would she tell this to strangers", so anything HOLD or PRIVATE is out of the public cut, full stop, and lives in the private cut only if the material and the hours exist. Fable flags; the proxy decides; her decision after the reveal overrides everyone's.

### 3.6 Reveal day (Sat 27 Feb 2027) and the week before

Mon 22 Feb full run on the real playback chain; Wed 24 Feb room brief to guests; Thu 25 Feb cold start from a powered-off laptop on the real device and cable; Fri 26 Feb second cold start, pack list, printed run sheet. On the day: set-up four hours before (sound tested with a spoken line, captions burned in, Do Not Disturb, mail and chat closed); guests by a route she will not see; a distractor brings her by a plan that is true; the quiet word twenty minutes before in a separate room (seat, companion, watch with the room or alone first, a stop signal, may the room be filmed, wide shot only); a 30–60 second welcome ("nothing to say and nothing to do"); the public cut; twenty seconds of silence; lights up slowly; "No speech. There's food."; a 10–25 minute pause with no toasts and no questions about the film; later, in a quiet corner, the private cut and the invitation. Fallback ladder on a card: laptop, second laptop, USB straight to the TV, phone by cable, a three-minute audio-and-stills version, a postponed private evening said plainly. Two USB sticks in different pockets. Nobody asks her to speak.

### 3.7 The keepsake

Lean: a drive and disc plus a printed book whose chapters can be found without a QR code; static QR codes only if any. From §1.4 item 7, the drive also carries the **offline seekable page** (the scrubber), which needs no server and plays on the PC she takes it home to. Also in the keepsake: the marker CSVs (item 6), the Card A slips with the blank slip and a pen (item 8), the coverage strip (item 9), the as-found and returned photographs (item 10), and the consent and corrections ledgers. After the reveal: a short text the next day; the keepsake and the invitation on days 2–5 ("version 1; yours to correct and add to"); the recorded "her turn" conversation about Sat 6 Mar, opened with the coverage map's darkest stretch; the consent ledger closed Mon 29 Mar; "her version" delivered about Sat 24 Apr if she wants one; two copies on different media, refreshed by Feb 2032.
